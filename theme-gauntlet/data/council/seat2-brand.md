# Seat 2 — Brand Strategist memo
## annadenning.com single-site gauntlet, n=20 (decisions.md D1). Frame source: data/brand-frame.md.

## Three strongest calls

**1. The subject of the brand arrived; the promised feeling did not.** 18 of 20 buyers used a spiritual-register word in their cold five-second read — but only 6 said "spiritual" straight, while 8 said "new-agey"/"new-age" and 4 said "mystical" (five_second adjectives, all 20 verdicts; aggregates confirm spiritual 6, new-agey 7, new-age 1, mystical 4). "New-agey" is the arms-length version of "spiritual" — the reader recognizes the genre and steps back from it. Meanwhile the frame's core promise, "calming," appeared cold for only 2 of 20 (B01, B10), and "transformative" and "nurturing" appeared for 0 of 20 at both the five-second and recall stages.

**2. "Premium" is not just absent — it is inverted.** The frame says premium applies to the £997-£1497 certification tier. In practice, 4 buyers cold-read the site as "homemade" or "handmade" (B01, B04, B12 "homemade"; B10 "handmade"), and no buyer guessed above $300 for a course from the landing screen. The only two buyers who guessed four figures were the two personas who arrived hunting a certification (B03 "$500-$1500", B9 "a few hundred to a couple thousand"). The premium tier's actual on-site job was tripping up casual shoppers: B06 hunted a beginner course and dead-ended on the $997-$1997 certification as the top, most prominent card (b06-03-wrong-course-certification-price.png, abandoned); B05 and B16 both clicked "Art of Manifesting Method" expecting the book and landed on the same $997-1997 page (shots/b05-04-coaching-program-pricing.png). The consumer tier reads homemade; the premium tier reads misplaced.

**3. Thirteen of twenty buyers spent one of their three end-of-session recall words on a complaint.** Recall is the scarcest brand real estate there is — three words — and B03 gave one to "muddled-pricing," B04 "uncertain," B05 "roundabout," B06 "confusing," B07 "pricey," B08 "sprawling," B09 "date-confusing," B12 "pricey-to-find," B13 "spendy," B14 "confusing," B16 "roundabout," B18 "cluttered" AND "unreliable," B20 "hard-to-reach." None of those words exist in the brand frame. They were all manufactured by the same machinery: a catalog with zero prices (b12-02-course-catalog, b10-02-course-catalog.png), 10,000-pixel sales pages with the price at the bottom (b10-05-zendala-price-clear.png, b12-09-spirals-long-scroll-no-price), prices first revealed on the card-entry page (b01-03-price-shock-99.png, b13-06-checkout-price.png), and a surprise 3-day trial at checkout (b04-05-checkout-trial-surprise.png).

## (a) Coverage — the site's seven reached-for adjectives, cold and at recall

- **spiritual**: cold 6 exact (B01, B04, B12, B14, B15, B16) + 4 "mystical" + 8 "new-agey/new-age"; recall 9 exact (aggregates confirm). The strongest landing of the seven — but nearly half arrives in the skeptical register.
- **creative**: cold 8 as "artsy/arty" (B03, B05, B06, B09, B14, B15, B17, B19); recall 9 (artsy 8 + arty 1). Buyers say "artsy," never "creative" — decorative rather than generative, but it lands.
- **calming**: cold 2 (B01, B10); recall 3 (B01, B02, B10) + B11 "soothing." Weak for the headline promise. The imagery earns it in moments — B06's one delight was "the calm, colorful hand-drawn artwork" (b06-01-landing.png); B11 called the blog "genuinely soothing" (shots/b11-10-zendala-content.png) — but the shopping experience cancels it (see contamination).
- **credible**: cold 0 — the closest cold read was B07's "unproven-looking at first glance." Recall: 2 "credentialed" (B07, B08) + 1 "trustworthy" (B15). Credibility exists on this site but is stored almost entirely in one page: the Our Graduates directory of 28 named facilitators, cited as top trust evidence by 5 buyers (B01 b01-08-graduates-directory.png, B07 shots/b07-03-graduates-directory.png, B08 b08-04-facilitators-directory, B09 b09-06-graduates-directory, B19 b19-02-directory-found). Course pages themselves carry none of it: B07 searched the homepage, store, and multiple course pages and found zero testimonials or ratings anywhere (shots/b07-07-no-testimonials-found.png).
- **transformative**: 0 cold, 0 recall. B16's recall "manifesting" is topic memory, not felt transformation.
- **nurturing**: 0 cold, 0 recall. The nearest neighbors are "personal" (6 cold, 3 recall) and "earnest" (2 cold) — buyers received a person, not a nurturing space.
- **premium**: 0 cold, 0 recall. Its slot was taken by "homemade" x3 + "handmade" x1 cold, and "pricey/spendy/pricey-to-find" x3 at recall — cost complaints, not premium perception.

## (b) Contamination — off-frame words and the moments that made them

Cold: **homemade/handmade 4** (B01, B04, B12, B10 — the landing collage of hand-drawn hearts and mandalas plus founder photo, b12-01-landing); **funnel-like** (B02) and **lead-gen-forward** (B08 — two email-capture forms visible on landing before any teacher identity, b08-01-landing); **jargon-heavy** (B08), **wordy** (B04), **text-heavy** (B13), **unproven-looking** (B07), **a little confusing** (B14).

Recall, with sources: **roundabout x2** (B05, B16 — the book exists only as Amazon affiliate links inside Resources, shots/b05-05-abandon-no-onsite-checkout.png, b16-06-no-direct-purchase-path); **confusing x2** (B06 — no course named "NeuroGraphica" despite the hero using the word, b06-02-courses-list-no-basics.png; B14 — no gift path, checkout ties access to the payer's email, b14-05-zendala-price-and-email-note.png); **date-confusing** (B09 — Jan 9, 2026 presented as upcoming on Aug 19, 2026, and "by application only" contradicted by "course is full" on the same page, b09-04-teacher-training-stale-date, b09-05-course-full-contradiction); **unreliable + cluttered** (B18 — members' Library blocked twice by a security error, shots/b18-03-library-blocked.png); **hard-to-reach** (B20 — contact email findable only inside the Privacy Policy, b20-03-email-found-in-privacy-policy); **muddled-pricing** (B03 — GBP and USD stacked with no selector, b03-04-pricing-cards); **pricey-to-find** (B12), **sprawling** (B08), **uncertain** (B04).

One more identity leak: the second face. 5 buyers (B02, B08, B10, B13, B15) hit the Zendala course credited to "Ann Diane Tai / Tangle with Ann" on a site branded Anna Denning; B13 said it made it "unclear whose site I was even on" (b13-05-zendala-course.png), B02 logged "a moment of doubt about whether I'd wandered onto the wrong seller's page" (b02-02-zendala-course-page). B08 also caught the nav Instagram handle (@neurographics.art) not matching the footer's (@annadenningart).

## (c) Price-guess vs the actual ladder

17 of 20 buyers explicitly noted no price visible on the landing screen. Guesses for courses clustered $50-150 (B06, B10, B12, B13, B14, B15 in-range; B01 low at $30-50; B02, B08, B17, B20 high at $100-300) — the design prices the brand as a modest hobby-course shop, roughly right for the $59-99 tier and nowhere near carrying £997. The two membership guessers were accurate (B04 $20-50/mo, B18 $30-40/mo vs actual $38). The certification price was guessed only by the two buyers pre-primed for it (B03, B09). Verdict on the ladder: the design communicates the bottom rung and hides the fact that a four-figure rung exists — until a casual buyer trips over it as the biggest card on the catalog (b06-03-wrong-course-certification-price.png). And within the bottom rung, hiding the number until checkout converts a fair price into a shock: B01's first attempt surfaced $99 against a $70 budget three screens in (b01-03-price-shock-99.png).

## (d) feels_like and audience match

feels_like: **brand 17 / template 3 / reseller 0** (B01, B02, B13 said template; verified against raw verdicts). Founder-led identity is decisively received — 14 buyers named Anna unprompted in their tell-a-friend sentence.

Audience match splits exactly along the frame's stated target (adults, skewing women 30-65). Inside the target, buyers self-recognized: B10 (62, low vision) — the Zendala "Who This Course Is For" section "made me feel like it was written for someone like me"; B11 (late-night scroller off a reel) — "exactly the mood the reel promised... weirdly calming to scroll through at night"; B04 felt "a sweet, artsy weekly drawing group run by one woman named Anna" (and still bailed on the trial surprise). Outside the target, the one young cold-social buyer delivered the verdict her persona card literally asked for: B13, 20-something barista from TikTok — "it felt more like your aunt's wellness hobby than something I'd grab off a TikTok" (b13-06-checkout-price.png; would_return: false). That is on-frame targeting, not a failure. The genuine mismatch is B07, squarely in-target (semi-retired, GP-prescribed hobby): the flagship promoted in the nav "felt built for therapists and coaches, not someone just wanting a relaxing hobby" (shots/b07-05-coaching-cert-price.png) — the professional tier is shouting over the consumer tier at the exact audience the frame claims.

## (e) Plain verdict

**Identity received in outline, distorted in delivery.** Who Anna is and what this school is about landed on 18-20 of 20 buyers within five seconds — spiritual-creative, founder-led, for wellness-minded women — and 17 of 20 read it as a real brand. But four of the seven claimed adjectives (transformative, nurturing, credible, premium) went 0-for-20 in cold reads, and the delivery layer overwrote them with "homemade" (4), "funnel-like/lead-gen-forward" (2), and thirteen complaint recall-words. The single page moment most responsible for the distortion is the **price-less course catalog** (b12-02-course-catalog, b10-02-course-catalog.png): it forces every intent — hobbyist, gifter, browser — down 10,000-pixel funnel pages to a checkout-only price, which is the exact mechanism that turns "calming" into "roundabout," "confusing," and "pricey-to-find," and it leads with the £997 certification card in front of $59 shoppers. The single moment most responsible for what identity DID land is the **Our Graduates directory** — 28 named, checkable facilitators, cited by 5 buyers as their strongest trust evidence, and the only place "credible" was ever earned.

## What I would stake my reputation on

That this site's brand problem is not identity but sequencing. Twenty cold buyers, 18 correct genre reads in five seconds, 17 "feels like a brand," 14 naming the founder unprompted — the identity transmits. What fails is that the two assets which would earn the missing adjectives are positioned last where they should be first: the price (hidden until checkout for 10+ buyers, producing "homemade" at the low end and shock at the high end, b01-03-price-shock-99.png) and the proof (28 verifiable graduates buried behind a nav link while zero testimonials sit on any course page, shots/b07-07-no-testimonials-found.png). Put the $59-99 numbers on the catalog tiles and the graduates on the course pages, and this panel's own words say the reads flip — B01 bought the moment the price appeared under budget, and every buyer who found the directory ranked it their top trust evidence. Until then, the site keeps claiming "calming, credible, premium" while manufacturing "roundabout, confusing, homemade" in 13 of 20 buyers' last three words.
