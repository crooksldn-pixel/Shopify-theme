# Seat 3 — Veteran Store Operator

Single-site run, n=20 (decisions.md D1). 10 of 20 finished their errand; trust averaged 2.85 of 5; 11 of 20 would come back. My job: separate what costs orders from what merely looks untidy.

## Three strongest calls, up front

1. **Prices are hidden from the shelf, and 9 of 20 buyers paid for it.** B01 learned the Zendala course was $99 — over her $70 budget — only on the checkout page itself, three screens deep (b01-03-price-shock-99.png). B12 and B13 both had to land on a live card-number form just to learn a price (b12-04-price-found-at-checkout, b13-06-checkout-price.png).
2. **The membership — the recurring-revenue product — went 0-for-5.** Five buyers engaged with it (B04, B12, B15, B18, B20); two abandoned, three said they would not return. The killers were an undisclosed 3-day trial appearing as a card form at checkout (b04-05-checkout-trial-surprise.png) and a "Live Weekly Sessions" pitch that never states a day, time, or timezone anywhere (b18-02-membership-no-schedule.png, b20-05-membership-bottom-no-faq.png).
3. **The four-figure tier has a dead buy button and expired dates.** B03 clicked the Mentored tier's Split Payment button twice and it did nothing but snap her back to the top of the page. B09 — an art therapist deciding whether to recommend the school to her supervision group — walked when the Teacher Training page presented Jan 9, 2026 and June 2026 as current on Aug 19, 2026 (b09-04-teacher-training-stale-date.png) while contradicting itself with "this course is full now" (b09-05-course-full-contradiction.png).

**1. **2. ## (b) Monday morning — roughly a day's work, all Kajabi page edits

1. **Put the price on every catalog tile and in the first screen of every sales page.** Worth: retires the tax that 9 of 20 buyers paid, and stops checkout being the price tag (B01, B12, B13).
2. **Two sentences on the membership page:** "Live sessions: Thursdays at [time] [timezone]" and "Joining starts a 3-day free trial — card required, then $38/mo." Worth: this exact wording gap abandoned B04's signup and sent B18 and B20 away; membership saves compound monthly.
3. **Fix or remove the Mentored Split Payment button; fix the Standard checkout's "Mentored Certification Includes" bullets.** Worth: today, the most expensive click on the site does nothing (B03, twice).
4. **Date pass on both certification pages:** one accurate, year-included next-intake line; delete passed dates; reconcile "by application only" against "course is full." Worth: B09 said in her verdict that one accurate dated banner "would resolve most of my hesitation."
5. **Make the annual badge honest** — "2 months free," or drop annual to ~$342 if "3 months" must stand. Worth: B04, the precise buyer that badge targets, did the math and downgraded the whole site for it.
6. **A "Contact" link in nav or footer, email stated on the About page.** Worth: B20's pre-sales money question gets answered somewhere a shopper would look.
7. **One checkout sentence:** "Buying as a gift? Enter the recipient's email — the access link goes there." Worth: B14's $59 was one sentence away.

## (c) The Do-nothing list — looks bad in a report, cost no one a purchase

- **The look, the vibe, the "homemade" feel.** 17 of 20 read the site as a real person's brand, not a template; the recall adjectives (spiritual ×9, artsy ×8, calming, personal) sit inside the site's own positioning (brand-frame.md). Nobody bailed over aesthetics. Do not redesign.
- **The long storytelling sales pages themselves.** The friction on them was always the missing price, never the prose — B11's midnight read of the Zendala blog post is the single thing that earned her return visit (b11-10-zendala-content.png). Move the price card up; keep the words.
- **The Instagram error (B08) and the Amazon bot-check (B05).** Third-party responses to our datacenter probe (decisions.md D11); B08 still scored trust 4 and would return. Leave the social icons alone.
- **The Cloudflare block on login (B18).** Same D11 caveat — and B17 walked the identical login path cleanly, with a working recovery form (b17-03-forgot-password.png). Fix the public schedule wording (item b2); do not touch the login system.
- **Checkout defaulting to United States / "ZIP code" for UK payers.** B02 and B15 both flagged it at severity 1 and both still finished at trust 4, with £75 holding steady page-to-card-form (b15-06-checkout-filled.png).
- **The hamburger menu needing two taps** (B06, B11) — irritation, zero abandonments traced to it.
- **Coupon fields rejecting invented codes** (B01, B14) — worked as designed; B01 herself called it fair.
- **Repeated inline email-capture boxes** (B08, B11) — both ignored them and kept shopping.
- **Facilitator directory quirks** (B19: no Spain quick-button, "All" not clearing the search) — she still completed, scored trust 4, and called it "a real directory."

## (d) The one-week structural bet

**Build the shop shelf.** One "Courses & Pricing" page where every offer appears under the same name as the page it opens, with teacher, a "start here" level tag, price in both currencies, and what's included — and rewire every homepage/catalog card to carry that same name and price. This one build attacks the two widest failures at once: the 9-buyer price hunt and the 4-buyer naming mismatch that cost B06's order outright. It gives B10 the side-by-side he wanted, gives B12 something to report to his daughter, gives B06's successor a card that says "NeuroGraphica — beginner — $59," and becomes the natural home for the gift note and an honest, findable book section with its Amazon buttons. The best counter-evidence on the site already proves the pattern works: the one course a buyer found with name, discount, and price all legible ("7 Chakras NeuroGraphica," $59 from $159.99) is the one that got bought — twice picked as the product remembered (B01, B10).

## What I would stake my reputation on

The merchandise is fine and the trust assets are unusually good — 28 named, checkable graduates carried B01, B07, B08, B09, and B19 further than any testimonial would have. What's bleeding orders is the last fifty feet: prices withheld until checkout (9 of 20 buyers), a membership that ambushes with a trial card form and won't name its own class day (5 of 5 engaged buyers walked or soured), shelf labels that don't match the products (lost B06's ready money), expired dates on the four-figure pages (lost B09), and a dead payment button on the priciest tier (B03). Every one of those is a text edit or a single page build, not a redesign — and I would bet that the Monday list alone moves this panel's 10-of-20 completion and 11-of-20 return rate visibly on a rerun, while touching the visual identity not at all.
