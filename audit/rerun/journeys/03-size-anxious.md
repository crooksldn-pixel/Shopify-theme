# 03 — Size-anxious denim buyer, wants the £60 blue wash jeans but is ~33" waist and has been burned by bad fits before

**Device:** Mobile (390x844) · **Goal:** Buy /products/cb2-wash-jeans IF the measurements convince me — check the table, the cm/inch toggle, the measuring method, and the size-swap terms before committing · **Mood:** Wary. Last two pairs of "baggy" jeans I bought online went back. I think in inches.

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site on my phone.
**Got:** A big COOKIE CONSENT card sitting over the lower half of the screen. Behind it, a dark "evidence terminal" homepage — "13 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", CROOKSLDN, OWN THE STREETS. Header promises FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £30 and same-day dispatch before 18:00.
**Expected:** A cookie banner, sadly. Fine.
**Felt:** "Standard. Accept, move on. Free shipping over £30 noted — jeans are £60 so that's covered."
**Next:** Tap Accept.

### Step 2 — Accepted cookies, immediately got a second popup
**Did:** Tapped Accept on the cookie banner.
**Got:** About a second later a FULL-SCREEN takeover: "CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY — Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited." Buttons: RUN IT / NOT NOW, small print "One code per player." (Screenshot r03-06.)
**Expected:** To be looking at the shop by now, not a second overlay straight after the first.
**Felt:** "One popup after another — that's cheeky. And the 10% wants my phone number ('code sent by text')? For a store I've never bought from? No chance. I don't hand out my number for a tenner off." Closed it with the ×. It closed cleanly, no dark pattern, no guilt-trip button, which I'll grudgingly credit.
**Next:** Find the jeans.

### Step 3 — Catalogue, found the jeans
**Did:** Tapped CATALOGUE, scrolled the register.
**Got:** A numbered list — NO. 03 DENIM, BLUE WASH YARD JEANS, £60.00, "AVAILABLE". Other items show partial stock honestly ("2 OF 5 SIZES LEFT" on the grey sweats), which I like — it means AVAILABLE probably actually means available.
**Expected:** To have to hunt; didn't. Card told me availability before I tapped.
**Felt:** "Good sign — a store that admits when sizes are missing on the card is less likely to lie to me in a size table."
**Next:** Open the product.

### Step 4 — Product page, first read
**Did:** Opened BLUE WASH YARD JEANS, £60.
**Got:** Size row XS S M L XL (alpha sizes), a SIZE GUIDE button right under it, "Order before 18:00 and it ships today", and four accordions: CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS / SPECIFICATION / ITEM DESCRIPTION / MEASUREMENTS.
**Expected:** Waist/leg sizing on denim. W32L32-style.
**Felt:** "Alpha sizes on jeans. Here we go again — 'M' means nothing. This is exactly how I got burned before. Everything now depends on that measurements table being real."
**Next:** Open MEASUREMENTS.

### Step 5 — The measurements table (the whole reason I'm here)
**Did:** Opened MEASUREMENTS. It opens with a CM/IN toggle (CM on by default) and the line "TRUE TO SIZE. THESE ARE THE GARMENT'S OWN FULL MEASUREMENTS." Tapped IN because I think in inches.
**Got:** Columns SIZE / WAIST / INSEAM / LEG OPENING. In inches: XS 30/29/18 · S 32/30/19 · M 34/30.5/20 · L 36/31.5/21 · XL 38/32/22. The toggle flips instantly and remembers itself (aria-pressed flips, cm shows 76.2/86.4/91.4 etc — exact 2.54 conversions). (Screenshots r03-11, r03-12.)
**Expected:** Either no table, or suspicious round numbers that smell invented.
**Felt:** "Right, let me actually interrogate this. Waist steps 30-32-34-36-38 — clean 2-inch grading, that's how real spec sheets look. Inseam creeps 29 to 32 with the size, half-inch steps — plausible, that's garment grading, not someone typing randoms. Leg opening 18-19-20-21-22 — even inch steps. The cm numbers are all exact inch conversions (76.2, 86.4, 91.4), so someone wrote this in inches and converted — the brand thinks in inches like me. Verdict: these read like a genuine measured spec, not filler. Nothing impossible, nothing copy-pasted between columns. AND it tells me the method — garment's own FULL measurements, i.e. all the way round, not flat. That's the sentence that's usually missing and the reason I've been burned before."
**Next:** Check the SIZE GUIDE button — maybe there's body-size guidance too.

### Step 6 — SIZE GUIDE button
**Did:** Tapped SIZE GUIDE next to the size row.
**Got:** No separate chart — it just scrolled me down to the same MEASUREMENTS table (which had kept my inches setting).
**Expected:** Maybe a body-measurements chart ("33 inch waist = size M/L").
**Felt:** "Slightly deflating — it's the same table wearing a different hat. Nothing maps MY body to THEIR sizes; I have to measure a pair of jeans I own and compare. The FAQ later says exactly that ('compare them straight against something you already own') — honest advice, and it's what I'd do anyway, but a fits-waist column would have saved me the tape measure."
**Next:** Read the description — what cut is this actually?

### Step 7 — Description vs the numbers
**Did:** Opened ITEM DESCRIPTION and SPECIFICATION.
**Got:** "OG jeans — blue wash, for the record. 14oz denim, OG straight cut, mid rise. Structured, not baggy. Made in Portugal." Spec repeats: 14oz, OG straight, mid rise, cold wash inside out.
**Expected:** Consistency between the words and the table.
**Felt:** "Hmm. 'Structured, not baggy' — but a 20-inch leg opening on the M is not what I call straight; that's wide. The copy is trying to reassure people like me and the numbers quietly say 'roomy'. I trust numbers over adjectives — I'm treating these as wide-ish jeans with a true waist. Also 14oz is heavy, rigid denim: no stretch rescue if I size down wrong."
**Next:** The other half of the decision: what happens if I get it wrong. Returns.

### Step 8 — Returns and size-swap terms
**Did:** Opened CHAIN OF CUSTODY on the PDP, then read the footer REFUNDS policy and the FAQ sizing/exchange answers.
**Got:** 14 days from delivery to tell them, 14 more to post back, unworn with tags. Return postage is mine for wrong size/change of mind. BUT: "There is no fee for a UK size swap itself, and we cover the postage sending the new size out to you", swaps depend on stock, refund if not. Returns start via an off-site AfterShip returns centre (order number + email) or by email — to crooksldn@gmail.com. Return address is "Oairo Uk Office, Bourne End Business Park, SL8 5AS".
**Expected:** "No returns on drops" or exchange silence — that's what usually kills it for me.
**Felt:** "This is better than I feared. A stated size-swap route where they pay the outbound leg is exactly the safety net a between-sizes buyer needs. I'd be out one £3-ish postage if I guess wrong, not £60. Two small wobbles: a gmail address for a brand ('crooksldn@gmail.com') looks amateur, and the return address is some company called 'Oairo' I've never heard of — presumably their warehouse, but for a nervy buyer every unfamiliar name costs a little trust. The proper returns portal with order lookup mostly wins it back."
**Next:** Decide: M or L.

### Step 9 — The decision, and adding to bag
**Did:** Sat with the table. My waist is ~33in. M = 34in garment waist (1in of room, snug but real), L = 36in (3in of room — belt territory, plus another inch of inseam I don't need). They say true to size; the fabric is rigid so no stretch either way. I was burned by BAGGY before — sizing up is how that happened. Picked M, tapped it, "IN STOCK" appeared, tapped ADD TO BAG.
**Got:** Clear feedback: "> Added — 1 in bag" with a View bag link, header flipped to BAG [1]. Cart shows BLUE WASH YARD JEANS / M / £60, "✓ TRACKED 48 FREE" on the shipping bar (£20 more for Tracked 24 — not tempted), estimated total £60, Shop Pay instalments offered. (Screenshots r03-18, r03-19.)
**Expected:** With this theme's drama, possibly an unclear add. It wasn't — instant, legible.
**Felt:** "M, final answer. 34 on my 33 with a free-swap net if it's wrong. That's the most informed I've felt buying jeans on a phone in a while."
**Next:** I'd press Check out for real. (Audit stops at the cart — no order placed.)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Would buy — size M in the bag, £60, ready to check out (stopped at cart per audit rules). The measurements table plus the free-swap-fee exchange policy jointly got me over the line.
**Total time:** ~14 minutes, most of it deliberately spent reading the table, FAQ and refund policy.
**Worst moment:** The full-screen Getaway popup landing one second after I'd cleared the cookie banner — two overlays before I saw a single product — and it wanting my phone number for the 10% code. Runner-up: SIZE GUIDE being just a scroll to the same garment table, with nothing that maps a 33-inch body waist to their alpha sizes.
**Best moment:** Toggling the table to inches and finding clean, self-consistent, obviously inch-native numbers WITH the method stated ("the garment's own full measurements") — plus the FAQ's honest "compare against something you already own". The table reads real, not invented.
**Would they come back?** Yes — if the M fits as the numbers promise. This store treats measurements more seriously than most big retailers; that's what earns a nervy buyer's repeat business.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Nothing would have STOPPED me at this point, but the thing that nearly did: if the exchange terms had been missing or "final sale", I'd have bailed at Step 8 — the table alone wasn't enough given the copy ("structured, not baggy") and the 20-inch hem quietly disagree about what these jeans are. A "fits waist 32–34in" line per size would have made the decision instant.
