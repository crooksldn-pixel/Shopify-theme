# 07 — Jamie, 24, wants one pair of white socks and nothing else

**Device:** iPhone-class mobile (390×844), normal wifi · **Goal:** the white MOTIONTEC socks — £6, in the bag, done · **Mood:** Wednesday evening, ~19:30, phone in one hand, wants this over in two minutes

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site. Cookie banner sat across the middle of the screen; behind it the ticker read "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £30 // ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH" and I could already see the catalogue runs down the homepage itself — socks listed at NO. 10 and NO. 11, £6.
**Got:** One tap on Accept (tap 1) and the banner was gone.
**Expected:** A cookie thing. Fine. Everyone has one.
**Felt:** "£30 for free shipping. My socks are six quid. So that's not happening — unless postage is silly money, we'll see."
**Next:** Straight down to the socks. Except—

### Step 2 — Got jumped by the Getaway popup
**Did:** About a second after the cookie banner went, a black takeover slid up: "CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY — Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited." Buttons: RUN IT / NOT NOW. I did the maths (10% of £6 is 60p) and then tapped RUN IT anyway because the button was purple and I have no discipline (tap 2).
**Got:** A second screen, pixel-arcade style: "CRACK THE CUFFS. 10% off your first order if you do. Three tumblers. Tap each one at the right moment." Another RUN IT (tap 3), then the actual game: "TAP TO STOP", a bar sweeping over three tumblers, "0/3 LOCKED". I tapped three times (taps 4–6), timed none of them, and got "CUFFS HOLD. Run it again — attempts are unlimited," plus a line underneath about skipping the game and joining the register on the site instead.
**Expected:** A boring email popup. This was at least a game — but I lost, to a popup, for 60p.
**Felt:** "That was 45 seconds of my life for nothing. And the prize needs my phone number — 'code sent by text' — for sixty pence off socks? Not a chance." (Observed only — I never got as far as any number entry, and wouldn't have typed one.)
**Next:** Close it and buy the socks like an adult.

### Step 3 — Dismissed it and jumped to the catalogue
**Did:** Tapped the × (tap 7), then tapped CATALOGUE under the logo (tap 8) which jumped me down to the product register.
**Got:** The register: 13 items with filter chips above — ALL, DENIM, SWEATS, T-SHIRT… and ACCESSORIES cut off past the edge of the screen. I went for ACCESSORIES to shortcut to socks and my tap hit nothing because the chip was basically off-screen (tap 9, wasted — you have to swipe the chip row sideways and nothing tells you that). Gave up on the filter and just thumbed down the list.
**Expected:** Filter chips that fit on a phone, or at least a hint there are more.
**Felt:** "Half a button hanging off the screen edge. Whatever — scrolling nine cards isn't the end of the world."
**Next:** Card NO. 11, WHITE/RED MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS, £6.00, AVAILABLE.

### Step 4 — Sock page, and a pack-size curveball
**Did:** Tapped the card (tap 10). Product page: big clean photo, £6.00, and instead of sizes a QUANTITY grid — 1pc / 3pc / 6pc / 12pc.
**Got:** Tapped through the packs out of curiosity: 3pc £15, 6pc £25, 12pc £45. Under it: "Order before 18:00 and it ships today (Mon–Sat) — Ordered now — leaves tomorrow" (evening, so fair). I also opened the CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS accordion (tap 11) to find what postage actually costs on a £6 order: Royal Mail Tracked, free over £30, free Tracked 24 over £80, UK 1–2 working days — but no price for shipping UNDER £30. Anywhere. (Method note: my main pass ran mid-afternoon and the line read "leaves today"; the evening state above was verified with the clock set to Wed 19:30 — it flips honestly.)
**Expected:** Socks to be one tap. And a line like "UK shipping £X, free over £30".
**Felt:** "Six-pack for £25 is £4.17 a pair, tempting… hang on, £25 STILL doesn't get free shipping. The six-pack lands £5 short of their own £30 bar. That's either clumsy or cheeky. And nobody will tell me what postage costs on a £6 order."
**Next:** One pair. Add it.

### Step 5 — Picked 1pc, added to bag
**Did:** Tapped 1pc (tap 12) — the grey "SELECT QUANTITY" button woke up into ADD TO BAG, with a sticky bar at the bottom carrying ADD TO BAG and a purple CHECKOUT NOW. Tapped ADD TO BAG on the sticky bar (tap 13).
**Got:** Instant feedback: the sticky bar flipped to "Added — 1 in bag", a little "View bag" link appeared, header went BAG [1]. No drawer shoved in my face.
**Expected:** Some cart drawer takeover. This was quieter and better.
**Felt:** "Clean. It told me it worked and got out of the way."
**Next:** Bag.

### Step 6 — The cart, and the carriage bar's opinion of my £6
**Did:** Tapped BAG [1] (tap 14).
**Got:** Cart page. Top of it: "> £24.00 to free Tracked 48" over a progress track — my £6 filled about 7% of it, with a tick mark further along (that's the £30 line, the track runs to £80 for "TRACKED 24 FREE"). Below: my socks, 1pc, £6.00, quantity steppers, a Discount accordion, "Estimated total £6.00 GBP", "Duties and taxes included. Shipping is calculated at checkout." Then "You may also like": crewneck £50, shorts £45, jeans £60, jorts £50.
**Expected:** The bar to have a go at me — it did, politely.
**Felt:** "£24 to free shipping — that's four times what I'm spending, on SOCKS. Not tempted, it's just background noise at my basket size. What would tempt me is knowing shipping costs £3-something and a line saying 'the 6-pack nearly gets you there' — but the six-pack is £25 so even that misses, and the 'you may also like' wants me to add FIFTY-QUID jumpers to save a few quid of postage. Read the room — where's the £6 black pair or the £18 duffle?"
**Next:** Checkout, take the postage hit, whatever it is.

### Step 7 — Check out, stop at the door
**Did:** Tapped Check out (tap 15).
**Got:** Real Shopify checkout on crooksldn.com, order summary £6.00, express-pay row, then Contact and Delivery forms. Shipping cost: still unknown — it only appears after I type an address. Stopped here (audit rule: no personal data, stop before payment).
**Expected:** By now, SOME number for delivery. I've reached the checkout and I still don't know if my £6 socks cost £8.50 or £11 delivered.
**Felt:** "Fifteen taps, three-ish minutes, six of those taps donated to a popup game I lost. The shop itself is quick — the socks bit was genuinely slick — but I'm entering my address to find out the postage on a £6 order, and that's the one number a £6 customer cares about."
**Next:** Would've filled it in and paid, grumbling slightly at whatever the postage turned out to be.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Would have bought — 1pc WHITE/RED MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS, £6.00, reached checkout landing and stopped per audit rules. Did NOT top up: the £24 gap is 4× the basket, and nothing cheap was offered to bridge it.
**Total time:** ~3 minutes (would've been under 2 without the Getaway detour).
**Worst moment:** Realising at the checkout door that the shipping price on a sub-£30 order is stated nowhere — not the PDP accordion, not the cart ("calculated at checkout"), not the FAQ ticker. For a £6 basket, postage IS the purchase decision.
**Best moment:** 1pc → sticky ADD TO BAG → "Added — 1 in bag". Three seconds, no drawer, no fuss. (Honourable mention: the dispatch line honestly flipping to "leaves tomorrow" in the evening.)
**Would they come back?** Yes — for socks or a proper order. The shop is fast when you let it be. The popup gets one polite refusal next time, not a game.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Not the outcome — the mood: print the flat UK shipping price next to "free over £30" (and in the cart), and make the top-up story coherent at small basket sizes — a £6/£18 suggestion against a £24 gap, or a 6-pack priced at £30 instead of £25 so the bar and the packs actually shake hands.
