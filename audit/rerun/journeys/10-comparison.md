# 10 — The comparison shopper, deciding between the two washes of the £60 YARD JEANS

**Device:** Mobile (390x844) · **Goal:** Decide BLUE WASH YARD JEANS vs GREY WASH YARD JEANS (£60 each) — see them side by side, judge the wash, pick one with confidence · **Mood:** Deliberate. I don't impulse-buy jeans; I want to know exactly what colour turns up.

### Step 1 — Arrived on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site on my phone, Wednesday evening.
**Got:** Dark terminal-style homepage, "13 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", big CATALOGUE button — and a cookie consent banner eating the bottom half of the screen.
**Expected:** A cookie banner, sadly. This one is huge but the Accept/Decline buttons are big and honest.
**Felt:** "Fine, fine — Decline. At least there's a proper Decline button and not a maze."
**Next:** Decline, head for the catalogue.

### Step 2 — The Getaway popup jumped me
**Did:** Declined cookies, and about eight seconds later a full-screen popup took over: "CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY — Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited. RUN IT / NOT NOW. One code per player."
**Got:** A whole-screen takeover on top of everything I was doing. It wants to text me a code — so it wants my phone number for 10% off.
**Expected:** Maybe an email popup. Not a full-screen game demanding my number by SMS.
**Felt:** "10% off £60 is £6 — not worth giving my number for. And it came back TWICE more while I browsed, every fresh visit. First time: mildly fun idea. Third time: leave me alone."
**Next:** NOT NOW. To the catalogue. (Observed only — never gave a number.)

### Step 3 — The register (catalogue)
**Did:** Tapped CATALOGUE, scanned the product register.
**Got:** A numbered evidence-file grid, 2 columns. Found my two: NO. 03 DENIM — BLUE WASH YARD JEANS £60, and NO. 05 DENIM — GREY WASH YARD JEANS £60. Not adjacent: the BLUE WASH YARD JORTS (NO. 04) sit between them, so the two jeans are diagonal across two rows — one scroll apart, never in the same row. Also spotted the controls: FLAT / ON MODEL view toggle, and category tabs ALL / DENIM / SWEATS / T-SHIRT / ACCESSORIES. The tee cards list "Colourways: BLACK, WHITE" but the jeans cards show no colourway swatches at all — the two washes are just two separate catalogue entries.
**Expected:** Either one "YARD JEANS" product with two swatches, or at least the two washes side by side.
**Felt:** "Why are the jorts wedged between the two jeans? Number order beats comparison order here. But hold on — there's a DENIM tab."
**Next:** Tap DENIM.

### Step 4 — DENIM filter: the actual comparison moment
**Did:** Tapped DENIM, scrolled a touch.
**Got:** Only the 4 denim pieces, 2x2: blue jeans top-left, blue jorts top-right, grey jeans bottom-left, grey jorts bottom-right. Both JEANS fully visible in one screen, stacked in the left column. The flat photos are big and honest: blue is a proper mid stonewash, grey is a dark washed-black/charcoal. The wash difference is instantly obvious.
**Expected:** Exactly this. Two taps from the homepage.
**Felt:** "THIS is the comparison screen. One screenshot and I can see both washes. Why couldn't the ALL view do this?"
**Next:** Try ON MODEL — I want to see them worn.

### Step 5 — ON MODEL toggle: broken promise
**Did:** Tapped ON MODEL.
**Got:** All four denim cards switched to the SAME photo — a model in a black tee and GREY SWEAT SHORTS. Not jeans. Not jorts. Not either wash. The identical charcoal-cellblock-shorts photo on every single denim card (confirmed: every card's visible on-model image is crooksldn-charcoal-cellblock-shorts.png).
**Expected:** The jeans on a body, blue vs grey, so I could judge the washes in real light.
**Felt:** "That's just wrong. Four different products, one photo of a fifth product. If I hadn't been paying attention I'd think all the denim looks like grey shorts. This toggle actively lied to me."
**Next:** Back to FLAT. Into the blue jeans page.

### Step 6 — BLUE WASH YARD JEANS product page
**Did:** Tapped the blue jeans card (tap 4 of the journey).
**Got:** PRODUCT 03 / 13. Big flat photo — but it's the BACK of the jeans (pockets with the handcuff embroidery). PHOTO 1 OF 2: second thumbnail is a real worn shot — model in a grey sweatshirt, the blue jeans loose and slouchy over white trainers. The wash reads exactly like the flat photo. £60, sizes XS–XL all available, ships same day before 18:00. Description: "OG jeans — blue wash, for the record. 14oz denim, OG straight cut, mid rise. Structured, not baggy. Made in Portugal."
**Expected:** A front view somewhere. There isn't one — flat photo is back only, on both products.
**Felt:** "The worn shot seals the blue — I can see the actual colour on a person. But 'Structured, not baggy'? Mate, look at your own photo — those are baggy. And the copy still calls them 'OG jeans' when the title says YARD JEANS. Which name is real? Feels like the labels got renamed and the copy didn't."
**Next:** Find the grey wash. Do I have to go all the way back?

### Step 7 — Hop to grey via MORE FROM THIS DROP
**Did:** Scrolled below the accordions, found "MORE FROM THIS DROP" — thumbnails for the other three denim pieces including GREY WASH YARD JEANS £60. Tapped it (tap 5).
**Got:** Straight onto the grey PDP. One tap between washes (plus a long scroll to reach the link). Grey page: PHOTO 1 OF 1 — a single flat back shot. No worn photo, no front view, nothing else. Description is the identical boilerplate with one word swapped: "OG jeans — grey wash, for the record..." Same spec, same sizes.
**Expected:** At least photo-parity with the blue. Ideally a swatch link saying "also in blue" near the title instead of buried below the fold.
**Felt:** "One photo. For £60 jeans. The 'grey' in the photo looks basically washed BLACK — would the real thing arrive grey or charcoal-black? The copy just says 'grey wash', which tells me nothing. Blue gets a model, grey gets neglect."
**Next:** Flip back and forth once more to compare the photos — the memory test.

### Step 8 — The back-and-forth
**Did:** Used browser back from grey PDP → blue PDP → register.
**Got:** A pleasant surprise: the register came back with the DENIM filter still applied (the URL carries ?cat=DENIM), so my comparison screen was intact. The FLAT/ON MODEL choice resets to FLAT — which honestly is a mercy given what ON MODEL does. Full flip between the two washes costs 1 tap each way via MORE FROM THIS DROP (after a scroll), or 2 taps via the filtered register where both are visible at once.
**Expected:** Filters lost on back — a genuine relief they weren't.
**Felt:** "Comparing colours from memory across two pages is always rubbish, but the DENIM grid saves it — that one screen with both washes is the whole comparison."
**Next:** Decide.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't buy tonight — but decided: BLUE WASH. The worn photo gives me real confidence in the colour (8/10). Grey I'd only rate 5/10 confidence — one studio back-shot of what looks like washed black, no worn shot, no front view, and copy that just repeats the title.
**Total time:** ~12 minutes.
**Worst moment:** The ON MODEL toggle showing the same charcoal sweat-shorts photo on all four denim cards — a comparison tool that actively misinforms. Close second: the Getaway popup reappearing on every visit.
**Best moment:** DENIM filter + FLAT view — both washes fully visible in one screen, two taps from home, wash difference unmistakable. And back-navigation keeping the filter.
**Would they come back?** Yes for the blue jeans — the product itself looks strong and same-day dispatch reads well. But I'm buying the blue partly because the grey wasn't shown properly; the store steered my £60 with photo coverage, not the product.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Photo-parity for the grey wash — one worn shot of the grey jeans and I might have picked it. (Fix the denim on-model images while you're at it; and either link the washes as colourways near the title or put the two jeans adjacent in the register.)
