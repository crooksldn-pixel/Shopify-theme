**Device:** iPhone-size mobile (390×844), sofa wifi, Wednesday ~21:00 · **Goal:** tee + jeans + socks in one order; expects to change his mind at least twice on the way · **Mood:** relaxed, decisive, zero patience for a cart that can't keep up

**Got:** 10% off a basket that's going to be £90ish? Worth a look — tapped RUN IT. It slid up a second, near-identical pitch sheet — "CRACK THE CUFFS. Three tumblers. Tap each one at the right moment… Code expires in 20 minutes. (this drop closes 15.09)" — with ANOTHER RUN IT button, and above it a huge blank black void where presumably the game lives. (r08-03, r08-04)
**Expected:** Tap RUN IT, cuffs appear.
**Felt:** "Two pitches deep and I haven't seen a handcuff yet — and the prize comes by TEXT. You're not getting my number for £9. Out." Closed it with the ×.
**Next:** Build the fit. Tee first.

**Next:** Cart, to see what £85 actually unlocked.

### Step 9 — The size change that wasn't — my blunder, with accomplices
**Did:** In the cart, looked at the jeans row for a size control: there's quantity −/+ and a bin. That's it. No size dropdown. So, the workaround: tap the item's title, change size on its page, re-add. I tapped the TOP row's title — the jeans have been the top row, in my head — landed on a product page with M preselected, tapped L, ADD TO BAG. (r08-28)
**Got:** Except the top row wasn't the jeans any more — the cart reshuffles newest-first, and the top row was the MONEY CLIVE TEE I'd added two minutes ago. The page I "changed to L" was the tee. I now owned TWO Money Clive tees (M and L) and my jeans were still M. Header said BAG [4], cart said £116. I didn't clock any of it — dark page, same layout, same pill grid, M preselected exactly as I expected, sticky bar tucked at the bottom.
**Expected:** A size selector on the cart row. Failing that, for MY tap to land where my memory said it would.
**Felt:** (at the time) nothing — that's the problem. The blunder was silent.
**Next:** Finish the "swap": bin the old size, bin the socks (decided the fit doesn't need them), checkout.

**Felt:** "Trust the checkout. Watch the cart."

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't (audit stop at the payment landing) — but the £91 fit was built, verified and one form away; he'd have paid.
**Total time:** ~26 minutes — roughly 10 of them spent undoing one wrong tap.
**Worst moment:** Standing at checkout looking at £50.00 where £85 should be, and realising the cart had let him delete his jeans and duplicate a tee without one signal — the bag badge had been stuck on a wrong number through it all, and the rows had reshuffled themselves under his thumb.
**Best moment:** The carriage bar's arithmetic — every freshly-loaded value was exactly right (£5→£30, £14/£24/£30→£80), and the second checkout matching the rebuilt cart line-for-line.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the stock is his taste and nothing actually broke — but he'll treat the cart like a self-checkout till: scan, then read the screen back, every time.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** A size control on the cart row. The whole disaster existed because "change a size" means leave-the-cart → re-add → hand-delete the old line, across rows that reorder themselves, with links that preselect sizes you never chose, under a bag counter that doesn't update.
