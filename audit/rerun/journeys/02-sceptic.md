# 02 — The Sceptic, £60 for jeans from a label I've never heard of? Prove it.
**Device:** mobile (390x844) · **Goal:** find contact details, returns policy, shipping costs and proof other people buy here before I'd ever type a card number · **Mood:** interested but wary; one bad signal and I'm gone

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site, evening, on my phone.
**Got:** Dark "evidence terminal" aesthetic, handcuffs logo, "CROOKSLDN — OWN THE STREETS™", "> 13 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE". A large cookie consent sheet covering the bottom 40% of the screen. Top bar: "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £30 // ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH".
**Expected:** Some popup nonsense — every small brand does it.
**Felt:** "Styled to death, which cuts both ways — scam shops are often the best-dressed. But the shipping bar answering a real question before I asked is a decent start."
**Next:** Declined cookies (tap 1 — I never accept on a shop I don't know).

### Step 2 — The Getaway popup, one second after declining cookies
**Did:** Barely a breath after the cookie banner went, a full-screen popup: "CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY — Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited." Buttons: RUN IT / NOT NOW. Out of pure suspicion I tapped RUN IT to see what they were really after. An embedded game screen loaded — headed "CRACK THE CUFFS." (the old name, apparently — the popup said GETAWAY, the game says CUFFS) — "Three tumblers. Tap each one at the right moment... One code per player. Attempts unlimited. Code expires in 20 minutes. (this drop closes 15.09)" with a second RUN IT gate. I closed it with the ×. Gave it nothing.
**Got:** Confirmation of exactly what I suspected: the discount costs you your phone number ("code sent by text").
**Expected:** An email field, maybe. Not SMS.
**Felt:** "There it is. A brand I've known for ninety seconds wants to text me. That's the one thing I hand out slower than card numbers. And the game can't decide what it's called — GETAWAY on the cover, CRACK THE CUFFS inside. Sloppy."
**Next:** Dismissed (tap 2 of toll before I could even start). On with the real test: four hunts.

### Step 3 — Surveyed the homepage before hunting
**Did:** Scrolled the full homepage.
**Got:** Lookbook frames, a 13-item catalogue register with honest availability flags ("2 OF 5 SIZES LEFT", "DROPPED 24.08"), a "PROPERTY BAG" section showing how orders ship (evidence tag, security seal, keyring), a mailing-list signup ("REGISTER AS INFORMANT"), and a proper footer: QUESTIONS / TERMS / SHIPPING / REFUNDS / TRACK ORDER / RETURNS / CONTACT US / EMAIL / INSTAGRAM / TIKTOK.
**Expected:** A linktree-grade footer with nothing in it.
**Felt:** "Everything I came to find is at least *linked*. Sold-out sizes and drop dates are quiet proof somebody's buying. Still zero reviews or press anywhere on this page, though."
**Next:** Hunt 1: contact details.

### Step 4 — Hunt 1: contact details — 1 tap
**Did:** Scrolled to the footer, tapped CONTACT US. **Tap count from homepage: 1.**
**Got:** A real contact page: "There is no call centre here. The same few people who pack the orders read this inbox." Email crooksldn@gmail.com, @crooksldn socials, "we reply within 1–2 working days", and — the thing I actually hunt for — a physical address: Oairo UK Office, Bourne End Business Park, Bourne End, Buckinghamshire, SL8 5AS. Post and returns only, no shop counter.
**Expected:** A bare form with no address.
**Felt:** "An address I could send a letter (or a small-claims form) to. Genuinely reassuring. But crooksldn@gmail.com — a gmail address on a shop asking £60? That's the kind of detail that keeps my card in my pocket. Get a domain inbox, lads. No phone number either."
**Next:** Hunt 2: returns.

### Step 5 — Hunt 2: returns policy — 1 tap
**Did:** Back to home, footer, tapped REFUNDS. **Tap count from homepage: 1.** (The footer RETURNS link goes straight to an external Aftership returns centre; TERMS repeats it all in plain English.)
**Got:** /policies/refund-policy: 14 days from delivery, unworn with tags, you pay return postage for change of mind, size swaps free (they pay the outbound), faulty items covered both ways, refunds 5–7 days to original payment method, "doesn't affect your statutory rights", and the same Bourne End return address — though written sloppily here ("Oairo Uk Office, Bourne end Business Park").
**Expected:** Either nothing, or lorem-ipsum boilerplate.
**Felt:** "This is written by a human who has actually processed a return. Fair terms, correctly humble about statutory rights. The inconsistent capitalisation of their own address is the only smudge."
**Next:** Hunt 3: shipping costs.

### Step 6 — Hunt 3: shipping costs — 0 taps for the headline, 1 tap for the detail
**Did:** The announcement bar already told me "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £30" on arrival (**0 taps**). Footer → SHIPPING for the full table. **Tap count from homepage: 1.**
**Got:** Standard £3, Tracked 24 £4.99, free over £30, free Tracked 24 over £80. Same-day dispatch before 18:00 Mon–Sat. UK 1–2 working days. International duties honestly flagged as not included.
**Expected:** "Calculated at checkout" and nothing else.
**Felt:** "Concrete numbers, and they match what the banner and the refund policy say. Consistency across pages is what liars always get wrong — these pages agree with each other."
**Next:** Hunt 4: proof that other humans buy here.

### Step 7 — Hunt 4: proof of other buyers — 1 tap to socials, and that's all there is
**Did:** Searched every page I'd seen for reviews, stars, press, "as seen in", Trustpilot — nothing, including on the £60 BLUE WASH YARD JEANS product page. Only route left: footer INSTAGRAM (**1 tap**, external).
**Got:** Instagram refused to load for me (an error page — HTTP 429). TikTok link exists too; same story, external and unverified tonight. So: zero on-site reviews, zero press, and socials I couldn't actually see.
**Expected:** At least a strip of customer photos or an embedded feed.
**Felt:** "This is the hole. Nobody on this entire site, except the brand, says the brand is real. The circumstantial evidence — sold-out sizes, drop dates, a returns centre that implies returns actually happen — is decent, but it's all their own testimony."
**Next:** Read the legal terms — who am I actually paying?

### Step 8 — Read TERMS and the legal Terms of Service
**Did:** Opened TERMS (plain-English trading terms, "LAST REVISED 20.08.2026") and the linked TERMS OF SERVICE.
**Got:** The line I hunt for on every unknown shop: "CROOKSLDN, the trading name of Crooks Clothing Company LTD, a company registered in England and Wales, registered office Unit M, Oairo Uk Office, Bourne End, SL8 5AS." No company number given, but a named limited company I could look up at Companies House. The plain-English terms even explain courier claim windows and anti-bot cancellations.
**Expected:** No legal entity at all — that's the usual tell.
**Felt:** "A named Ltd with a registered office is the single strongest thing on this site. A company number would have finished the job, but I can find that myself in thirty seconds."
**Next:** One last sniff test: what does checkout look like?

### Step 9 — Checkout sniff test (stopped at the landing, nothing entered)
**Did:** Product page for the £60 jeans → size M → ADD TO BAG ("Added — 1 in bag") → cart (clear line item, £60, "Duties and taxes included. Shipping is calculated at checkout") → Check out. FAQ had already told me: Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, all major cards.
**Got:** Redirected to crooksldn.com — a real domain, standard Shopify checkout: order summary, express checkout, email/delivery form. The express-checkout wallet buttons drew as blank grey boxes on my connection, which looked odd for a moment. I stopped there and entered nothing.
**Expected:** Some off-platform card form, the classic red flag.
**Felt:** "Shopify's own checkout on their own domain. My card details never touch their gmail-run operation — that's the decider. If anything goes wrong, a credit card or Apple Pay gives me the chargeback route."
**Next:** Verdict time.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't buy tonight — but passed my vetting. Cautious yes: I'd pay by credit card or Apple Pay, never bank transfer, and I'd screenshot the order page.
**Total time:** ~13 minutes
**Worst moment:** The popup demanding my phone number ("code sent by text") one second after I'd declined cookies — asking for the one identifier I guard hardest, before earning anything — with the gmail.com contact address as a close second.
**Best moment:** Finding "Crooks Clothing Company LTD, registered in England and Wales" plus a physical returns address in the terms — followed by checkout landing on standard Shopify rails at crooksldn.com.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the four-tap test was passed (contact 1 tap, returns 1 tap, shipping 0–1 taps, socials 1 tap), and the policies agree with each other, which is what fakes always get wrong. On the no-reviews question: survivable, not fatal — the Ltd entity, real address, statutory-rights-literate policies and Shopify checkout carry enough weight, and sold-out sizes are quiet evidence of other buyers. But it's survivable *despite* them, not because of them.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** On-site proof that other humans have bought — even six tagged customer photos or an embedded @crooksldn feed on the homepage. Tonight the only voice vouching for CROOKSLDN was CROOKSLDN, and the Instagram link that was meant to fix that wouldn't even load.
