# 12 — Priya, 29, wants the returns policy before she'll even consider buying — and she refuses to dig through menus
**Device:** iPhone-class mobile, 390×844, decent wifi · **Goal:** find the returns policy (and while she's at it: delivery time, sizing help, refund terms) using search only · **Mood:** Tuesday night on the sofa, mildly impatient, zero tolerance for hamburger-menu spelunking

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site. Near-black screen, everything in monospace capitals, a big COOKIE CONSENT panel across the bottom with Accept / Decline / Manage preferences.
**Got:** The cookie panel covered the bottom third of the phone. Tapped Decline.
**Expected:** A cookie banner — everyone has one.
**Felt:** Fine, standard. The evidence-terminal look is a choice, but I'm not here for vibes, I'm here for one paragraph of policy.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Tapped SEARCH in the header — and got ambushed
**Did:** Spotted SEARCH sitting right in the top nav (not buried behind an icon — good). Went to tap it.
**Got:** A full-screen popup slid in over my tap — "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED — CRACK THE CUFFS — escape with a private discount", with a BEGIN button and a wall of promo small print. I hit the × in the corner.
**Expected:** To be typing in a search box by now.
**Felt:** You put a game between me and the search button. It's on-brand, I'll give them that, but I audibly sighed.
**Next:** continued (closed it, tapped SEARCH again)

### Step 3 — The search page itself
**Did:** Landed on the search page and actually looked at it before typing.
**Got:** A big input saying "Item, category or question", and beneath it a DIRECT LINKS list: START A RETURN → RETURNS CENTRE, TRACK YOUR ORDER → TRACKING, QUESTIONS → FAQ. Then shop categories.
**Expected:** An empty box and nothing else.
**Felt:** Oh — "or question", and returns is literally the first link on the page. Promising. But I typed anyway, because that's what I do.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Typed "returns"
**Did:** Typed r-e-t-u-r-n-s and watched the suggestions.
**Got:** Typeahead offered one page: "START A RETURN — RETURNS CENTRE". Hit search anyway. The results page said **SEARCH: RETURNS — 0 RESULTS — NO ITEMS IN THE REGISTER MATCH THAT QUERY**, with the START A RETURN link still sitting above it.
**Expected:** A returns policy page as the first hit.
**Felt:** Zero results for "returns" on a clothes shop. The little link above saved it from being a brick wall, but the big "0 RESULTS" is what my eyes went to first.
**Next:** continued (tapped START A RETURN — 1 tap after submitting)

### Step 5 — The Returns Centre is… somewhere else entirely
**Did:** Tapped START A RETURN.
**Got:** A new tab opened on a different site — blinding white after all that black — "Returns Center", asking for order number and email, with its OWN cookie banner to dismiss. I don't have an order; I wanted the rules, not a form. Scrolled past the form and found a small "View return policy" link.
**Expected:** A page telling me how many days I get and who pays postage.
**Felt:** I asked a question and got handed a form on another website. And a second cookie banner in five minutes. Nearly closed the tab here.
**Next:** hesitated, then tapped "View return policy"

### Step 6 — An answer, eventually
**Did:** Read the return policy page on that white site.
**Got:** "Return and Exchange policy FAQ": 30 days from purchase, unused with tags, refund in 5–7 working days after approval. So: query → Enter → link → cookie dismiss → policy link → answer. Four or five interactions after typing, and the answer lives on a third-party domain.
**Expected:** This exact text, two taps ago, on the actual shop.
**Felt:** Fine, 30 days, decent. But why is your returns policy on someone else's website?
**Next:** went back to the shop tab

### Step 7 — Typed "delivery"
**Did:** Back on search, typed "delivery".
**Got:** Typeahead immediately offered "SHIPPING POLICY — POLICY". Tapped it — one tap — and got a proper on-site shipping page: free UK over £20, order by 18:00 for same-day dispatch, UK 1–2 working days, international 7–14, lost parcels sorted within 14 days. (The results page behind it also listed nine pairs of jeans and tees as "results" for the word delivery, which was nonsense, but the policy link sat on top so I didn't care.)
**Expected:** Some friction after the returns saga.
**Felt:** That's how it should work. Typed a word, tapped once, read the answer. Every question should behave like this one.
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Typed "size"
**Did:** Typed "size", expecting a size chart.
**Got:** Typeahead offered "SIZE GUIDE — SIZING". Tapped it — and landed at the top of a general page called COMMONLY ASKED QUESTIONS. No size guide in sight; the word SIZING is a section halfway down. Scrolled, tapped "HOW DO I KNOW WHAT SIZE TO BUY?", and the answer unfolded: measured product pages have a SIZE GUIDE table next to the size buttons, flat measurements, cm/inches toggle.
**Expected:** The link said SIZE GUIDE, so — a size guide.
**Felt:** The answer is genuinely good once you find it, but the link mis-sold me. It said SIZE GUIDE and delivered "here's our whole FAQ, start scrolling".
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Typed "refund" — and the double-take
**Did:** Last one. Typed "refund".
**Got:** Typeahead offered "REFUND POLICY — POLICY" (plus the returns centre again). The results page again said 0 RESULTS underneath, but one tap on the suggestion put me on the shop's own REFUND POLICY page: **14 days from delivery** to return or exchange, unworn with tags, size swaps free in the UK but **return postage on me**, refunds in 5–7 days, and a return address in Buckinghamshire.
**Expected:** The same 30-day story I read on the white site.
**Felt:** Hang on. The returns site said 30 days; your own policy says 14 and that I pay the postage. Which one is true? If I bought jorts tonight and tried to send them back on day 20, which policy am I under? This is the one thing I came to find out and I now have two answers.
**Next:** stopped there — I had my answer(s), and a new doubt I didn't arrive with

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't — buying was never tonight's job; the policy was. She got answers to all four questions, but the returns answer came back twice with different numbers (14 vs 30 days), which is precisely the kind of thing that makes her postpone a £50 jorts order.
**Total time:** ~13 minutes (a third of it on the returns detour).
**Worst moment:** "0 RESULTS" for the word "returns" on a clothing store, followed by being shipped to a white third-party site with a second cookie banner — "I asked a question and you gave me a form on another website."
**Best moment:** The typeahead PAGES suggestions. "delivery" and "refund" were each ONE tap from a complete, plainly written on-site policy. The persistent DIRECT LINKS row also means even a 0-results page is never a true dead end.
**Would they come back?** Yes with reservations — search mostly delivered, and faster than any menu would have. But she'd screenshot the 14-day policy before ordering, "so they can't quote me the other one".
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Make the query "returns" surface the shop's own REFUND POLICY page (it's literally titled "Returns & Refunds") instead of only the external portal — and make the two policies tell the same story about 14 vs 30 days.
