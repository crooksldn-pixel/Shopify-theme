## Outcome

**Bought / didn't:** Added `BLUE WASH OG JEANS` size M, £60, and stopped at the bag rather than paying. I chose blue for one reason: it is the only one of the two I have seen on a human being. That's not a decision about the wash, it's a decision about which product had a second photograph.

**Total time:** about 11 minutes, most of it spent reading the same paragraph twice.

**Worst moment:** Step 12, opening `ITEM DESCRIPTION` on the blue pair and finding the grey pair's sentence with one word swapped, after I'd already opened eight panels to get there. Out loud: *"Hang on — I've read this. It's the same page. Is anyone actually writing these?"*

**Best moment:** Step 3, honestly. Two clicks from a cold homepage and the two jeans I was choosing between were sitting side by side, same row, same size, same light, both `£60.00`, both `AVAILABLE`, nothing to scroll. Most shops bury one of them three rows down behind a "Sort by" menu. And the three panels staying open together on the product page was a real kindness — I could see the fabric, the sentence and the size table without playing whack-a-mole.

**Would they come back?** Yes, but not to compare anything. It's a good-looking shop and the buying part is quick and honest. I'd come back knowing exactly which item I wanted and go straight at it. I would not come back to browse two similar things, because I'd end up doing what I did today — arranging my own windows.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Give the grey pair the same model photograph the blue pair has. That single missing picture decided a £60 purchase — I bought the one I could see on a body. Everything else here is second: the `ON MODEL` button doing nothing on the denim page, the missing colour link between two obviously-related products, the copy that doesn't distinguish them. Fix the photograph and I'd have chosen on the wash. As it stands I chose on the photography budget.

## Three things the shop should know, from a shopper who came only to compare

1. **`ON MODEL` does nothing at all on `/collections/denim`.** It highlights itself and the four pictures stay exactly as they were — I checked by pressing it, pressing `FLAT`, and pressing it again. Meanwhile a real model photograph of the blue jeans is sitting there and only appears if your mouse happens to drift over the card. The button that promises the thing withholds it; an accident delivers it. (`10-A1-flat-clean.png` vs `10-A2-onmodel-clean.png` — identical cards; `10-E2-two-washes-blue-hovered.png` — the model, found by accident.)

2. **Nothing on either page says these two are the same jean.** No swatch on the card, no colour control on the product page, no "also in grey". The only link is `MORE FROM THIS DROP` at the very bottom of both pages, a screen and a half down, labelled as if it were a random cross-sell. It's the fastest route between the two washes and it's hidden in the least likely place.

3. **The second product page tells me nothing the first didn't.** Same spec, same care, same three sentences, same size table to the digit — one word changed. A shopper who opens both, as I did, doesn't come away informed; they come away suspicious. Either say plainly *"same jean, two washes — the grey is the softer, more washed-down one"* and make it one page with a colour switch, or give each wash a sentence that's actually about that wash.
