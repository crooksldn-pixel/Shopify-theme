# 10 — Ryan, the comparison shopper who can't decide between two washes
**Device:** iPhone-ish mobile, 390×844, normal wifi · **Goal:** Decide between the BLUE (£60) and GREY (£60) OG jeans — wants to see the washes side by side before committing · **Mood:** Focused. Been thinking about these jeans since the weekend; tonight he's settling it.

### Step 1 — Opened the site, got greeted twice
**Did:** Landed on the homepage. A "Crack the Cuffs" popup and a big cookie consent banner were both up. Closed the popup, tapped Decline on cookies.
**Got:** Two dismissals before seeing anything. After that, clean homepage.
**Expected:** Straight to the shop — he's on a mission tonight, not here to play.
**Felt:** "Alright, alright, I'm just here for the jeans." Mild toll, ten seconds, moved on.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Scrolled the register looking for the jeans
**Did:** Scrolled past the CROOKSLDN header into the catalogue (the homepage IS the catalogue — 14 items, evidence-log style). Scanned for jeans.
**Got:** BLUE WASH OG JEANS at NO. 03 and GREY WASH OG JEANS at NO. 05 — but not next to each other. The blue jorts sit between them, so the two washes are diagonal across two rows. Both cards show clear flat photos. Noticed the tees list "Colourways: BLACK, WHITE" on their cards — the jeans don't; the two washes are separate products with no swatch linking them.
**Expected:** Either one "OG JEANS" product with a colour swatch, or the two washes shoulder to shoulder.
**Felt:** "There they are — why isn't the grey one next to the blue one though?" Scrolling up and down between rows to eyeball two colours is exactly the thing he came to avoid.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Tapped the DENIM filter
**Did:** Spotted the filter chips above the grid, tapped DENIM.
**Got:** Grid snapped down to 4 items — blue jeans and grey jeans stacked in the left column, jorts on the right. With one small scroll he could see both flat shots almost in the same screen. First proper look at the two washes near each other: blue is a classic mid stone-wash, grey is dark, almost faded black. (10-06)
**Expected:** Fewer items, jeans easier to compare. Delivered.
**Felt:** "Okay, this is more like it." Genuinely useful — one tap and the noise is gone.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Tapped ON MODEL to see them worn
**Did:** Tapped the ON MODEL view toggle, expecting each pair on a body.
**Got:** All four denim cards switched to the SAME photo — one model in a black tee and grey sweat-shorts. Not jeans. The "BLUE WASH OG JEANS" card literally shows a guy wearing shorts. Confused, he tapped OUTLINE thinking it might be another photo view — it just removed the white sticker-edge around the cutouts. Tapped back to FLAT. (10-07, 10-08)
**Expected:** Blue jeans on a model vs grey jeans on a model — the dream comparison.
**Felt:** "Hang on — that's not even the jeans. That's the same picture four times." The one toggle built for exactly his question showed him someone else's outfit. It read as broken and made him trust the photos a bit less.
**Next:** hesitated, went back to FLAT

### Step 5 — Into the blue pair
**Did:** Tapped BLUE WASH OG JEANS.
**Got:** PDP, "PHOTO 1 OF 2": big flat shot of the back, and a second thumbnail — an actual on-model photo. Tapped it: the blue reads true on a body, proper baggy. Tapped the big photo hoping to zoom into the fabric — nothing happens. Also clocked: both photos are back views, he never sees the front of these jeans. (10-09, 10-10)
**Expected:** Front + back + on-model, pinch or tap to zoom.
**Felt:** "The model shot sells it, the blue's exactly what I thought. Wish I could zoom. And… is there no front?" Odd for £60 jeans, but the colour question is answered for blue.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Found a shortcut to the grey
**Did:** Scrolled down the blue PDP; under the accordions found MORE FROM THIS DROP with the grey OG jeans right there. Tapped it. (10-11)
**Got:** Straight to the grey PDP without going back through the catalogue.
**Expected:** He was braced for back-button gymnastics; this saved him one round trip.
**Felt:** "Oh handy." Small win.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — The grey pair undersells itself
**Did:** Studied the grey PDP. Opened SPECIFICATION and ITEM DESCRIPTION accordions looking for the colour in words.
**Got:** "PHOTO 1 OF 1" — a single flat back shot. No model photo, no front, nothing to tap through. Spec says 14oz denim, OG straight, Made in Portugal — but nowhere on either PDP does the text actually name the colour or describe the wash. And on the near-black site background, the dark grey denim half-disappears — he genuinely couldn't tell if it's grey or washed black. Also spotted "9-16 days delivery uk" buried in the description, right under a banner promising same-day dispatch. (10-12, 10-14)
**Expected:** At least photo-parity with the blue pair — ideally grey on the same model.
**Felt:** "One photo? The blue one gets a model and the grey gets one dark picture on a black screen. Is this grey-grey or basically black?" This is where the grey lost him.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 8 — Tapped LIGHT MODE out of desperation
**Did:** Noticed LIGHT MODE in the header and tapped it, mostly to get the dark jeans off the dark background.
**Got:** Whole terminal flips to a light paper look and the grey jeans finally read properly: washed charcoal, visibly grey, not black. (10-15)
**Expected:** Honestly wasn't sure it would help. It did — a lot.
**Felt:** "THERE we go. Should've done that ten minutes ago." Best trick on the site — accidental, but it answered his colour question better than anything designed to.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Hopped back and forth to compare
**Did:** From the grey PDP, used the MORE FROM THIS DROP row to jump back to the blue (now also on the light background), then once more to the grey. Two full hops: each one is a long scroll to the related row plus a tap, and the photos are back views at different… no, same angle at least.
**Got:** A workable memory-based comparison. Never both washes big on screen together — the related-row thumbnails (tiny) were the closest thing so far.
**Expected:** Some way to hold the two side by side.
**Felt:** "I'm playing colour-memory on a phone." It works, but it's the third-best version of what he wanted.
**Next:** continued

### Step 10 — Tried the back button, filter got wiped
**Did:** Hit browser back to return to his filtered denim grid for one more look.
**Got:** Back on the register — but the DENIM filter had reset to ALL and the view sat mid-grid. He'd have to re-tap DENIM every time he backs out of a product. (10-19)
**Expected:** The grid the way he left it.
**Felt:** "Oh come on, I set that." Small thing, but it punishes exactly the back-and-forth behaviour a comparison shopper does.
**Next:** went back — then tried a different route

### Step 11 — Search finally puts them side by side
**Did:** Tapped SEARCH. The hint says "SEARCH BY ITEM, CATEGORY OR COLOUR". Typed "og jeans".
**Got:** Both washes as results NO. 01 and NO. 02 — grey and blue, same size, same row, one screen. The side-by-side he'd wanted for the last ten minutes. Verdict landed instantly: the blue. He's seen it on a body, he knows exactly the colour arriving; the grey he'd be part-guessing even after light mode. (10-20)
**Expected:** A list of results; didn't expect it to be the best comparison view on the site.
**Felt:** "That's it. That's the picture I needed at the start." Blue wins — not because grey is worse, but because blue proved itself and grey never got the chance.
**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Blue, size M, done
**Did:** Tapped the blue pair from search, picked M (in stock), ADD TO BAG, into the bag, tapped Check out.
**Got:** Sticky bar confirmed "£60.00 · M", bag showed free Tracked 48 shipping at £60, checkout loaded with the order at £60. Stopped there.
**Expected:** Smooth — and it was; the buy path itself is the easiest part of the whole site.
**Felt:** "Easy. Decision was the hard bit, not the buying."
**Next:** continued to checkout intent — stopped before payment (audit rule)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought (reached checkout with BLUE WASH OG JEANS, M, £60). Chose blue over grey — the blue had an on-model photo and a readable wash; the grey had one dark back-shot on a dark background and no colour named anywhere in the text.
**Total time:** ~13 minutes — at least 4 of them spent hopping between two product pages trying to compare colours from memory.
**Worst moment:** Tapping ON MODEL on the denim grid and getting the identical photo of a guy in grey sweat-shorts on all four denim cards — "that's not even the jeans, that's the same picture four times." The toggle built for his exact question actively misled him.
**Best moment:** Two-way tie: the DENIM chip collapsing the register to a clean 2×2 in one tap, and LIGHT MODE rescuing the grey wash — the moment the charcoal finally looked like a colour instead of a shadow. Honourable mention: search results putting both washes in one row.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the buying flow is slick and the blue is exactly what the photos promised. But if the grey arrives for a mate looking "basically black", he'd say "yeah, the site never actually tells you."
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Photo parity on the grey pair — one on-model shot of the grey jeans (or a swatch linking the two washes on one PDP) and he might have bought the grey, or both. His confidence: blue 9/10 on colour received; grey would have been 5–6/10 — "grey wash" with one dark photo could plausibly arrive anywhere between mid-grey and faded black.
