# 05 — Marcus, 31, worked retail for years, believes every "bundle" is a card trick until proven otherwise
**Device:** iPhone-size mobile (390×844), home wifi, Tuesday ~21:45 · **Goal:** wants the charcoal crewneck, has seen the "complete the set" offer mentioned, and has "10CROOKS" saved from the brand's Instagram bio — came to work out whether the £85 set is a real saving or the usual bundle mark-up shuffle · **Mood:** interested but arms folded

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage, straight into a discount game
**Did:** Opened the site. Got the full-screen "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED / CRACK THE CUFFS / ESCAPE WITH A PRIVATE DISCOUNT" popup with a BEGIN button. Actually read the small print at the bottom — codes expire after 60 minutes, single-use, one play per customer. Closed it. (05-01-arrival-home.png)
**Got:** Popup gone, cookie consent box still sat at the bottom. Tapped Decline — there was a proper Decline button, and it worked first time, no guilt-trip second screen.
**Expected:** To have to dig for a "reject" option like on most sites.
**Felt:** A prize-wheel in handcuffs. No thanks — I've already got a code in my back pocket from their Instagram, I'm not gambling my "one attempt per customer" for a worse one at ten to ten on a Tuesday. Respect for the Decline button actually declining, though.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Priced up the pieces from the shop floor before touching anything
**Did:** Scrolled into the catalogue grid and read the tiles like a stock list. (05-03-catalogue-prices.png)
**Got:** NO. 01 CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK £50.00. NO. 02 CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS £45.00. Side by side. Fourteen items, no "set" tile anywhere in the grid.
**Expected:** To have to open two product pages just to get the two numbers.
**Felt:** Good — both prices in the open before anyone's tried to sell me anything. £50 + £45 = £95. That's my anchor and I got it myself. Whatever the set page claims now, I've already done the sums.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Crewneck page, met the offer
**Did:** Tapped the crewneck. £50.00, picked M. Below the stock line, a thin row: empty checkbox, tiny photo of shorts, "Cop the full fit — add the matching Cellblock Shorts. Save £10." (05-05-crewneck-pdp.png, 05-06-set-line-collapsed.png)
**Got:** Just that sentence. No prices on the line at all. "Save £10" — off what, from what, says who.
**Expected:** Exactly this kind of vagueness. This is where they usually get you.
**Felt:** A saving with no numbers attached is an invitation, not a claim. Fine — I brought my own numbers.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Ticked the box to see it commit itself to a figure
**Did:** Tapped the row.
**Got:** Panel unfolded: CELLBLOCK SHORTS SIZE with its own XS–XL row — M already selected, which I never chose — then "£95 £85 for the set" with the £95 struck through, "FREE UK TRACKED 24 INCLUDED", and the buy button now reading "ADD THE FULL FIT — £85". (05-07-set-ticked.png)
**Expected:** Some woolly "bundle value!" badge that never shows the reference price.
**Felt:** Now that's a claim I can check: £95 crossed out is exactly the £50 + £45 I added up from the grid, so no inflated "was" price — that's the trick I was hunting for and it isn't here. Two eyebrows though: it picked the shorts size for me (M, same as my top — quietly), and I'm not taking their £45 on trust just because a struck-through number agrees with me.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 5 — Unticked it and went to check the shorts' own price tag
**Did:** Untick, backed out, opened the Charcoal Cellblock Shorts page itself. (05-08-shorts-pdp-price.png)
**Got:** CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS, £45.00, plain as day. And the same offer in the mirror: "Cop the full fit — add the matching Cellblock Crewneck. Save £10."
**Expected:** Half-suspected the shorts might be £39.99 on their own page and the "£95" quietly padded.
**Felt:** Checks out. £45 is £45 from every angle, and the offer says the same tenner from both sides. So the set is genuinely £85 for £95 of clothes. Fine. First round to them.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Added the full fit
**Did:** Back to the crewneck, M, ticked the box again, left the shorts on M (it had picked M for me again — noted), tapped "ADD THE FULL FIT — £85". (05-09-before-add.png)
**Got:** A little "Added — 1 in bag / View bag" flash, and the bag badge went to [1]. (05-10-after-add.png)
**Expected:** A cart drawer, honestly.
**Felt:** The flash did its job. "[1]" for two garments made me squint — one what? — but I was heading to the bag anyway to count what's actually in there. I always count.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — The bag, and — well, well — a discount box
**Did:** Opened the bag. Read every line. (05-11-cart-top.png)
**Got:** One line: CELLBLOCK SET — CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK - M / CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS - M — £85.00. Both garments named with sizes. Below it a collapsed row: "Discount +". Opened it — a proper "Discount code" field with an Apply button, right there in the cart.
**Expected:** No code field until checkout — that's the norm, and my brief to myself was "find where it first lets you type it".
**Felt:** Pleasantly wrong-footed. A cart that lets you play your code before the till is a store not scared of the maths. Two marks off though: the crossed-out £95 from the product page is gone — the bag just says £85 flat, like the deal never happened — and the thumbnail only shows the jumper.
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Played the Instagram code
**Did:** Typed 10CROOKS into the field, hit Apply. (05-14-code-typed.png)
**Got:** Instantly: Subtotal £85.00, a tag "10CROOKS −£8.50", Estimated total £76.50 GBP. Even the pay-in-3 line updated from £28.33 to £25.50. A little 10CROOKS chip with an × appeared under the field. (05-15-after-apply.png)
**Expected:** Genuinely expected "this code cannot be used with items on offer" — that's the classic. Bundle discounts and codes never stack.
**Felt:** It STACKS? Ten percent off the already-discounted set. Did the sums on the spot: buying the two pieces separately with this code would be £95 minus £9.50 = £85.50. The set with the code is £76.50. The set wins by nine quid. Yes, bundling technically "costs" me a pound of code value — 10% of 85 instead of 95 — but I'm nine pounds better off overall. The set story doesn't just survive, it compounds. I was ready to write the verdict there and then.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — The till tried to un-ring it
**Did:** Tapped Check out to see the final figure. (05-16-checkout-landed.png)
**Got:** Checkout page. Order summary: £85.00. Eighty-five. No 10CROOKS anywhere. An empty "Discount code" field sitting in the summary like nothing happened. My bag said £76.50 thirty seconds ago. (05-18-checkout-85-again.png)
**Expected:** £76.50 carried through. That's the whole point of applying it in the bag.
**Felt:** THERE it is. Knew something would wobble. The bag quotes me £76.50 and the till quietly writes £85 — if I were the sort who taps through on autopilot, I'd have paid full whack believing I had the discount. This is exactly the kind of thing I check totals for, and exactly why most people should and don't.
**Next:** hesitated — nearly wrote the whole store off

### Step 10 — Typed the code again, at the till this time
**Did:** Found the Discount code field in the order summary, typed 10CROOKS again, applied it.
**Got:** Subtotal £85.00, Order discount 10CROOKS −£8.50, Total £76.50 GBP, and a line saying "TOTAL SAVINGS £8.50". The set line expands to show both garments, 1× crewneck M and 1× shorts M. (05-20-checkout-code-applied.png)
**Expected:** Half-expected "not valid with this item" now the till was involved.
**Felt:** Right number in the end — £76.50, same as the bag promised — but I had to enter the same code twice to keep it. And look at the receipt's own accounting: "TOTAL SAVINGS £8.50". By its own maths I've saved £18.50 off the £95 these two pieces cost separately — the tenner from the set has vanished off the books entirely. The saving is real; the paperwork just refuses to brag about it, which for once is the opposite of the crime I came to catch.
**Next:** continued — stopped at the details form (audit ends here, nothing entered)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached checkout with the Cellblock Set (crewneck M + shorts M) at £76.50 with 10CROOKS applied. Came to catch a fake bundle and instead verified a real one: £95 of separates for £85, and the Instagram code stacks on top, beating separates-plus-code by £9.
**Total time:** ~11 minutes — a proper audit visit: two price cross-checks, one code applied twice.
**Worst moment:** Landing on checkout and seeing £85.00 with the code gone after the bag had shown £76.50 — "the bag said seventy-six fifty, the till's trying to charge me eighty-five. Sixty seconds ago!" A trusting shopper would have paid the difference without ever knowing.
**Best moment:** Ticking the set box and watching it print the exact maths I'd already done from the catalogue tiles — £95 struck through, £85 for the set, no inflated anchor. And a discount field in the cart itself that took the code and even recalculated the pay-in-3. For a store dressed as a police terminal, the pricing is the cleanest thing in it.
**Would they come back?** Yes — grudging respect. The numbers all check out from every angle, which almost never happens. But he will re-count every total at their till forever, and he'd tell a mate "use the code AT checkout, don't trust the bag to remember it".
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** If he hadn't re-checked the checkout total, the story flips completely — he'd have paid £85 while believing the code was on, and the inevitable discovery afterwards would have read as deliberate. The code applied in the bag has to survive to the till. Second on the list: keep the £95 anchor visible in bag and checkout — the £10 set saving disappears from the record at the exact moments money is being confirmed ("TOTAL SAVINGS £8.50" should read £18.50).
