# 05 — Elliot, buys the clothes but never buys the bundle until he's done the sums himself

**Device:** desktop 1440×900, ordinary broadband · **Goal:** the Cellblock crewneck — and to prove the £85 "set" is a marketing number before he pays it · **Mood:** not hostile, just unconvinced. He has been sold a "was £120" before and found the thing had never been £120.

---

### Step 1 — Opened the crewneck page from a link, straight onto the product
**Did:** Went directly to the Charcoal Cellblock Crewneck page. Didn't browse, didn't go via the homepage — a friend sent the link.
**Got:** A near-black terminal-looking page. `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK`, `£50.00`, a row of sizes `XS S M L XL`, `Select Size`, `SIZE GUIDE`, `Order before 18:00 and it ships today (Mon–Sat)` and `> Ordered now — leaves tomorrow`. A consent panel sat across the bottom; I hit `Decline` without reading it. (`audit/screens/05-03-crewneck-top.png`)
**Expected:** A product page with a price and a size picker.
**Felt:** It doesn't look like a shop, which normally makes me *more* careful, not less. But the price is a plain `£50.00` in the same size type as everything else — no giant red number, no "was" next to it, nothing crossed out. That's the first thing I check and it passed.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Read the set offer, which is one line and no pictures
**Did:** Scanned down past the size row. Found a small checkbox with a thumbnail of some shorts and one sentence.
**Got:** `Cop the full fit — add the matching Cellblock Shorts. Save £10.` That is the whole offer. The buy button below it said `SELECT A SIZE`. No price, no "was", no countdown, no "only 3 left". (`audit/screens/05-04-set-line-collapsed.png`)
**Expected:** A big orange BUNDLE & SAVE panel with a fake strikethrough.
**Felt:** "Save £10" off *what*, though. That's the entire question and the line doesn't answer it. I'm not annoyed — I'd rather have one honest sentence than a panel screaming at me — but I'm not going to take "save £10" on trust from a shop I've never used.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Ticked the box before choosing my own size, deliberately
**Did:** Ticked the set checkbox first. I wanted to see the numbers before I committed to anything, including a size.
**Got:** A second size row unfolded — `CELLBLOCK SHORTS SIZE`, `XS S M L XL` — plus `Pick a Cellblock Shorts size` and a line reading `FREE UK TRACKED 24 INCLUDED`. **Still no price.** The button still said `SELECT A SIZE`. (`audit/screens/05-05-set-open-nosize.png`)
**Expected:** The £85 to appear the moment I ticked it.
**Felt:** Mildly irritating and, thinking about it, fair. It won't quote me a set price until it knows both sizes exist. But it does mean the one number I actually want is hidden behind two more decisions, and a shop that wanted to hide something would do exactly that.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Picked M, then M for the shorts, and finally got a number
**Did:** Tapped `M` on my row, then `M` on the shorts row.
**Got:** `IN STOCK` under my size row, and in the set panel a struck-through `£95` followed by `£85 for the set`. The button relabelled itself to `ADD THE FULL FIT — £85`. (`audit/screens/05-06-set-open-sizes.png`)
**Expected:** £85 and a "was" price I would then have to go and disprove.
**Felt:** There it is. `£95` crossed out, `£85` next to it. This is the exact shape of the thing I don't trust — so now I go and find out whether £95 is a real number or one they made up this morning.
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — Noticed the headline price never moved
**Did:** Looked back up at the top of the page while the set was still ticked.
**Got:** It still says `£50.00` at the top, in the biggest price type on the page, while the button underneath says `ADD THE FULL FIT — £85`.
**Expected:** The headline to switch to £85.
**Felt:** Two different prices on one screen. I know which one I'd be charged because the button is explicit, but for about two seconds I had to work it out, and "work it out" is where shops usually get you. It's not dishonest — it's just the one place on this page where I had to think twice.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Went and checked the shorts on their own page. This was the whole point of the visit.
**Did:** Scrolled to the bottom of the crewneck page first — there's a `MORE FROM THIS DROP` rail with `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS £45.00` sitting right there. Then I opened the shorts' own product page anyway, because a rail price and a product price are not always the same number.
**Got:** `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS`, `£45.00`. Same plain price, no strikethrough, no "was", no sale tag anywhere on it. Its own set line reads the mirror image: `Cop the full fit — add the matching Cellblock Crewneck. Save £10.` Its own rail shows `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK £50.00`. (`audit/screens/05-08-shorts-price.png`)
**Expected:** Honestly? To find the shorts at £39 somewhere, so the £95 would fall apart.
**Felt:** It didn't fall apart. £45 is £45 on its own page, on the crewneck's rail, and later in the cart's own recommendations — the same number in three separate places, none of them dressed up as a discount. Same for the £50.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — Did the arithmetic
**Did:** £50.00 + £45.00 = £95.00. £95.00 − £85.00 = £10.00.
**Got:** The sum comes out exactly. The `£95` on the set panel is not an invented anchor — it is genuinely what these two garments cost if you buy them one at a time, today, at their normal prices. The £10 is a real £10.
**Expected:** To catch them inflating one of the two to manufacture the saving.
**Felt:** Right, I was wrong. That is the first bundle I've checked in a while where the "was" price is just addition. No compare-at price on either garment, nothing crossed out anywhere except the £95 that actually adds up. I'll say it plainly: **£85 genuinely beats buying both separately, by ten pounds.**
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Bought it. Or rather, put it in the bag.
**Did:** Pressed `ADD THE FULL FIT — £85`.
**Got:** `BAG [1]`. One item, not two.
**Expected:** Two lines in the bag and a fiddle to remove one.
**Felt:** One click, one line, no popup, no "customers also bought" interstitial. Good.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Read the cart carefully, because that's where bundles usually go wrong
**Did:** Opened the bag.
**Got:** A single line — `CELLBLOCK SET`, and under it `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK - M` and `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS - M`, priced `£85.00`. Across the top, a progress bar: `> Free Tracked 24 — unlocked`, `✓ TRACKED 48 FREE`, `✓ TRACKED 24 FREE`. On the right: `Estimated total £85.00 GBP` and `Duties and taxes included. Shipping is calculated at checkout.` (`audit/screens/05-09-cart-set.png`)
**Expected:** £85, and a line telling me I'd saved a tenner.
**Felt:** The £85 is exactly what the button promised — no quiet £3 appearing between the page and the cart, which is the usual trick and is not happening here. But **nothing in this cart mentions the saving at all.** The `£95` and the "Save £10" both live back on a page I've already left. If I'd come to the cart from anywhere else I'd see one item at £85 and no reason to believe it was a deal. The moment I actually committed, the argument for committing disappeared.
**Next:** continued

### Step 10 — Used the code my mate sent me
**Did:** Found the `Discount` row on the right — it's a `+` accordion, closed by default. Opened it, got a box placeheld `Discount code` and a button, typed `10CROOKS`, pressed `Apply`. (`audit/screens/05-10a-code-typed.png`)
**Expected:** `This code isn't valid for this item.` That is what happens on every other shop when you try a public code on a bundle.
**Got:**
> `Subtotal` `£85.00`
> `10CROOKS` `-£8.50`
> `Estimated total` `£76.50 GBP`
> `Pay in 3 interest-free instalments of £25.50 with shop`
(`audit/screens/05-11-cart-set-after-code.png`)
**Felt:** I actually said "you're joking" out loud. It took it. A code off Instagram, on the bundle, no argument. **The set price is £76.50, not £85.**
**Next:** continued

### Step 11 — Did the second sum, the one that matters
**Did:** Worked out what the code does to the alternative. Both garments separately with the same code: £95 × 0.9 = £85.50. The set with the same code: £76.50.
**Got:** The set still wins — by £9.00.
**Expected:** To find the code destroyed the logic of the bundle.
**Felt:** This is the bit that saved them, and I want to be fair about it: **the bundle survives its own discount code.** Whichever way I hold it, buying the set is cheaper than buying the two garments, code or no code. The maths is sound in both directions. What is *not* sound is the sentence on the product page.
**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Emptied the bag and tried the code on an ordinary cart
**Did:** Cleared it, went back, picked `M`, `ADD TO BAG` on just the crewneck, and opened the cart to try the code on something normal.
**Got:** The code was **already applied** — I hadn't retyped it. `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK`, `Size: M`, `£50.00`, then `Subtotal £50.00`, `10CROOKS -£5.00`, `Estimated total £45.00 GBP`. I applied it again anyway; same numbers. (`audit/screens/05-12-cart-single.png`, `audit/screens/05-14-cart-single-after-code.png`)
**Expected:** An empty discount box.
**Felt:** So it's 10% off everything, it works on a single item, and it clings to your session even after you empty the bag. Nothing wrong with any of that — but it confirms this is an ordinary always-on 10% code, not some one-off a friend was slipped. Which makes what it does to the set price worse, not better.
**Next:** continued

### Step 13 — Noticed what the cart offers a shopper who *didn't* tick the box
**Did:** Started clean — crewneck `M` only in the bag, no code — and read the line sitting above the cart.
**Got:** `£20.00 to free Tracked 24`, then `Complete the set — add the Cellblock Shorts, save £10.` `Cart total £50.00 GBP`. The "complete the set" line is a link. (`audit/screens/05-15-cart-offer-line.png`)
**Expected:** A one-click "add the set for £85".
**Felt:** Good — the shop is chasing me with the offer even though I ignored it on the product page. That's the right instinct. Let's see where it goes.
**Next:** continued

### Step 14 — Followed that link and did the obvious thing at the other end
**Did:** Clicked `Complete the set — add the Cellblock Shorts, save £10.` Landed on the shorts' own product page — `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS`, `£45.00`, `SELECT A SIZE`. Picked `M` and pressed `ADD TO BAG`, which is the only thing that page is asking me to do. (`audit/screens/05-16-offer-destination.png`)
**Got:** A cart with **two** lines — `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS Size: M £45.00` and `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK Size: M £50.00` — and `Cart total £95.00 GBP` / `Estimated total £95.00 GBP`. **No discount. No saving. Not a word about the set.** (`audit/screens/05-17-cart-after-following-offer.png`)
**Expected:** £85, since that is what the link I clicked promised.
**Felt:** This is the one place the thing genuinely trips over itself, and I want to be precise about the cost. A line in my cart said `save £10`. I clicked it. I did exactly what the page it sent me to told me to do. I ended up paying **£95 for the same two garments that were £85 one screen earlier** — a tenner worse off than if I'd never seen the offer. And now that both are in the bag as separate lines, nothing anywhere tells me a £85 version of this exact cart exists. The offer didn't just fail to help; it walked me past the deal and shut the door. The only route to the £10 is a checkbox on a product page I'd already left.
**Next:** continued

### Step 15 — Stopped at the cart. Did not check out.
**Did:** Left it at £76.50 in the bag and closed the tab.
**Got:** —
**Felt:** I'd buy this. But I'd buy it with the code, and I'd tell my mate the £85 on the page isn't the price.
**Next:** gave up on purpose — no order placed

## The question this journey was sent to answer

**Is the £85 a real price or a marketing number?**

**Both, and the order matters.**

The £85 is *arithmetically* real, and I tried hard to prove otherwise. The crewneck is £50 and the shorts are £45 on their own pages, on each other's "more from this drop" rails, and in the cart's own recommendations — three independent sightings each, none of them wearing a strikethrough. There is no compare-at price on either garment. So the crossed-out `£95` is not an anchor someone typed in; it is £50 + £45. The `Save £10.` is a real ten pounds. On the specific accusation I arrived with — *"they inflated the singles to invent the saving"* — this shop is **not guilty**, and I'd say so to anyone who asked me.

What the £85 is **not** is *the price of the set*. `10CROOKS` — a public code, 10% off, working on everything, sticking to your session — takes the set to **£76.50**. So the product page states a set price that anyone with Instagram can beat by £8.50, and the cart will hand it over without a murmur.

**What that does to my view of their honesty:** it doesn't make me think they're crooks. It makes me think **nobody has multiplied £85 by 0.9.** Those are different sins and the second one is forgivable, but only until someone notices — and I noticed inside five minutes, with no special knowledge, using a code a friend texted me.

Here is the sharp end of it, and it's a real cost, not a theoretical one. The page says `Save £10.` The truthful version, for anyone holding that code, is: the set saves you **£9**, and the set's actual price is **£76.50**. Both of the numbers printed on the product page are wrong for the shopper who has the code — and the shop has no way of knowing I don't. The brand's whole proposition is that it doesn't flannel you: plain prices, plain `ADD TO BAG`, no counters, no fake urgency, no invented scarcity. It earns that everywhere else. Then it prints a set price its own discount code undercuts. **The one number they'd most want a sceptic to believe is the one number that turned out to be soft.**

The fix is arithmetic, not design: either stop `10CROOKS` combining with the bundle, or stop calling £85 "the set price". I don't care which. I care that they pick one.

**And a separate one, which costs them cash rather than credibility.** There are three prices for the same two garments on this site, and which one you pay depends entirely on which route you took:

| Route | What I paid |
|---|---|
| Ticked the set box on the product page | **£85.00** |
| Ticked it, then used a public Instagram code | **£76.50** |
| Followed the cart's own `save £10.` link and did what the next page told me | **£95.00** |

The last row is the site's *own recommendation* and it is the most expensive of the three. I clicked a line that said `save £10.`, followed it to the shorts' page, pressed the `ADD TO BAG` that page was offering, and landed on `Cart total £95.00 GBP` with two separate lines and no discount. That is a shopper being talked out of a tenner by the shop's own upsell, and unlike the £85/£76.50 problem it costs *them* nothing and costs *me* everything — I'd have paid £85 happily. Whoever wrote the cart line and whoever wrote the product-page checkbox do not appear to have met.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't — stopped at the cart with £76.50 in it, as instructed. But I'd have completed this one, and that surprised me, because I arrived intending to catch them out.
**Total time:** About 12 minutes, most of it spent checking the shorts' price and doing sums.
**Worst moment:** Two, and the second is the expensive one. Watching `10CROOKS` knock £8.50 off the "£85 set price" without a fight — *"so it was never £85 then."* And then clicking a cart line that said `save £10.` and ending up at `Cart total £95.00 GBP` — *"I've just paid ten quid extra for taking their advice."*
**Best moment:** Genuinely — opening the shorts' own page and finding £45. Not £45 crossed out from £55. Just £45. Three places, one number, no theatre. That is the single most trust-building thing on this site and they probably don't know it's doing any work.
**Would they come back?** Yes. A shop whose "was" price is honest addition is rarer than it should be, and I'll go back for the shorts on their own.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Making `£85` the actual floor for the set — either by excluding the bundle from `10CROOKS`, or by having the set panel and the cart quote the same price the cart will finally charge. Two more that cost nothing and are arguably worth more money: point `Complete the set — add the Cellblock Shorts, save £10.` at something that actually delivers £85 rather than at a £45 `ADD TO BAG`; and once the set *is* in the bag, say so in words — right now the cart shows one line at £85 and never mentions the £95 or the tenner, so at the exact moment a sceptic is deciding whether to check out, the entire argument for the set has vanished.
