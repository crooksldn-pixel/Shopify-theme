# 07 — Jamie, 24, wants one pair of white socks and nothing else
**Device:** iPhone-class mobile (390×844), normal wifi · **Goal:** grab the white MOTIONTEC socks a mate showed him — £6, done · **Mood:** Tuesday night on the sofa, ~21:40, half watching TV, wants this over in two minutes

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site, thumb hovering, ready to find socks.
**Got:** Black terminal-looking homepage, "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH" scrolling at the top, and a cookie consent panel eating the entire bottom half of the screen.
**Expected:** A shop. Maybe a picture of clothes.
**Felt:** The hacker-terminal thing is a vibe, fine. The cookie wall is half my screen though — nothing happens until I deal with it. Also clocked "order by 18:00" — it's gone half nine, so that ship's sailed already.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Tapped Accept on the cookies (tap 1)
**Did:** Hit the big Accept button — it's the top one, closest to my thumb.
**Got:** Banner gone, clean homepage: "14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", big CROOKSLDN, a CATALOGUE button.
**Expected:** Exactly this.
**Felt:** One tap tax, standard. No popup ambushed me after, which honestly surprised me a bit.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Tapped SEARCH and typed "socks" (tap 2 + 5 letters)
**Did:** Didn't fancy scrolling a catalogue for socks — tapped SEARCH in the header, keyboard came straight up (the box was already focused, didn't even have to tap it), typed "socks".
**Got:** Live suggestions appeared while I was still typing: WHITE/RED MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS £6 with a photo, black/blue ones under it.
**Expected:** To have to press enter and wade through a results page.
**Felt:** Oh that's quick. The exact socks, with a picture, before I'd finished thinking. Best bit of the whole visit.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Tapped the white socks suggestion (tap 3)
**Got:** Product page. Big photo of the socks, £6.00, IN STOCK, "Ordered now — leaves tomorrow", and a QUANTITY row: 1pc / 3pc / 6pc / 12pc. Sticky bar pinned to the bottom: ADD TO BAG and CHECKOUT NOW.
**Did:** Had a quick look. One photo only, but it's socks, what else do I need.
**Expected:** Price, picture, buy button. Got all three without scrolling, more or less.
**Felt:** "Leaves tomorrow" — fair, it's late. The 3pc/6pc/12pc thing caught my eye though, because the top bar said free shipping over £20 and I'm at £6.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 5 — Poked the 3pc option, then went back to 1pc (taps 4, 5)
**Did:** Tapped 3pc just to see.
**Got:** Price flipped to £15.00 — three pairs for fifteen quid, so a £3 saving.
**Expected:** £18, honestly.
**Felt:** Tempting for half a second... but £15 STILL doesn't get free shipping. The £20 line sits exactly where sock money can't reach it — 3 pairs £15 is short, 6 pairs is way past what I want. If the 3-pack hit the threshold I'd probably have caved. It doesn't, so — one pair, as planned.
**Next:** went back (tapped 1pc)

### Step 6 — Hit ADD TO BAG on the sticky bar (tap 6)
**Did:** Tapped ADD TO BAG at the bottom.
**Got:** ...nothing dramatic. No basket sliding out. Stared for a second, then noticed BAG [0] had become BAG [1] up top. Apparently there was also a little "Added — 1 in bag" note somewhere down the page, but I never saw it.
**Expected:** A basket drawer to pop up like everywhere else.
**Felt:** Did that work? Oh — [1], yeah, fine. Half a second of doubt I didn't need. (Only later did it register there was a CHECKOUT NOW button sitting right there that would've skipped all this.)
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — Tapped BAG [1] (tap 7)
**Got:** Cart page. Right at the top: "£14.00 to free Tracked 48" with a little progress bar, barely filled, and two markers — TRACKED 48 FREE, then TRACKED 24 FREE further along. My socks, £6.00, estimated total £6.00, "Shipping is calculated at checkout." Below: "You may also like" — a £50 crewneck, £45 shorts, £60 jeans.
**Did:** Read the bar, did the maths, scrolled the suggestions.
**Expected:** Some pressure to spend more.
**Felt:** The bar's honest at least — tells me the gap and doesn't nag. But £14 more to save maybe £3 postage? I'm not buying £14 of extra stuff to save three quid. And the "you may also like" wants me to add a FIFTY POUND crewneck to a £6 sock order — read the room. If they'd shown me the £6 black socks or that £18 duffle right there, different conversation.
**Next:** continued — checking out at £6, whatever shipping costs

### Step 8 — Tapped Check out (tap 8)
**Got:** Proper checkout. £6.00 at the top, Shop Pay / PayPal / Google Pay express buttons, then the usual email-name-address form.
**Did:** Ignored the express buttons (can't be bothered signing into anything), thumbed in email and address. Unticked "Keep me updated" while I was there.
**Expected:** Standard Shopify form. It was.
**Felt:** Fine. Boring in the good way. This is the longest part of the whole thing — two-odd minutes of typing my address for some socks.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Shipping options appeared, and I stopped at payment
**Got:** Tracked 48 — £3.00 — "Estimated delivery Fri, 28 Aug". Tracked 24 — £4.99 — "Thu, 20 Aug–Fri, 21 Aug". Total with the £3 option: £9.00.
**Did:** Double-took at the dates. Kept Tracked 48 anyway. Got to the card form and that's where my audit ends — but I was in, £9, ready to pay.
**Expected:** Couple of quid postage, socks by the weekend.
**Felt:** Hang on — the 48-hour one says the 28th?? That's TEN DAYS away, and the 24-hour one comes Thursday? One of those dates is lying. For a second it made me think about paying the £4.99 — five quid postage on six quid socks, nearly doubling it — which is exactly the kind of thing that makes people close the tab. £9 for socks. Went from a £6 impulse to a £9 decision, but fine.
**Next:** continued to payment — stopped there (audit rule)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought (reached payment with £6 socks + £3 Tracked 48 = £9.00). The free-shipping bar never stood a chance of upselling me: every sock-sized top-up (2nd pair £12, 3-pack £15) still misses £20, and the cart's suggestions started at £45.
**Total time:** ~6 minutes — about 90 seconds of it shopping, the rest typing my address. 8 taps + typing "socks" from landing to the checkout page (could've been 4 with that CHECKOUT NOW button I ignored).
**Worst moment:** The shipping dates at checkout — "Tracked 48, estimated 28th of August?! The 24-hour one gets here Thursday — so what is the 48 doing for ten days, walking?"
**Best moment:** Search. Two taps, five letters, and my exact socks appeared with a photo before I'd hit enter. Cold homepage to product page in three taps.
**Would they come back?** Yeah — for a planned buy. It's fast and nothing fought me except the cookie wall. But the £6-order maths (50% postage surcharge, unreachable free-ship line) means next time I'd wait and batch it with a tee.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Put reachable top-ups under that carriage bar. Show me the £6 black socks or the £18 duffle next to "£14.00 to free Tracked 48" and I plausibly leave £24 in the till instead of £9 — instead they showed me a £50 crewneck. (And fix that 28 Aug estimate on Tracked 48 before it scares off someone less committed than me.)
