# 19 — Marcus, 34, browsing on his laptop on the sofa after work
**Device:** Desktop 1440x900, fast home broadband · **Goal:** No mission — heard about the brand, has a free evening, wants a proper look around and maybe a treat · **Mood:** Relaxed, unhurried, mouse in hand, cup of tea

### Step 1 — Opened the site and stared at a dimmed black screen
**Did:** Loaded the homepage.
**Got:** The whole page went dim behind an overlay and for a good four or five seconds there was nothing in the middle — just a black page, a faint site behind it, and a lone × in the top corner (19-01-arrival.png). Then a popup finally painted in: "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED — CRACK THE CUFFS — escape with a private discount. Lock all 3 tumblers before time runs out. Tap each one to stop it." A cookie banner sat underneath it all.
**Expected:** A homepage.
**Felt:** For a few seconds I genuinely thought the site was broken — dimmed screen, nothing in it, just an ×. Then the game appeared and I forgave it, because that's actually a fun way to say hello. Also it says "Tap" — I have a mouse, but fine.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Played the game, won, got asked for my phone number
**Did:** Clicked BEGIN, then clicked each of the three spinning tumblers to lock them.
**Got:** 3/3 LOCKED — code 1-4-6 (19-07-tumblers-clicked.png). Then instead of a discount code: "SECURE YOUR LINE — STEP 1 OF 2", a UK mobile field and a tick box saying CROOKSLDN can text me drops and offers (19-08-game-result.png). Nowhere does it say what the discount actually is.
**Expected:** To win a code I could use tonight, on this laptop.
**Felt:** I cracked your cuffs and you won't even tell me what I won unless I hand over my phone number? On my laptop, in the evening, for a mystery percentage? No. Closed it. Declined the cookies too while I was in a declining mood.
**Next:** went back (closed the popup and kept browsing)

### Step 3 — First proper look at the homepage
**Did:** Scrolled the homepage top to bottom (19-09-homepage-clear.png, 19-11-scroll-1/2/3.png, 19-12-footer.png).
**Got:** A hero with the CROOKSLDN name, "OWN THE STREETS™" and a CATALOGUE button — all sitting right-of-centre, with the entire left half of my 1440px screen just empty black. Then a full catalogue grid, four columns, every card numbered like evidence (NO. 01, NO. 02…), prices and AVAILABLE tags. Then a genuinely lovely "EVERY ORDER SHIPS LIKE THIS" section — evidence tags, security seal, cuff keyring, photographed like a crime scene. Then "REGISTER AS INFORMANT".
**Expected:** A dark brand-heavy homepage; on a big screen, something using the big screen.
**Felt:** The grid and the packaging section use the width beautifully. The hero doesn't — half my screen is dead black and the title hugs the right side like something's missing on the left. And at the end of the grid there's a big flat purple rectangle where two more products should be — looks like a gap in the shelf, not a design choice (19-11-scroll-2.png).
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Tried to "register as informant" and couldn't
**Did:** Read the REGISTER AS INFORMANT box — "Drops go to the register before they go public" — and looked for where to sign up (19-14-register-box.png).
**Got:** A heading, two lines of copy, a divider, small print about "one text per drop, reply STOP to leave"… and no field, no button, nothing. The box also only occupies the left half of the section; the right half is empty.
**Expected:** A phone or email box and a JOIN button.
**Felt:** You wrote a paragraph convincing me to join a text list and then didn't give me anywhere to type. Am I blind? I actually moved the mouse around the box looking for a hidden input. Nothing.
**Next:** hesitated, then moved on

### Step 5 — Noticed the site ignores my mouse
**Did:** Ran the cursor along the header — CATALOGUE, SEARCH, BAG, LIGHT MODE, MENU — and over product cards, the way you idly do on desktop.
**Got:** Nothing. No underline, no colour shift, no image swap on cards. The cursor turns into a little hand and that is the entire response (19-10-nav-hover.png, 19-13-card-hover.png).
**Expected:** Links that light up, cards that swap to a second photo or at least acknowledge me.
**Felt:** Small thing, but on a laptop this is how a site talks to you, and this one doesn't. It feels like a phone layout that happens to be wide. The terminal theme could do a great hover — a blinking cursor, an invert — and it does none of it.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Into the catalogue, played with the toggles
**Did:** Clicked CATALOGUE in the header, then tried ON MODEL, looked for OUTLINE, then clicked DENIM (19-15-catalogue-arrival.png).
**Got:** A clean four-column page, "ALL — 14 ITEMS", FLAT/ON MODEL switch, category chips. ON MODEL went purple but the pictures in front of me looked exactly the same — flat garments on black (19-16-onmodel.png). I honestly couldn't tell you what it did. The OUTLINE toggle I'd seen on the homepage isn't here at all. DENIM instantly cut the grid to four bits of denim — snappy, no reload (19-18-denim.png) — but the header stubbornly kept saying "ALL — 14 ITEMS" above my four items, and the address bar never changed, so I couldn't have sent this filtered view to a mate.
**Expected:** ON MODEL to show people wearing the clothes; the count to update; toggles to be the same everywhere.
**Felt:** The filtering is quick and I like the evidence-log numbering. But ON MODEL is a button that does nothing I can see — I clicked it twice to check — and a header that says 14 when it's showing 4 makes the page feel like it isn't listening.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — Blue Wash OG Jeans
**Did:** Clicked into the blue jeans from the denim view. Poked the gallery, hovered the sizes, hit SIZE GUIDE (19-20-jeans-pdp.png).
**Got:** Proper two-column desktop layout — big photo left, buy panel right, "PRODUCT 03 / 14" breadcrumb. Only 2 photos though, and clicking the big image does nothing — no zoom, cursor doesn't even change. Sizes XS–XL don't react to hover either, and neither does ADD TO BAG. SIZE GUIDE was a nice surprise: instead of a popup it jumped me down to a MEASUREMENTS accordion, already open, CM/IN toggle, with my selected size's row highlighted in the table (19-27-size-guide.png).
**Expected:** Click-to-zoom on £60 jeans, more than two photos.
**Felt:** The layout respects the screen; the photos don't. Two shots and no zoom for jeans is thin — I wanted the fabric up close. The measurements table highlighting my size was genuinely smart though.
**Next:** continued — and while scrolling back up I noticed the buy button had scrolled away entirely; no bar follows you on desktop. On a short page it's survivable, but I did have to scroll up to buy.

### Step 8 — The crewneck and the set offer — best bit of the night
**Did:** Went to the charcoal crewneck, picked size L, and ticked the little checkbox: "Cop the full fit — add the matching Cellblock Shorts. Save £10." (19-30-set-offer.png)
**Got:** The box unfolded: a shorts size picker already set to L to match me, "£95 → £85 for the set", "FREE UK TRACKED 24 INCLUDED", and the big button renamed itself to "ADD THE FULL FIT — £85" (19-32-set-ticked.png).
**Expected:** Fiddly bundle mechanics, probably a separate page.
**Felt:** This is how you sell me two things. It guessed my shorts size, told me the price before and after, and the button says exactly what I'm about to do. No notes. Ticked, size checked, button pressed.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Pressed the £85 button and… silence
**Did:** Clicked ADD THE FULL FIT — £85.
**Got:** Nothing moved. No drawer slid out, no tick, no "added". After about a second the tiny BAG counter in the header went from [0] to [1] (19-33-drawer-open.png). One. I'd just added two garments.
**Expected:** A cart drawer sliding in showing both pieces, like every shop I use.
**Felt:** I sat there for a couple of seconds wondering if it had worked, then squinted at the header. [1]? Did it only add the jumper? An £85 click deserves more than a number quietly changing in the corner of the screen.
**Next:** hesitated — then clicked BAG to check

### Step 10 — The cart put my mind at rest
**Did:** Clicked BAG [1], which turned out to be a link to a cart page — there's no bag drawer on desktop at all.
**Got:** A tidy cart page: "CELLBLOCK SET" as one line containing CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK – L and CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK SHORTS – L, £85, a full free-shipping bar reading "Free Tracked 24 — unlocked", quantity stepper, Discount box, instalments note, express-pay buttons (19-36-bag-view.png).
**Expected:** Both items in there — and they were, bundled as one line, which explains the [1].
**Felt:** Relief, and the free-shipping bar being maxed out is a nice pat on the head. Two grumbles: the set's thumbnail only shows the jumper, and "You may also like" recommended me… the crewneck and shorts already sitting in my bag. Try harder.
**Next:** continued

### Step 11 — Evening wander: menu, light mode, FAQ
**Did:** Opened MENU from the header, hovered around it, flipped LIGHT MODE on and off, then went to QUESTIONS in the footer (19-37-menu-open.png, 19-38-light-mode.png, 19-39-faq-top.png).
**Got:** MENU is a proper right-hand panel, about a third of the screen, dimming the rest — shop categories, info links, and a little animated pixel heist map advertising "PLAY CASE:001 NOW". And the links in it change colour under the mouse — the only place on the whole site that does. LIGHT MODE flips the entire site to a paper-white terminal that actually looks finished. The FAQ is a real page — questions grouped under DELIVERY / SIZING / RETURNS / ORDERS, opening cleanly, honest answers.
**Expected:** A mobile hamburger blown up full-screen; a half-broken light theme; a wall-of-text FAQ.
**Felt:** Pleasantly wrong on all three. The menu panel is the most desktop-feeling thing here — which is faintly absurd, since it's the one bit of chrome a desktop shopper needs least. The menu lists TRACKSUITS though, which I never saw in the catalogue.
**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Nearly bought the jeans too, thought better of it
**Did:** Went back for the blue jeans — size M, ADD TO BAG. Same silence as before, badge ticked [1] to [2]. Opened the cart: £145 total (19-45-cart-two-lines.png). Stared at it, decided the set was the treat and the jeans could wait for payday, and hit Remove on the jeans line.
**Got:** Jeans gone, back to the set at £85 (19-47-final-cart.png). Remove worked first time.
**Expected:** Exactly this.
**Felt:** £145 on a Tuesday is a Saturday decision. The silent add annoyed me a second time though — I only knew it worked because I've learned to watch the little number.
**Next:** continued

### Step 13 — Checkout
**Did:** Clicked Check out with the set in the bag.
**Got:** Straight into a clean Shopify checkout — CELLBLOCK SET £85, expandable to show both pieces at L, express pay options, UK delivery form (19-43-checkout.png).
**Expected:** This.
**Felt:** No drama, everything I put in is here. This is where I'd pay.
**Next:** stopped at the payment step — that's the treat sorted

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought (checkout intent) — the Cellblock set, £85, both pieces in L. The set checkbox did the selling.
**Total time:** ~30 minutes of genuinely enjoyable poking about.
**Worst moment:** Winning the cuffs game and hitting "SECURE YOUR LINE — STEP 1 OF 2" — "I did your little game and you want my mobile number before you'll even say what the discount is? On my laptop?" Close second: pressing an £85 button and getting total silence.
**Best moment:** Ticking "Cop the full fit" — shorts size pre-matched to mine, £95 struck through to £85, button renaming itself "ADD THE FULL FIT — £85". The single most confident bit of selling on the site.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the brand is fun, the set won me over, and light mode + the FAQ suggest someone cares. But desktop is clearly the second-class citizen: no hover life, no add-to-bag feedback, a half-empty hero, a signup box you can't sign up to.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Nothing changed the purchase — but a visible "added to bag" response (a drawer or even a toast) would have removed both moments where I genuinely doubted the site had heard me, and I'd have added the jeans on impulse instead of going to the cart, seeing the total, and talking myself out of it.
