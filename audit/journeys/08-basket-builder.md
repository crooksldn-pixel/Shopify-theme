# 08 — Dre, 22, building his first full CROOKSLDN outfit on payday week
**Device:** iPhone-class mobile (390×844), normal wifi · **Goal:** tee + jeans + socks in one order — wants the free next-day shipping if the maths works, needs it before Friday · **Mood:** Tuesday ~21:35, sofa, focused but fussy — he WILL change his mind at least twice, he knows this about himself

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site, ready to browse properly, not just grab one thing.
**Got:** The black evidence-terminal homepage with "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH" ticking across the top — and a cookie consent panel squatting on the bottom half of the screen. No popup ambushed me this visit.
**Expected:** A shop and probably some kind of banner tax.
**Felt:** Clocked the £20 free shipping line immediately — that's nothing, I'm planning to spend eighty-odd. The cookie thing is huge on a phone but it's one tap.
**Next:** continued (tapped Accept)

### Step 2 — Into the CATALOGUE
**Did:** Tapped CATALOGUE, thumbed down the grid looking for the tees.
**Got:** Numbered product cards like case files — "NO. 07 / T-SHIRT / MONEY CLIVE TEE / Colourways: BLACK, WHITE / £25.00 / AVAILABLE". Found the tee with the money-bag logo and tapped it.
**Expected:** A normal product grid.
**Felt:** The "NO. 07" filing thing is actually useful for scanning. Everything says AVAILABLE which is calming when you're buying three things at once.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Tee page: picked M, black, added it
**Did:** On MONEY CLIVE TEE (£25): size row XS S M L XL, colour BLACK/WHITE. Noticed the highlight was already sitting on XS — would 100% have bought an XS by accident if I'd rushed. Tapped M, left it black, hit ADD TO BAG.

**Expected:** A slide-out basket. Didn't get one.
**Felt:** The sticky bar confirming "M · BLACK" is genuinely reassuring. The add itself is so quiet I double-checked the header number. Why is XS the default though? Nobody's default is XS.
**Next:** continued — peeked at the bag to see the shipping bar

### Step 4 — First look at the bag: £25, first threshold already ticked
**Did:** Tapped BAG [1].
**Got:** Cart page. Top of it: "> £45.00 to free Tracked 24" over a progress bar about a third full, with two markers underneath — "✓ TRACKED 48 FREE" already ticked, "TRACKED 24 FREE" still waiting. My tee: M, BLACK, £25.00, stepper and a bin. Below: "You may also like" — crewneck £50, shorts £45, BLUE WASH OG JEANS £60, jorts £50.
**Expected:** Some "spend more" nudge.
**Felt:** That tick is the £20 line crossed and dusted — free standard postage already sorted at one tee, no drama about it. And it's already pointing me at the next carrot: £45 more for free next-day. I'm literally about to add £60 jeans. Go on then.
**Next:** continued — the jeans were right there in the suggestions

**Expected:** The bar to fill up and celebrate — that's its whole job, and this was THE moment.
**Felt:** Proper double-take. I scrolled back up specifically to watch the bar and it hadn't moved. For about five seconds I genuinely wondered if the jeans had gone in at all — the "Added — 2 in bag" note and the bar were telling me two different stories.
**Next:** hesitated — went to the bag to get the truth

**Felt:** "Unlocked" is a lovely word, and the maths is right. But I had to come here to find out. The product page gaslit me for a minute there.
**Next:** continued — socks to finish

**Felt:** "A HUNDRED AND FIFTY-ONE?!" — out loud. If I were rushing, THIS is where someone pays for two pairs of jeans by accident: same product, same photo, one letter different, stacked rows at 11pm. Binned the M pair, £91.00, breathe. But that's three taps and a product-page round trip to change one size, with a £60 booby trap in the middle.
**Next:** continued — final trim

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached checkout with the outfit at £85.00 (Broadcast tee M + OG jeans L), free Tracked 24 unlocked. The socks didn't survive the final cut, and the store never noticed it lost them: the header said BAG [4] as I checked out with two items.
**Total time:** ~16 minutes — maybe 6 of shopping and 10 of my own dithering, which is the point: the cart got edited seven times and never lost a penny of the maths.
**Worst moment:** The £151.00 cart during the jeans size change — "A HUNDRED AND FIFTY-ONE?!" There's no size control on a cart row, so a swap means add-then-delete with both £60 pairs sitting in the bag in between, identical photos, no "you've got this in another size" warning. Runner-up, said twice: the shipping bar on product pages telling me "£45.00 to free Tracked 24" / "£4.00 to free Tracked 24" seconds AFTER the add that crossed the line — wrong at the only moment it matters, both times.
**Best moment:** The cart page's carriage bar being ruthlessly live and honest: tick at £20 crossed ("✓ TRACKED 48 FREE"), "> Free Tracked 24 — unlocked" at £85, and the instant, no-sugar "> £4.00 to free Tracked 24" the second I binned the tee at £91. Every cart-page number — totals, instalments, the bar — moved the moment I did. Honourable mention: the cart's title link reopening the jeans with my size pre-selected.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the cart survived a tee swap, a size change, a quantity poke and two removals without ever getting the money wrong, and that's the thing basket-builders actually test. But I'd always do a final open-the-bag-and-count before paying, because the header BAG number stopped tracking reality the moment I started editing.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** A size selector on the cart row (or even a "swap size" link that removes the old one). It turns the scariest moment of my visit — £151 of accidental double denim — into a non-event, and it's the single most predictable thing an outfit-builder will do at 11pm. (Second cheapest fix: make the header BAG count and the product-page bar update on add/remove — the data's clearly there, the cart page proves it.)
