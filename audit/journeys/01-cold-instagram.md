# 01 — Tobi, tapped a story on the bus and landed on a £60 pair of jeans from a brand he has never heard of

**Device:** mobile 390×844, one hand, ordinary 4G, standing on a bus · **Goal:** work out what this is, what it costs, whether they have his size, whether it's a real shop, and when it would turn up — in about a minute and a half · **Mood:** curious, thumb already moving, one bump away from closing the tab

*The clock below starts the instant the story link was tapped. Page-load and cookie-sheet times are measured. Everything after that is my own reading and tapping pace, which is the point of a 90-second budget.*

---

### Step 1 — Tapped the link in the story
**Did:** Tapped through from Instagram. Straight onto a product, no homepage, no idea what CROOKSLDN is.
**Got:** **0:02.3** — the whole page arrived in one go. Title, price, photo, all at the same moment. No white flash, no bit-by-bit assembly, nothing jumping.
**Expected:** Four or five seconds of grey boxes, like most links out of Instagram.
**Felt:** Fast. Genuinely fast. That is the single biggest thing a link out of a story has to get right and it got it right.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Looked at what was actually on the screen before touching anything
**Did:** Nothing. Just read the screen.
**Got:** **0:02.4**, half a second behind the page, a `COOKIE CONSENT` panel slid up and took the bottom 43% of the screen — it starts 485px down an 844px screen and runs to the bottom edge. What it landed on top of: **the product's name, the £60.00, the entire size row, and the buy bar.** All four are between 638px and 844px. What was left above it: a shipping strapline, a handcuffs logo, a nav row, `PRODUCT 05 / 12   DENIM`, `PHOTO 1 OF 1`, and a photo of a pair of jeans cropped off at the knee by the top of the panel. (`audit/screens/01-01-landing.png`)
**Expected:** A cookie thing. Everyone has one. I expected it at the bottom, in a strip.
**Felt:** I did not know what the thing in the photo was called or what it cost. I could see it was black baggy jeans with little cartoon hands on the back pockets, and the word `DENIM`. That is all. Three of the five things I came to find out were behind a grey panel written by Shopify.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Read the cookie panel, briefly, because it was in the way
**Did:** Skimmed it while my thumb hunted for the smallest button.
**Got:** `COOKIE CONSENT` then: "We and our partners, including Shopify, use cookies and other technologies to personalize your experience, show you ads, and perform analytics, and we will not use cookies or other technologies for these purposes unless you accept them. Learn more in our Privacy Policy". Then three stacked full-width controls: `Accept`, `Decline`, `Manage preferences`. (`audit/screens/01-01-landing.png`)
**Expected:** Boilerplate.
**Felt:** This is the first full sentence of English this brand ever showed me, and it says *Shopify* and *show you ads*. Everything else on the screen is trying very hard not to look like a Shopify store, and then the biggest block of text on it names Shopify in the second line. I'd not have consciously clocked it, but it's the first impression and it isn't theirs.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Declined
**Did:** **0:07** — tapped `Decline`. Not out of principle; it was the second button and I wanted the panel gone.
**Got:** **0:08** — panel gone. Underneath: `GREY WASH OG JEANS`, `£60.00`, `SIZE`, and a row of five boxes `XS S M L XL`. A bar had appeared stuck to the bottom of the screen reading `GREY WASH … £60.00` with two greyed buttons, `SELECT A SIZE` and `CHECKOUT NOW`. (`audit/screens/01-03-consent-gone.png`)
**Expected:** The panel to go away.
**Felt:** Relief, and then a small annoyance that all of this had been sitting behind the panel the whole time. Also: `Decline` is a real button the same size as `Accept`, not hidden inside `Manage preferences`. Most sites bury it. I noticed.
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — Read the name and the price
**Did:** Read the two lines that had just appeared.
**Got:** `GREY WASH OG JEANS` and `£60.00`. Nothing else — no "was £80", no discount, no timer, no "23 people are looking at this".
**Expected:** Some kind of push. There always is.
**Felt:** £60 is a normal price for jeans from a small label, and the absence of a fake discount actually reads as confidence rather than as a missing feature. The name is plain English, which after `PRODUCT 05 / 12` I wasn't sure it would be.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Flicked up to see the whole photo
**Did:** One thumb flick to get the jeans fully on screen.
**Got:** The jeans, flat, from the back, on black. Cartoon hands in chains printed on both back pockets. Under it: `PHOTO 1 OF 1`. (`audit/screens/01-03-consent-gone.png`)
**Expected:** To swipe sideways through four or five photos, at least one on a person.
**Felt:** This is where it started going wrong for me. **One photo.** I came from a video of somebody wearing these. The page shows me the back of a pair of trousers lying flat. I can't see the front, I can't see the fit on a body, I can't see how long they are on someone. £60 is not a lot of money but it is enough that I want to see the thing on a human, and there is nothing here. `PHOTO 1 OF 1` is honest, and it is also the page telling me it has nothing else to show me.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 7 — Tapped a size, and got it wrong
**Did:** **~0:14** — went for `M` and hit `S`. Bus, one hand.
**Got:** `S` filled in solid purple with a ring around it. A line appeared under the row reading `IN STOCK`. The bottom bar went from grey to purple and rewrote itself to `GREY WASH OG JEANS  £60.00 · S`, with `ADD TO BAG` and `CHECKOUT NOW` now live. (`audit/screens/01-06-size-wrong.png`)
**Expected:** To see the box I hit go dark.
**Felt:** No confusion at all about what I'd selected — the selected box is unmistakable and the bottom bar repeats it back to me with the price. That's better than most.
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Corrected it to M
**Did:** **~0:18** — tapped `M`.
**Got:** `S` went back to an outline, `M` filled in, bottom bar changed to `£60.00 · M`. `IN STOCK` still there. Instant. (`audit/screens/01-07-size-mine.png`)
**Expected:** That.
**Felt:** Fixing a mis-tap cost me one tap and no thought. Good. And `IN STOCK` in plain capitals is exactly the right two words — I know where I stand.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Noticed a small purple square on the XL box
**Did:** Looked at the size row properly.
**Got:** A tiny purple square in the top-right corner of the `XL` box. Nothing else on the page mentions it. It's on `XL` whether or not `M` is selected. (`audit/screens/01-10-buy-band-M.png`)
**Expected:** N/A — I don't know what it is.
**Felt:** It looks like it's trying to tell me something — last one? about to go? — and I have no way to find out. A mark with no key is just a smudge. Minor, but on a page this sparse every extra mark gets read.
**Next:** continued

### Step 10 — Read the two lines about shipping, one after the other
**Did:** **~0:22** — read down from the size row.
**Got:** In this order, stacked, with nothing between them:
`Order before 18:00 and it ships today (Mon–Sat)`
`> Ordered now — leaves tomorrow`
Then the big `ADD TO BAG`, and under it a bright violet `Buy with shop` button, then `More payment options`. (`audit/screens/01-10-buy-band-M.png`) *(It was 18:27 in London when I looked.)*
**Expected:** One line telling me when it turns up.
**Felt:** I read those two lines three times. "Ships today" then "leaves tomorrow", directly under each other. I worked out afterwards that one is the rule and one is what the rule means *right now*, and that I'd missed the 18:00 cutoff by half an hour — but on a bus, in the second I gave it, it just looked like the page contradicting itself. The second line is the true one and it's the one in smaller, dimmer text.
And neither of them answers what I actually asked, which is **when does it land on my doormat.** "Leaves tomorrow" is their end of it, not mine.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 11 — Clocked the Shop Pay button
**Did:** Looked at the violet button under `ADD TO BAG`.
**Got:** `Buy with shop`, in Shopify's own bright violet, the brightest thing on an otherwise black-and-grey page. (`audit/screens/01-10-buy-band-M.png`)
**Expected:** Nothing in particular.
**Felt:** Honestly? This is the single strongest "you will not lose your money here" signal on the whole page, and it isn't theirs. I recognise it, I've paid with it before, and my card details are already in it. It sticks out badly against everything else on the page — it's the only rounded, coloured, branded thing on screen — and I'd still keep it, because for a stranger it does more work than any amount of design.
**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Went looking for who these people are
**Did:** **~0:30** — flicked down fast, past four closed strips — `SPECIFICATION +`, `ITEM DESCRIPTION +`, `MEASUREMENTS +`, `CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS +` — past `MORE FROM THIS DROP` (`BLUE WASH OG JEANS £60.00`, `BLUE WASH JORTS £50.00`, `GREY WASH JORTS £50.00`) and kept going to the bottom. (`audit/screens/01-05-buy-area.png`)
**Got:** **~0:45** — a footer: `SHOP` (NEW, TEES, DENIM, SWEATS, ACCESSORIES), `INFORMATION` (QUESTIONS, TERMS, SHIPPING, REFUNDS, TRACK ORDER), `CONTACT` (INSTAGRAM, TIKTOK, EMAIL), `GAME` (Play CROOKSLDN: The Getaway), and at the very bottom `EVIDENCE TERMINAL V0.2 // CROOKSLDN // OWN THE STREETS™ // © 2026`. (`audit/screens/01-12-footer.png`)
**Expected:** An about line. A "we're two people in Hackney". Something.
**Felt:** The footer is tidy and everything you'd want a link to is in it — returns, shipping, tracking, questions — but there is no sentence anywhere telling me who these people are. `EVIDENCE TERMINAL V0.2` is a joke I'd have enjoyed if I already trusted them; arriving cold it's one more thing that isn't a name. The `Play CROOKSLDN: The Getaway` link is the most human thing on the page, and it's a game.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 13 — Weighed up whether this is real
**Did:** Sat with it for a few seconds.
**Got:** For: `PRODUCT 05 / 12` — twelve products, not forty thousand, which is not what a dropshipper's catalogue looks like. Nothing on the page is fake-urgent. There's an Instagram and a TikTok link. There's a `© 2026` and a `TRACK ORDER` page. Against: no reviews anywhere, no photo of the product being worn, no photo of a person, no company name, no address, nothing that says who is behind it.
**Expected:** At least a star rating.
**Felt:** The terminal look reads as **a real shop being deliberately odd**, not as a scam. And the reason is specific: the money bits are boring. `£60.00`, `SIZE M`, `IN STOCK`, `ADD TO BAG` — plain, unstyled, un-clever. Scam sites do the opposite; they look like a normal shop and then shout at you. This shouts nowhere near my wallet. Nobody sets up a fake shop with twelve products, one photo each and no discounts.
But "not a scam" isn't the same as "I know who you are." There is no human anywhere on this page.
**Next:** continued

### Step 14 — Opened `CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS` because it was the only strip with the word SHIPPING in it
**Did:** **~0:55** — scrolled back up and tapped it.
**Got:** Four numbered blocks:
`01 LOGGED` — "Orders placed before 18:00 are dispatched the same day, Monday to Saturday. After 18:00, the next dispatch day."
`02 DISPATCHED` — "Shipped with Royal Mail Tracked. Free UK shipping over £20, and free Tracked 24 over £70."
`03 IN TRANSIT` — "Tracking issued by email. **UK 1–2 working days.** International 7–14 working days."
`04 DELIVERED` — "You have 14 days from delivery to return unworn goods with tags attached. Return postage is yours unless we sent the wrong thing or it arrived faulty. Start a return by email: crooksldn@gmail.com."
(`audit/screens/01-acc-chain.png`)
**Expected:** Shipping prices.
**Felt:** **1:15 in, and there it is: "UK 1–2 working days."** The one sentence I have been looking for the whole time is the third of four blocks inside the fourth of four strips, all of which are shut when you land. Royal Mail Tracked is a real answer, 14 days to return is a real answer, and they're both good — you just have to go digging in a drawer marked `CHAIN OF CUSTODY` to find them.
The bit that stopped me: the only way to contact these people about a £60 order is **crooksldn@gmail.com**. A gmail address. It's the one detail on the entire page that made me think *maybe this is a bloke in a bedroom*. Everything else says small-and-serious; that says small-and-improvised.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 15 — Added it to the bag instead of buying it
**Did:** **~1:25** — tapped `ADD TO BAG`.
**Got:** A line appeared under the buttons: `> Added — 1 in bag` with `View bag` next to it, and the counter in the header went to `BAG [1]`. Took a couple of seconds. The bottom bar swapped its price for the same message, wrapping `> Added — 1 in bag` across three lines and dropping `£60.00 · M` off it. (`audit/screens/01-16-added-confirmation.png`)
**Expected:** A cart panel to fly in over the screen, like everywhere else.
**Felt:** Quiet, and I liked it. Nothing covered the screen, nothing yanked me somewhere else, and I could still see the jeans. `Added — 1 in bag` plus the header going to `[1]` is enough — two things agreeing is more convincing than a big animation. The bottom bar losing the price to fit the message is scruffy but it didn't cost me anything.
**Next:** stopped — my stop was coming and I'd decided.

## Outcome

### The five questions, and when the answers arrived

| # | Question | Answered at | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What is this?** | **0:02** partly, **0:08** properly, **never** fully | At 0:02 I had a photo and the word `DENIM` — the name was behind the cookie sheet until 0:08. What they actually *are* — "14oz denim, OG straight cut, mid rise. Structured, not baggy. Made in Portugal." — is inside `ITEM DESCRIPTION`, shut on arrival. I never opened it inside 90 seconds. |
| 2 | **What does it cost?** | **0:08** | `£60.00`. It was rendered at 0:02 and sat behind the consent sheet for six seconds. Shipping cost, oddly, was the *first* thing answered — `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20` is the top line of the page at 0:02. |
| 3 | **Do they have my size?** | **0:18** | Sizes were visible at 0:08 but nothing tells you what's available until you tap one and get `IN STOCK`. For M, one tap, instant, unambiguous. |
| 4 | **Is this real?** | **partly, ~1:00 — never properly** | Nothing on the page proves the brand exists. The best I got was negative evidence (twelve products, no fake urgency, no fake discounts), the Shop Pay button at ~0:22, and Instagram/TikTok links in the footer at ~0:45. No reviews, no faces, no company name. The one contact detail is a gmail address at 1:15. |
| 5 | **When would it arrive?** | **1:15, and only because I went looking** | The buy area tells you when it *leaves* (`Ordered now — leaves tomorrow`) and contradicts itself doing it. When it *arrives* — "UK 1–2 working days" — is block 3 of 4 inside the 4th accordion, closed by default. Most people in my position never see it. |

**Bought / didn't:** Didn't. Added to the bag at ~1:25 and left it there, which in practice means I didn't buy. Two things stopped me and neither was the design: **one flat photo of a £60 pair of jeans I'd just watched someone wear**, and not knowing when they'd land without going digging.

**Total time:** ~1 minute 30. I didn't bounce — I never came close. The page was fast, the price was obvious and the size took one tap. I ran out of *reasons*, not patience.

**Worst moment:** 0:02, watching a Shopify-worded grey panel drop onto the name, the price, the size row and the buy button all at once, leaving me a cropped photo and the words `PRODUCT 05 / 12`. Out loud: *"what even is this, then."*

**Best moment:** 0:18. Tapping `M` and having the box fill in, `IN STOCK` appear, and the bar at the bottom rewrite itself to `£60.00 · M` with the buttons going live. Three separate confirmations of one tap, no lag, no doubt. That is the best-built thing on the page and it's exactly the bit that has to work.

**Would they come back?** Yes — and this surprised me. It looks like nothing else and it doesn't behave like a shop trying to trick me. I'd have gone to the Instagram from the footer to see the jeans on a person, and if the account looked alive I'd have bought from the tab still sitting open. The terminal thing is a reason to remember them, not a reason to distrust them. That only holds because the money words are plain: the second `£60.00` had been styled as `EXHIBIT VALUE: 60` I'd have gone.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** A second photo of the jeans on a person. That's it, and it's not a theme change. Failing that: put **`UK 1–2 working days`** on the buy band next to `IN STOCK`, instead of three levels down inside `CHAIN OF CUSTODY`, and don't print the 18:00 rule directly above the sentence that says the rule has already been missed.
