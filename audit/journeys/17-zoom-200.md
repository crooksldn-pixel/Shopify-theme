# 17 — Dev, 34, partially sighted, lives at 200% browser zoom
**Device:** desktop 1440x900 at 200% zoom (effective 720x450 viewport), fast connection · **Goal:** treat himself to a CROOKS tee he's seen on his feed · **Mood:** relaxed Tuesday-night browse, but braced — most shops fall apart at 200%

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened the site, waited for it to settle.
**Got:** A near-black screen that went dimmer while something loaded, with a huge COOKIE CONSENT panel filling the bottom half of the screen — at 200% it covers 47% of the viewport. Behind the dimming he could just make out "14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE". (17-01-arrival.png)
**Expected:** A homepage he could actually see.
**Felt:** "Half my screen is cookie small print and the other half's gone dark. Give me a second to even find the page."
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 2 — The popup game appeared
**Did:** Before he touched the cookie banner, a full-screen "CRACK THE CUFFS — ESCAPE WITH A PRIVATE DISCOUNT" thing took over. Read it. Big monospace type, genuinely easy to read at zoom, and a nice big × in the corner if he wanted out. A discount though — he tapped BEGIN. (17-02-popup.png)
**Got:** A 10-second countdown and three big tumbler tiles marked TAP — stop all three before time runs out. (17-04b-after-begin-click.png)
**Expected:** Some sort of scratch-card. A reflex game was a worry — his eyes need a moment to find things.
**Felt:** "Timer games and my eyes don't mix. But those tiles are massive, fair play. Go on then."
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Played, and won
**Did:** Tapped the three tumblers one at a time, roughly a second and a half each.
**Got:** All three locked inside the time. Then, instead of a code: "SECURE YOUR LINE — STEP 1 OF 2", asking for his mobile number and consent to marketing texts. (17-06-game-result.png)
**Expected:** "You cracked it — here's your code."
**Felt:** "Hang on. I won. Now you want my number before I get the prize? That's not what BEGIN said." Wants the discount, so — fine.
**Next:** hesitated, then continued

### Step 4 — Gave his number
**Did:** Typed his mobile, ticked "I agree CROOKSLDN can text me", pressed CONTINUE. The form itself was big and clear at zoom. (17-07b-phone-agree.png)
**Got:** "UNLOCK YOUR CODE — STEP 2 OF 2." An email field, an optional marketing tick-box, a birthday field labelled "Optional — for a gift on your birthday" — in American mm/dd/yyyy, on a UK shop — and a greyed-out REVEAL MY CODE button.
**Expected:** The code. It said step 1 of 2, so one more click, surely.
**Felt:** "Step two of two is… more of my details. And why is my birthday in American?"
**Next:** continued, warily

### Step 5 — Tried to reveal without the email
**Did:** Everything on this screen read as optional, so he pressed REVEAL MY CODE straight away.
**Got:** Nothing — the button was dimmed out, dead until an email goes in. So the email isn't optional at all; only the birthday is. (17-11-code-revealed.png)
**Expected:** The word "Optional" to mean optional.
**Felt:** "So it's my phone AND my email for this code. You could have said that before I played."
**Next:** grumbled, typed his email

### Step 6 — REVEAL MY CODE… revealed nothing
**Did:** Entered his email, watched the button light up white, pressed REVEAL MY CODE. Waited. Pressed it again. Waited. Tried once more, even ticking the marketing box in case that was the trick.
**Got:** Nothing. No code, no error, no spinner. The screen just sat there. Every press, same silence. (17-14-after-reveal.png)
**Expected:** A discount code — he'd won the game, handed over a phone number and an email for it.
**Felt:** "You are joking. I gave you everything and the button's a dud. That's my number gone for nothing." Genuinely annoyed now.
**Next:** gave up on the discount — closed it with the big × (which, credit, is huge and easy to hit)

### Step 7 — Cleared the cookie wall
**Did:** The cookie panel was still sat over the bottom half of the page. Hit Accept — the buttons were big and clearly separated at zoom. (17-07-after-popup-close.png)
**Got:** The homepage, finally, maybe four minutes after arriving.
**Expected:** To be shopping well before now.
**Felt:** "Right. Can I actually look at some clothes now?"
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Homepage at 200%
**Did:** Scrolled down the homepage properly. (17-20-home-top.png)
**Got:** Big pixel-type headline, "14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", a clean header that all fits on one row — CATALOGUE, SEARCH, BAG [0], LIGHT MODE, MENU — and product cards stacked two across. Nothing clipped, nothing overlapping, and crucially no sideways scrolling anywhere. (17-21-home-scroll2.png)
**Expected:** The usual 200% mess — nav collapsed on top of itself, text spilling out of boxes.
**Felt:** "Oh. This actually holds together. That almost never happens." The dark background with big pale mono text suits his eyes, honestly.
**Next:** continued — clicked CATALOGUE

### Step 9 — The catalogue
**Did:** Scanned the grid, two columns, big flat-lay photos, "FLAT / ON MODEL" toggle and category filters along the top, all legible. Scrolled until a tee caught his eye. (17-22-catalogue-top.png)
**Got:** "NO. 07 — MONEY CLIVE TEE — £25.00 — AVAILABLE". Price and stock readable at a glance. Clicked it. (17-23-catalogue-evil-clive.png)
**Expected:** Squinting at tiny price labels. Didn't have to.
**Felt:** "Twenty-five quid, available, sold. Easy."
**Next:** continued

### Step 10 — Product page, and a small trap dodged
**Did:** Landed on the tee's page. At his zoom the photo fills the whole first screen — but a sticky bar sat pinned along the bottom: "MONEY CLIVE TEE £25.00 · XS · BLACK — ADD TO BAG / CHECKOUT NOW". (17-24-pdp-top.png)
**Got:** Buy buttons in reach without a single scroll — but preloaded with XS, and he's a Medium. The bar does at least spell the size out. Scrolled two screens down to the size buttons — five big tiles, XS to XL — tapped M, and the sticky bar changed to "£25.00 · M · BLACK". (17-27-size-M-selected.png)
**Expected:** To hunt for the buy button. Instead it found him — he just had to notice it was about to sell him the wrong size.
**Felt:** "Good job I read that little XS. Someone in more of a hurry buys a child-sized tee here." But the size tiles themselves: big, obvious, one tap.
**Next:** continued

### Step 11 — Added to bag… did it work?
**Did:** Tapped ADD TO BAG in the sticky bar.
**Got:** Nothing he could see. No message, no drawer, no tick, button label unchanged. The bag counter did change — but it lives in the header, two screens up, way outside his zoomed view. (17-28-after-add.png)
**Expected:** Some acknowledgement where his eyes were — anything.
**Felt:** "Did that go in or not?" Classic. He tapped it again to be safe.
**Next:** continued, unknowingly with two tees in the bag

### Step 12 — BAG [2]?!
**Did:** Scrolled back to the top to check the header. (17-31-header-bag-count.png)
**Got:** "BAG [2]". Both taps had worked, silently. Clicked BAG.
**Expected:** BAG [1].
**Felt:** "Two? I pressed it twice because you told me nothing the first time. That one's on you, not me."
**Next:** continued to the cart to fix it

### Step 13 — Fixed the quantity in the cart
**Did:** Cart page: "Cart 2 — MONEY CLIVE TEE, M, BLACK — £50.00", with a big − 2 + control and a bin icon. Tapped −. (17-32-bag-open.png)
**Got:** Quantity 1, £25.00, updated cleanly. One niggle: the header still read BAG [2] until the next page load, which made him double-check the line item. The shipping meter up top — "£20.00 to free Tracked 24… ✓ TRACKED 48 FREE" — took a second read, but the bit that mattered (free delivery already secured) landed. (17-34-after-decrease.png)
**Expected:** Fixing it to be a faff. It wasn't — controls were big and obvious.
**Felt:** "Cart's the clearest page on the site. Even the bin icon's a decent size."
**Next:** continued — hit Check out

### Step 14 — Checkout
**Did:** Pressed the big Check out button. (17-35-cart-summary.png)
**Got:** Shopify checkout — and a flashbang: pure white after twenty minutes of near-black. At 200% it's a lot of bright screen. But: single column, nothing cut off, no sideways scroll, big fields. Filled email, name, address without one mis-tap. (17-36-checkout-top.png, 17-39-checkout-filled.png)
**Expected:** The checkout to be the safest part. It was, once his eyes adjusted.
**Felt:** "Warn a man before you switch the lights on. But every box is where it should be."
**Next:** continued

### Step 15 — Shipping confirmed, stopped at payment
**Did:** Watched the shipping options load: "Tracked 48 — FREE" and "Tracked 24 — £4.99". Picked free in his head, scrolled as far as the Payment section — card fields ready. (17-42-shipping-loaded.png)
**Got:** A complete, correct order — one Medium black tee, £25, free delivery — one card number away from done.
**Expected:** Exactly this.
**Felt:** "That's a purchase I could finish, no help, no guesswork. The shop passed. The discount game failed."
**Next:** stopped (audit ends before payment)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached payment with the right item, right size, £25 + free Tracked 48. The theme never once forced horizontal scrolling or hid a control off-screen at 200%; every step of the purchase was completable.
**Total time:** ~19 minutes — of which nearly 7 were the popup game and its phone/email toll-booth that never paid out.
**Worst moment:** Pressing REVEAL MY CODE — after winning the game and handing over his phone number and his email — and getting absolute silence. Three presses, no code, no error. "I gave you my number for a button that does nothing."
**Best moment:** The product page's sticky buy bar — title, price and selected size always visible at the bottom of his tiny effective viewport, updating live when he tapped M. For someone who usually loses the buy button entirely at 200%, that's the whole game. (Runner-up: a cart page with genuinely big controls.)
**Would they come back?** For the clothes and the layout, yes — the site is unusually livable at 200%. But he'd never touch a CROOKS "discount" popup again, and he half expects spam texts for nothing.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Visible add-to-bag feedback where the button is (an "ADDED ✓" state or opening the bag) — it would have saved the double-add, the scroll-up to check, and the cart correction. And if the discount code had actually revealed, he'd have left delighted instead of ~£2.50-in-time poorer and slightly burned.
