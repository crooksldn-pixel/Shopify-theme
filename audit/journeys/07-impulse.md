# 07 — Kaz, in and out for a £6 pair of socks and nothing else

**Device:** mobile 390×844, ordinary 4G, one thumb, standing up · **Goal:** socks, £6, done — not browsing, not reading · **Mood:** brisk and slightly impatient; the whole trip should cost less attention than the socks cost money

---

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage and got a cookie panel instead of a shop
**Did:** Opened the site. Waited about three seconds without touching anything.
**Got:** Black terminal-looking page. Thin strip across the top: `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH`. Handcuffs logo, `CATALOGUE  SEARCH  BAG [0]  LIGHT MODE  MENU`, `> 12 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE`, big `CROOKSLDN`, `OWN THE STREETS™`, a purple `CATALOGUE` button. Then a grey slab came up over the bottom half: `COOKIE CONSENT` — "We and our partners, including Shopify, use cookies and other technologies to personalize your experience, show you ads, and perform analytics, and we will not use cookies or other technologies for these purposes unless you accept them. Learn more in our Privacy Policy", with `Accept`, `Decline`, `Manage preferences`. (`audit/screens/07-01-landing.png`)
**Expected:** Products.
**Felt:** I've seen no product yet. The panel takes the bottom half of the screen and it's written in a completely different voice to everything above it — sentence case, "personalize" with a z, a paragraph long. The site is shouting in mono capitals and then a lawyer leans in. At least `Decline` is a real button next to `Accept` and not hidden in `Manage preferences`.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Tapped Decline, and a second full-screen thing took its place
**Did:** Tapped `Decline`.
**Got:** The cookie slab went. Instantly the whole screen went black and I got a takeover: `CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY`, "Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited.", a purple `RUN IT`, an outlined `NOT NOW`, and small print "One code per player. Code expires 20 minutes after you win." An `×` in the top-left corner. (`audit/screens/07-02-getaway-takeover.png`)
**Expected:** The shop.
**Felt:** Two blockers back to back, and the second one is worse because it's the whole screen, not half of it. I'd have shrugged at either on its own — it's the queue of them. And the offer is aimed at the wrong person: 10% off a £6 order is sixty pence, and it wants me to play a game and hand over my phone number to get it. I'm here for socks. The `×` is up in the top-left, which on a phone held in the right hand is the one corner my thumb can't reach, so I used `NOT NOW` instead — which is at least honest wording.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Tapped NOT NOW, and finally saw the shop
**Did:** Tapped `NOT NOW`.
**Got:** The homepage, clear at last. `CATALOGUE  12 ITEMS`, toggles `FLAT / ON MODEL / OUTLINE`, filters `ALL T-SHIRT DENIM SWEATS ACCESSORIES`, then a numbered two-column list — `NO. 01` through `NO. 12`, each with a category, a name, a price and `AVAILABLE`. (`audit/screens/07-03-home-clear.png`)
**Expected:** This, two taps ago.
**Felt:** Right — *now* I like it. Every price is printed on the list. No "from £", nothing behind a tap. That's the first thing on this site that's respected my time.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Scrolled straight down for the cheapest thing on the page
**Did:** Flicked down the list looking for socks. Didn't read a single word of the copy on the way.
**Got:** `NO. 10  ACCESSORIES  /  BLACK/BLUE MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS  /  £6.00  /  AVAILABLE`, with a clear photo of black and blue socks. `NO. 11` is `WHITE/RED MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS £6.00` right below it. (`audit/screens/07-04-socks-card.png`)
**Expected:** To have to open two or three products to find the price.
**Felt:** Perfect. Two seconds of scrolling, price on the tile, photo actually shows the socks, and I could see the whole cheap end of the shop in one glance — two pairs of socks at £6 and a bag at £18, then it jumps to £25. Took the black/blue ones because they were first.
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — Tapped the socks and got a page that asked me for a size
**Did:** Tapped the card.
**Got:** `← CATALOGUE`, `PRODUCT 10 / 12`, `ACCESSORIES`, one photo, `PHOTO 1 OF 1`, then `BLACK/BLUE MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS`, `£6.00`. Under the price, a small purple heading `QUANTITY` and four boxes: `1pc  3pc  6pc  12pc`. Under those a grey line, `Select Quantity`. Then two dispatch lines. Then the big button — greyed out — reading **`SELECT A SIZE`**. The bar stuck to the bottom of the screen said the same thing. (`audit/screens/07-05-socks-pdp.png`, `07-06-buy-controls.png`)
**Expected:** `£6.00` and an add button.
**Felt:** I stopped for a second, which on a £6 purchase is a second too long. The heading says `QUANTITY`, the boxes say `1pc / 3pc / 6pc / 12pc`, the little grey line says `Select Quantity` — three things all telling me the same true thing — and then the one control I actually need shouts `SELECT A SIZE`, twice, in the two biggest bits of type on the screen. **I genuinely thought for a moment that a size row hadn't loaded and I was about to be asked shoe size.** I looked back up the page for one. There isn't one. Socks don't have one here. The small grey line was right and the two huge buttons were wrong, and the huge buttons are the ones you read. Nobody abandons over this, but on a £6 impulse buy the thing you're selling is *no friction*, and that's a bump.
**Next:** continued

### Step 6 — Tapped 1pc, and everything came alive
**Did:** Tapped `1pc`.
**Got:** The box lit purple. `Select Quantity` became **`IN STOCK`**. The big button became `ADD TO BAG`. The sticky bar became `BLACK/BLUE MO…  £6.00 · 1pc` with `ADD TO BAG` and `CHECKOUT NOW`, both now live. A bright blue `Buy with Shop` button and `More payment options` appeared under `ADD TO BAG`. (`audit/screens/07-07-1pc-selected.png`, `07-08-ready-to-buy.png`)
**Expected:** Exactly that.
**Felt:** Fast and unambiguous, and the sticky bar quoting `£6.00 · 1pc` back at me is genuinely good — it told me what I'd be paying for what, without scrolling. One thing sits wrong: the blue `Buy with Shop` button with round corners is the only thing on the entire site that looks like every other Shopify shop, and it's directly under the one button they designed. I ignored it, because on a shop I'd never heard of ten seconds ago I'm not signing into anything.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — Tapped CHECKOUT NOW and was at a checkout. Five taps, twenty-seven seconds.
**Did:** Tapped `CHECKOUT NOW` in the bar at the bottom.
**Got:** Straight to a Shopify checkout — no cart page, no drawer, no confirmation step. `CROOKSLDN` at the top, `Order summary` `£6.00`, `Express checkout` with Shop / PayPal / G Pay, `OR`, then `Contact`, `Email or mobile phone number`, and a pre-ticked `Keep me updated.` Then `Delivery`, `United Kingdom` already selected. (`audit/screens/07-09-checkout.png`)
**Expected:** A cart page in between. Was pleased not to get one.
**Felt:** That is the best thing this site does and nobody's told you about it. **Landing to checkout in five taps, and two of those five were spent closing things I didn't ask for.** Three taps of actual shopping: the product, the quantity, the button. `CHECKOUT NOW` skipping the bag entirely is exactly right for a £6 buy. The country was already `United Kingdom`, which saved me a fiddle with a dropdown of 200 countries.

The jolt is the paint job: I've spent thirty seconds in a black terminal and I'm now on a white Shopify form with blue links and rounded buttons. For half a second I checked the name at the top to be sure I hadn't been bounced somewhere else. Everyone's checkout looks like this so it doesn't kill trust — but the site's whole pitch is that it isn't a normal Shopify shop, and the moment I get my card out it becomes one.
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — Typed my details, and found out what postage costs
**Did:** Typed an email and an address — test values, `buyer+b07@example.com`, `Test Buyer`, `1 Test Street`, `London`, `SW1A 1AA`. Six fields.
**Got:** After the postcode, a `Shipping method` block appeared with two rows: **`Tracked 48  ·  Wed, 26 Aug–Fri, 28 Aug  ·  £3.00`** (pre-selected) and **`Tracked 24  ·  Mon, 24 Aug–Tue, 25 Aug  ·  £4.99`**. The total at the bottom went from £6.00 to **`Total  1 item  GBP  £9.00`**, above `Pay now`. (`audit/screens/07-10-checkout-address.png`, `07-15-shipping-methods.png`, `07-11-checkout-bottom.png`)
**Expected:** Postage, yes. Not half the price of the socks.
**Felt:** £3 on top of £6. Fifty per cent. And this is the **first screen in the entire journey that names a postage price** — I had to type a full address to find it out, at the last possible moment, one field away from the card box. Nothing on the product page said "£3.00". The only shipping words I'd seen anywhere were the strip at the top of the homepage, `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20`, which tells me what's free and never what's charged.

Would I have paid £9 for £6 socks? Honestly — grudgingly, yes, because I've already typed my address in and I want socks. But I said "you're joking" at the screen, and if I'd had to type all that again for any reason I'd have gone.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 9 — Read the delivery dates twice
**Did:** Looked at the two dates properly, because £4.99 was on the table.
**Got:** It's Thursday 20 August. `Tracked 24` says `Mon, 24 Aug–Tue, 25 Aug`. `Tracked 48` says `Wed, 26 Aug–Fri, 28 Aug`. Back on the product page it had said `Order before 18:00 and it ships today (Mon–Sat)` and, on the very next line, `> Ordered now — leaves tomorrow`. (`audit/screens/07-15-shipping-methods.png`, `07-06-buy-controls.png`)
**Expected:** "Tracked 24" to mean roughly tomorrow-ish.
**Felt:** This is the bit that would actually have lost the sale if I'd needed them for the weekend. I'm being offered a service called **24** that arrives in four to five days, and one called **48** that arrives in six to eight. Whatever the real reason, from where I'm standing the names and the dates flatly disagree, and it makes the cheap option feel like the only honest one. The product page didn't help either: it told me it ships today *and* that it leaves tomorrow, in two consecutive lines, and never said which applied to me right now.
**Next:** continued — but I stopped at the payment step and didn't pay

**Next:** continued

**Got:** The same band on both, in the same place, right under the header: `> £14.00 to free Tracked 48`. (`audit/screens/07-14-pdp-reloaded-with-6.png`)
**Expected:** That it lived only on the cart.

**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Did the sum it wanted me to do
**Did:** Asked myself the question the bar was asking: is it worth £14 more to save the postage?
**Got:** The catalogue's cheap end, from memory of the homepage list: socks £6, socks £6, duffle £18, then it jumps to tees at £25. So from £6, the second pair of socks takes me to £12 — **still short**. The cheapest thing that actually gets me over £20 is the £18 duffle, taking me to £24.
**Expected:** To find a £14-ish thing and be tempted.
**Felt:** No. Not a chance. To save £3.00 I'd have to spend at least £18, and the only item that does it is a bag I don't want. The bar isn't nagging me, it isn't in my way — it's just **arithmetically incapable of moving me**, because there is nothing in this shop between £6 and £18. If the gap had been £4 and there'd been a second £6 pair to close it, I'd have thrown it in without thinking; that's exactly the kind of shopper I am. Here the honest read is that at £6 I'm simply not the customer the threshold was built for, and the bar's real message to me is "you're a small order".
**Next:** stopped at the payment step, as instructed — never submitted

## Outcome

**Bought / didn't:** Didn't — walked to the payment step and stopped there, per the audit rule. Left honestly: I'd have paid the £9.00, resenting the £3.00, and I'd have picked `Tracked 48` because paying £4.99 for something called *24* that lands on Monday is insulting.

**Total time:** 27 seconds from landing to a live checkout screen. About two and a half minutes to the payment step with an address typed in.

**Worst moment:** The `QUANTITY` row headed by a `SELECT A SIZE` button. Out loud: *"Size? They're socks. Where's the size?"* — I scrolled back up the page hunting for a row that doesn't exist. Second place, and much more expensive: finding out postage is £3.00 on a £6 order at the very last screen, after typing six fields.

**Best moment:** `CHECKOUT NOW` in the sticky bar. It skipped the bag entirely and put me on a checkout with the right item and the right price in one tap, with the country already set to United Kingdom. That, plus every price being printed on the homepage list, is why this took five taps instead of nine.

**Would they come back?** Yes, for the other pair of socks, and I'd be quicker next time because the cookie panel and the game only happen once. What I would not do is come back specifically to buy one £6 item again knowing it's £9 at the till — next time I'd wait until I wanted something bigger, which is precisely the behaviour the free-delivery threshold is meant to cause and the £20 bar never actually asked me for.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Print the postage price on the product page — `£3.00 Tracked 48 / £4.99 Tracked 24, free over £20` — next to the dispatch line, in the same plain English as `IN STOCK` and `£6.00`. Everything else here is honest to a fault; postage is the one number the shop makes you work for, and it's the one that turns a £6 impulse into a £9 decision.

### The question this journey exists for

**Does the carriage bar tempt you into spending more, or just get in the way?**

**Neither — it never appeared.** On the five-tap route the free-delivery bar is not rendered once. With an empty bag it is absent from the homepage and the product page; the fastest path then goes product → checkout without ever loading another store page, and the bar lives on store pages only. The only free-delivery words on my whole route were the rotating strip at the top of the homepage, `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH`, seen at second zero with nothing in my bag and no reason to care — and it had rotated away to `12 PRODUCTS CURRENTLY ONLINE` by the time I'd have cared.

**Was I ever told, at a moment I could act on it?** Once, and only if I'd taken the slower route: the cart page shows `> £14.00 to free Tracked 48` above the fold, directly over the `Check out` button (`07-13-cart-carriage-bar.png`). That is the right message in the right place — and `CHECKOUT NOW`, the site's own best feature, routes straight past it.

**Would I have added something?** No, and not because of how the message is worded — because of the catalogue. I'm £14 short. The next-cheapest item is the other £6 socks, which only gets me to £12; the cheapest thing that clears £20 is the £18 duffle. **Spending £18 to save £3.00 is not a nudge, it's a joke.** Worth being clear that the mechanism itself is fine: had the gap been £4 with a £6 item available, I'd have added it instantly. The threshold just has nothing to sell to a £6 shopper.

**What postage did I end up expecting to pay, and where did I learn it?** **£3.00**, for `Tracked 48` — pre-selected, £9.00 total. I learned it on the checkout page, in the `Shipping method` block, *after* typing email, first name, last name, address, city and postcode — the last screen before the card fields (`07-15-shipping-methods.png`). No page before that named a price for postage. The closed `CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS` accordion on the product page comes closest, with "Shipped with Royal Mail Tracked. Free UK shipping over £20, and free Tracked 24 over £70" — which tells a shopper only what is free, never what it costs if it isn't.

### One thing this journey did *not* find

The sticky bar's `ADD TO BAG` and `CHECKOUT NOW` appeared completely dead — three taps in a row swallowed with nothing happening. That is Shopify's own preview bar sitting invisibly across the bottom 68px of the preview URL, as recorded in `RUN-NOTES.md`, not the theme. With it out of the way `CHECKOUT NOW` works first time, every time. Noted here so it does not get re-reported as a fault.
