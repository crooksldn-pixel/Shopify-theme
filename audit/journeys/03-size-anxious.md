# 03 — Dan, size-anxious denim buyer burned twice before by "baggy" fits
**Device:** mobile 390×844, normal network · **Goal:** the £60 BLUE WASH OG JEANS — but only if he can find real measurements and a sane swap policy, because his waist (~33") sits between M and L in every brand's chart · **Mood:** wanting the jeans but braced for disappointment; last two pairs of "baggy" jeans went back

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage, got ambushed twice
**Did:** Opened the site around 21:20. A full-screen "CRACK THE CUFFS — ACCESS BREACH DETECTED" game popup was sitting on top of a big cookie consent banner. Tapped the × on the popup, then tapped ACCEPT on the cookies.
**Got:** Two taps of admin before seeing any clothes. Under it: a dark evidence-terminal homepage, "14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", clear CATALOGUE button.
**Expected:** A cookie banner, sure. Not a mini-game stacked on top of it.
**Felt:** "Two things to close before I've seen a single pair of jeans. The discount game looks fun, but not tonight — I'm here to check a measurement, not defuse handcuffs."
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Into the catalogue, scrolled straight past the jeans
**Did:** Tapped CATALOGUE in the header. Grid of 14 numbered items like evidence records. Flicked down twice looking for denim — ended up at socks and a duffle bag. Scrolled back up.
**Got:** The jeans were NO. 03, near the top: "DENIM — BLUE WASH OG JEANS — £60.00 — AVAILABLE". Two flicks had carried him past ten products.
**Expected:** To spot denim mid-page; the numbered-card layout made every product look the same weight, so he overshot.
**Felt:** "Cards all look identical at speed — good thing there's only 14. Found them though, and 'AVAILABLE' stated right on the card is reassuring."
**Next:** went back (up), then tapped the jeans

### Step 3 — First look at the product page
**Did:** Tapped the BLUE WASH OG JEANS card. Scrolled the page top to bottom once, fast, to see what he's dealing with.
**Got:** Big flat back-shot of properly wide jeans, "PHOTO 1 OF 2", sizes XS–XL all tappable with XS pre-selected, IN STOCK, a SIZE GUIDE link right under the sizes, and four accordions: SPECIFICATION, ITEM DESCRIPTION, MEASUREMENTS, CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS.
**Expected:** Half expected a "size up, trust us" one-liner instead of any actual data. Seeing both a SIZE GUIDE link *and* a MEASUREMENTS accordion was more than he'd hoped for.
**Felt:** "Okay. There's a measurements section AND a size guide. If they're the same thing done properly, we're in business. Why is XS already selected though — who is that for?"
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Tapped SIZE GUIDE
**Did:** Tapped the SIZE GUIDE link under the size buttons.
**Got:** The page scrolled itself down and the MEASUREMENTS accordion opened on its own: a CM/IN toggle, the line "GARMENT LAID FLAT. ALL MEASUREMENTS IN CENTIMETRES.", and a five-row table — SIZE / WAIST / INSEAM / RISE / HEM for XS through XL.
**Expected:** A popup with a generic body-size chart, or worse, a jump to nothing.
**Felt:** "That's exactly what I wanted and it even told me HOW they measured — laid flat. That sentence is the difference between a chart I can use and a chart I ignore."
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — Hit the IN toggle and interrogated the numbers
**Did:** Tapped IN (thinks in inches), read every row. Then tapped CM and IN again to double-check the two tables against each other, because he's been burned by charts that don't survive arithmetic.
**Got:** M: waist 16.5in / inseam 31.5 / rise 11.4 / hem 8.3. L: 17.3 / 32.3 / 11.8 / 8.7. The conversions are honest — 42cm really is 16.5in, 44cm really is 17.3in, no sneaky rounding. Laid flat means double it: M = 33.0" waist, L = 34.6".
**Expected:** Either made-up numbers that don't step sensibly, or inches that don't match the centimetres.
**Felt:** "The maths is clean and the relationships make sense — rise and hem grow with size, the hem's wide enough to actually be baggy. One eyebrow raised: every column steps exactly +2cm per size, even the inseam. Real garments measured with a tape wobble; this is the factory spec sheet, not someone measuring my actual pair. Plausible spec, not proof. And M is EXACTLY my waist — zero room on rigid 14oz denim. Hm."
**Next:** hesitated — between M and L, exactly as feared

### Step 6 — Opened SPECIFICATION and ITEM DESCRIPTION, found the site arguing with itself
**Did:** Opened the two accordions above measurements looking for fit words and fabric.
**Got:** SPECIFICATION: "14oz denim · CUT: OG straight, mid rise · Made in Portugal". ITEM DESCRIPTION: "Heavyweight denim with a baggy, stacked fit" — followed by a stray "9-16 days delivery uk / 16-21 days international".
**Expected:** One consistent story about the fit, and no shipping talk in a product description.
**Felt:** "So is it 'straight' or 'baggy, stacked'? Those are different trousers. And the banner at the top says same-day dispatch while the description says 9 to 16 DAYS delivery? Which one am I ordering from? This is the sort of copy-paste sloppiness that makes me re-check everything else."
**Next:** hesitated — trust wobbled, went to read the returns terms properly

### Step 7 — Read CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS
**Did:** Opened the last accordion.
**Got:** A four-stage tracked timeline: same-day dispatch before 18:00 Mon–Sat, Royal Mail Tracked, UK 1–2 working days, and "14 days from delivery to return unworn goods with tags attached. Start a return by email: crooksldn@gmail.com."
**Expected:** A returns window, and ideally the word "exchange" — it's not there.
**Felt:** "14 days, fine. But nothing about swapping a size, nothing about who pays postage — and returns run through a gmail address? For a brand? The one question I actually have — 'if the L is a boat, what does the swap cost me' — isn't answered here."
**Next:** continued — went hunting in the footer

### Step 8 — Footer → REFUNDS page, found the actual answer
**Did:** Scrolled to the footer, tapped REFUNDS.
**Got:** A proper policy page: "Changed your mind or wrong size? 14 days to return or exchange any unworn item with tags on. Size swaps are free within the UK — however return postage is covered by the customer." Return address: "Oairo Uk Office, Bourne End Business Park…". Contact by email or DM, refunds in 5–7 days, statutory rights noted.
**Expected:** Boilerplate that dodged the size-swap question.
**Felt:** "There it is — free UK size swaps, I just pay the postage back. That's the sentence that lets me order between sizes. Why is that only here and not on the product page? Also… who is 'Oairo Uk Office'? Not exactly CROOKS, is it. Noted, not fatal."
**Next:** continued — back to the jeans

### Step 9 — Went looking for the jeans on a body
**Did:** Back on the product page, tapped the second thumbnail. First tap hit the wrong spot (thumbnails are small, 54px) and nothing changed; second tap landed.
**Got:** "PHOTO 2 OF 2" — model in the blue jeans, properly baggy, stacked hard on the trainers. No caption anywhere saying the model's height or which size they're wearing.
**Expected:** "Model is 6'1" and wears L" — the single most useful sentence on any denim page.
**Felt:** "Good — they ARE baggy, whatever the spec sheet says about 'straight'. But that photo tells me nothing without the model's size. Two photos total for £60 jeans is thin — no front flat shot, no fabric close-up."
**Next:** continued

### Step 10 — Picked M, stared at it, picked L
**Did:** Tapped M. Watched the sticky bar change to "£60.00 · M". Sat with it. Did the sums out loud one more time — M = 33.0" exactly his waist, rigid 14oz, cold-wash-only. Tapped L.
**Got:** "£60.00 · L" in the sticky bar. L = 34.6" waist (1.6" of room, belt sorts it), 32.3" inseam that will stack the way the photo shows.
**Expected:** To dither — and he did, but the flat measurements made it an actual decision rather than a coin toss.
**Felt:** "M is exactly my waist with zero ease on denim that won't give — that's the pair that goes back. L with a belt is how these are meant to sit anyway. And if I'm wrong, the swap's free. L."
**Next:** continued

### Step 11 — Added to bag and walked to the checkout door
**Did:** Tapped ADD TO BAG, saw "Added — 1 in bag · View bag", tapped through to the bag, then Check out.
**Got:** Bag page: BLUE WASH OG JEANS, Size L, £60.00, "£10.00 to free Tracked 24" nudge, duties and taxes included. Checkout opened — order summary £60.00, express pay options, delivery form pre-set to United Kingdom.
**Expected:** Exactly this.
**Felt:** "Smooth. The '£10 to free Tracked 24' almost got me looking at socks. The bag page suddenly looks like every other Shopify shop though — the evidence-terminal act drops right at the end."
**Next:** stopped at the payment step — that's the order he'd place

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached checkout with size L. The flat-measurement table (with method stated and honest cm/in conversion) turned a between-sizes gamble into a decision, and the refunds page's "size swaps are free within the UK" removed the last excuse. He'd have placed the order.
**Total time:** ~21 minutes — most of it inside the measurements table and the returns hunt.
**Worst moment:** Opening ITEM DESCRIPTION and reading "9-16 days delivery uk" directly under a banner promising same-day dispatch — "which is it, lads?" — plus the same accordion calling the cut "straight" while the description says "baggy". For someone already suspicious, the site contradicting itself is exactly the wrong smell.
**Best moment:** Tapping SIZE GUIDE and having the page scroll down and open a real flat-measurement table by itself — cm/in toggle that converts correctly, "GARMENT LAID FLAT" stated. Nine denim shops out of ten don't do this; it's the reason he bought.
**Would they come back?** Yes — conditionally. If the L arrives and matches the table, this becomes his jeans shop and he'll preach the measurement table to friends. If the numbers turn out to be spec-sheet fiction (that perfectly uniform +2cm grading in every column, including inseam, reads factory tech-pack rather than tape-on-garment), he's gone for good and the free swap won't save it.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** It wouldn't have changed the purchase, but it nearly derailed it: put the size-swap sentence ("free UK size swaps, you cover return postage") in the product page's SHIPPING & RETURNS accordion, and add "model is …, wears size …" under photo 2. He spent about eight of his twenty-one minutes finding answers the page could have handed him.
