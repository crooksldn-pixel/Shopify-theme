## Outcome

**Bought / didn't:** Got the £50 crewneck into the bag and stopped at the cart. I'd have finished it. I didn't buy the trousers I was more interested in, because the two trouser measurement tables I opened were identical and I no longer believe the numbers.

**Total time:** about 12 minutes, most of it pressing things rather than shopping.

**Worst moment:** Pressing `ON MODEL` and getting the same photo of one man in shorts on all twelve products, including the £60 jeans. Out loud: *"That's not the jeans. That's not any of them."*

**Best moment:** `SIZE GUIDE` → one press, table open, `IN` → every number converts **and the sentence above the table changes from "ALL MEASUREMENTS IN CENTIMETRES" to "ALL MEASUREMENTS IN INCHES"**. Nobody does that. Second best: light/dark and outline both survived every page change and a refresh, with no white flash.

**Would they come back?** Yes. This is the most interesting-looking shop I've clicked into in a long time and the switches are half the reason. But I'd come back for the crewneck and the socks, not the trousers.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Real measurements per product. Two different garments showing the same five rows is the only thing that actually cost a sale here — everything else was decoration I enjoyed poking at.

## Q3 — Does the `Outline` toggle earn its place?

**Straight answer: no, not as it currently stands. Ship it ON as a permanent treatment and delete the button.**

Not "it's a nice idea, keep it". I pressed it about a dozen times across four pages and here is what it actually did:

**1. In light mode it does literally nothing.** I photographed the same three cards with the switch on and with the switch off, in light mode. The two sets of images are indistinguishable — pixel for pixel, `11-18-light-outlineON-card1.png` and `11-19-light-outlineOFF-card1.png` are the same file, and so are the card3 and card10 pairs. Meanwhile the equivalent dark pair (`11-16-dark-outlineON-card3.png` vs `11-17-dark-outlineOFF-card3.png`) genuinely differs. So half the time a shopper presses this button, the button lights up and the page does not change. That is the worst possible outcome for a control — worse than not having it — because it teaches the shopper that the toggles on this site are decorative.

**2. In dark mode it helps exactly one product.** On the charcoal crewneck against the near-black card, the halo is doing real work: without it the garment loses its own edges (`11-17-dark-outlineOFF-card1.png` vs `11-16-dark-outlineON-card1.png`). On the blue jeans I had to look twice. On the socks I couldn't reliably tell without checking the button, because the sock photo already carries a white edge of its own — so the "off" state still shows an outline, which makes the whole control look broken.

**3. It isn't where the browsing happens.** The button only exists on the homepage register. `/collections/all`, `/collections/denim` and search have `FLAT / ON MODEL` and the category filters but **no `OUTLINE` button** — while still rendering the outline treatment. So the setting applies on pages that don't offer the switch, and anyone landing on a collection never learns it exists.

**Which would I ship?** **Outline ON, everywhere, in dark; no button.** It's the better-looking of the two states in the mode this shop actually lives in, and it rescues the one product that genuinely needs rescuing. Turning it off makes the ragged white fringes already baked into the sock and crewneck photos read as a bad cut-out rather than as a deliberate treatment — on is the state that makes those images look intentional.

**Does it make a product easier to judge and buy?** Only on the charcoal crewneck, and only in dark. That is one card in twelve, in one of two themes. That does not justify a permanent control sitting in the same strip as the category filters — the filters change *what I can buy*, and putting a cosmetic no-op next to them lowers the credibility of the whole row. And the switch that most needs to be there instead is a working `ON MODEL`.

**The honest version:** I pressed it, it did something good once, I pressed it in light mode and nothing happened at all, and after that I only kept pressing it because I was testing it. A shopper who isn't me presses it once, sees no change (because half of them will be in light mode, and most of the cards barely move in dark either), and concludes the site's buttons don't work. That is a real cost for a shop whose entire proposition is that it isn't a generic Shopify store — the controls have to be more trustworthy than a normal shop's, not less.

**If it must stay** (design law allows all of this: no radius, no shadow, no gradient, no new colour, no fabricated content): give it a treatment that is visible in *both* themes — a dark rim in light mode as well as the bone rim in dark — put the button on the collection templates too so it exists where people browse, and label it for what it does to the product rather than `OUTLINE`. But I'd still rather you spent that slot on making `ON MODEL` show the right garment.

## Side note — the other two toggles, ranked

- **`LIGHT MODE` / `DARK MODE`: keep, unchanged.** Best-behaved control here. Label states the destination not the current state, survives every navigation and a refresh, repaints header, carriage bar, product page, accordions and cart together, and never flashes the wrong colour on load. Works on every page including the cart.
- **`FLAT` / `ON MODEL`: currently harmful, not merely unfinished.** All twelve products return the identical placeholder photo of a man in a black tee and grey shorts, under headings that say `SWEATS`, `DENIM`, `T-SHIRT` and `ACCESSORIES` and prices from £6 to £60 (`11-23-onmodel-light-outlineON.png`). Until at least most products have a real model shot, a shopper pressing this is shown the wrong product twelve times. Unlike the outline switch, this one doesn't need deleting — it needs the photographs. Until they exist I'd hide the toggle rather than let it answer a direct question with a wrong picture.
- **`CM` / `IN`: keep, and copy its manners elsewhere.** Converts correctly (`76.2cm → 30in`, `73.7cm → 29in`, `45.7cm → 18in`), and rewrites the prose line above the table from "ALL MEASUREMENTS IN CENTIMETRES" to "ALL MEASUREMENTS IN INCHES" instead of leaving it contradicting the numbers. Only gripe: it's the one setting that doesn't follow you to the next product, while dark/light and outline both do.
