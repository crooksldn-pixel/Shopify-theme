# 11 — Marcus, 27, product designer who pokes every switch before he trusts a site
**Device:** iPhone 390×844 first, then a 1440×900 laptop pass · **Goal:** heard the site "looks like a police terminal", came to see if the gimmick holds up — and maybe pick up jeans if it does · **Mood:** Tuesday 10pm, drink in hand, ready to be unimpressed

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage and got jumped by a game
**Did:** Opened the site on my phone. Before I saw a single product, a full-screen "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED — CRACK THE CUFFS — ESCAPE WITH A PRIVATE DISCOUNT" thing took over, with a faint BEGIN button and a wall of small print about codes expiring in 60 minutes.
**Got:** A popup styled exactly like the rest of the site, sitting on top of a cookie banner I could see lurking underneath.
**Expected:** A boring 10%-off email popup.
**Felt:** Honestly? Instantly on brand. Most popups I close on reflex; this one I actually wanted to press. That's rare.
**Next:** continued — pressed BEGIN, obviously

### Step 2 — Played Crack the Cuffs, hit the toll booth
**Did:** Pressed BEGIN. Three spinning digit reels appeared — "LOCK ACTIVE — TAP TO STOP", 7 seconds on the clock, 0/3 LOCKED. Tapped each reel to freeze the digits. Locked all three in time.
**Got:** Not a code. "SECURE YOUR LINE — STEP 1 OF 2": a +44 phone field and a tick box agreeing CROOKSLDN can text me drops and offers.
**Expected:** I cracked the cuffs — I expected the discount code, right there.
**Felt:** Ah, there it is. The game was the bait; the prize is my phone number and SMS marketing consent. Clever, and the reflex game is genuinely fun, but I "won" and got a form. Felt like the cuffs went on ME. Not giving a store my number at 10pm for an unnamed discount.
**Next:** closed the popup — slightly less charmed than 30 seconds earlier

### Step 3 — Dealt with the cookie banner
**Did:** With the popup gone, the cookie banner was still there — a big dark slab across the bottom third of the phone. Read the first line, tapped Decline.
**Got:** Gone in one tap, never came back.
**Expected:** Exactly this.
**Felt:** Fine. It's chunky on a phone but it answered to one tap and stayed dismissed.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Found the control panel above the catalogue
**Did:** Scrolled past the CROOKSLDN hero into the CATALOGUE. Above the products: a row of chips — FLAT / ON MODEL, then OUTLINE, then ALL / T-SHIRT / DENIM / SWEATS / ACCESSORIES. FLAT and OUTLINE already lit purple.
**Got:** An actual bank of display switches. My kind of page.
**Expected:** Filters, maybe. Not a view mode AND an image-treatment toggle.
**Felt:** This is a lot of controls for a shop with 14 items — but the default look is great: flat garment shots with a cream sticker-outline, like evidence photos cut out and pinned to a board.
**Next:** continued — started flipping switches

### Step 5 — Tapped ON MODEL
**Did:** Tapped ON MODEL and scrolled the whole catalogue top to bottom.
**Got:** Every single one of the 14 products turned into the SAME photo: one guy in a black tee and grey shorts standing on a little glass pedestal. The GREY WASH OG JEANS card — him, in shorts, not jeans. The BLUE WASH JORTS — same guy, same shorts. The socks — same guy. Fourteen identical clones, none of them wearing the thing the card is selling.
**Expected:** The clothes on a person, so I could judge the fit of what I'm actually buying.
**Felt:** I laughed out loud, and not kindly. This toggle promises the one thing I actually wanted — the jeans on a body — and delivers a wall of the same man 14 times. It's worse than useless: the jeans card now doesn't even show jeans. (11-09, 11-30)
**Next:** tapped straight back to FLAT

### Step 6 — Tapped OUTLINE off and on (dark mode)
**Did:** Zoomed in on the crewneck card, tapped OUTLINE off, looked, tapped it back on.
**Got:** Off: the cutout halo disappears and the garments sink into the black background — flatter, moodier. On: the cream edge comes back and the products pop like stickers.
**Expected:** Honestly no idea — the word "OUTLINE" told me nothing until I pressed it.
**Felt:** OK, it does a real thing, and the on-state is clearly better — which is presumably why it's the default. But would I, shopping, ever have wanted the off-state? No. It's a designer's A/B test left on the shelf for customers. (11-12, 11-13)
**Next:** continued — left it on, went hunting for the light switch

### Step 7 — Flipped the site to LIGHT MODE
**Did:** Tapped "LIGHT MODE" in the header — I'd clocked it on arrival; the label tells you what it does, which I appreciate.
**Got:** The whole terminal flipped to an off-white, scanned-paper look with faint scanlines. Everything readable, catalogue intact, my scroll position kept.
**Expected:** A half-baked inverted theme, because dark-first sites usually botch light.
**Felt:** Genuinely impressed. It doesn't look like "dark mode inverted", it looks like the same evidence file photocopied. This toggle earns its spot. (11-14, 11-15)
**Next:** continued — obviously had to try OUTLINE in light

### Step 8 — Tapped OUTLINE in light mode. Nothing happened.
**Did:** Scrolled back to the chips — OUTLINE still lit purple. Tapped it.
**Got:** The chip un-lit. The images did not change one pixel. Tapped my eyes across crewneck, shorts, jeans — identical before and after. The outline treatment apparently doesn't exist in light mode at all, but the button still sits there taking presses.
**Expected:** Some visible change, in either direction.
**Felt:** Dead switch. In a site this deliberate, a button that visibly toggles while doing nothing feels like a broken prop on a film set. I shrugged and left it — didn't press it back.
**Next:** continued — back to dark mode

### Step 9 — Back to DARK MODE, and the outlines were gone
**Did:** Tapped DARK MODE in the header, scrolled to the catalogue.
**Got:** The evidence-board halos were missing. The catalogue looked flatter and I couldn't say why for a good few seconds — until I remembered the "dead" tap in light mode. That press did count; it just had no effect I could see when I made it.
**Expected:** Dark mode exactly as I'd left it.
**Felt:** This is the sneaky one. A normal person doesn't debug this — they just feel the site got slightly worse and never know they did it themselves, with a button that looked broken when they pressed it. Pressed OUTLINE again and the halos came back. (11-17)
**Next:** continued — enough catalogue toggles, on to a product

### Step 10 — Opened the blue jeans, went straight for MEASUREMENTS
**Did:** Opened BLUE WASH OG JEANS (£60). Four folded sections: SPECIFICATION, ITEM DESCRIPTION, MEASUREMENTS, CHAIN OF CUSTODY. Opened MEASUREMENTS.
**Got:** A proper table — waist/inseam/rise/hem for XS–XL, "GARMENT LAID FLAT. ALL MEASUREMENTS IN CENTIMETRES." with CM / IN buttons above it, CM active.
**Expected:** A size-chart PDF or a modal, the usual.
**Felt:** This is how it should be done. Laid-flat method stated, real numbers, right there in the page. (11-20)
**Next:** continued — pressed IN, because of course I did

### Step 11 — Flipped CM to IN and checked their maths
**Did:** Tapped IN. Then, because I'm that person, checked a value: 38cm waist became 15in.
**Got:** The whole table converted, the caption changed to "ALL MEASUREMENTS IN INCHES", and 38 ÷ 2.54 is indeed 15. Tapped back to CM.
**Expected:** It to work — and it did.
**Felt:** Quiet, correct, useful. The only control today that did exactly what it said, both directions, with honest labelling. This is what the other toggles want to be when they grow up. (11-21)
**Next:** continued

### Step 12 — Tried the crewneck; the measurements weren't there at all
**Did:** Thought about the £50 crewneck too, opened its page, went looking for the same table.
**Got:** SPECIFICATION, ITEM DESCRIPTION, CHAIN OF CUSTODY — no MEASUREMENTS section, no SIZE GUIDE link. Also noticed on the baggies (which do have a table) that my inches choice hadn't stuck — back to centimetres.
**Expected:** Every garment to have the table the jeans just showed off.
**Felt:** So the best feature on the site is only on some products. For a sweatshirt I'd size down or up on, that's exactly where I wanted it. The cm/inch choice forgetting itself between pages is a small thing, but I noticed. (11-22)
**Next:** continued — moved to the laptop to finish properly

### Step 13 — Desktop pass: same switches, bigger stage
**Did:** Opened the site on my laptop. Got the Crack the Cuffs popup again (new device, fair enough — closed it, declined cookies again). Ran the same circuit: OUTLINE off/on, ON MODEL, LIGHT/DARK.
**Got:** Same behaviour everywhere. ON MODEL at four columns is even funnier — four identical men on pedestals shoulder to shoulder, labelled crewneck, shorts, jeans, jorts. OUTLINE does its subtle halo thing. LIGHT mode still handsome. Hovering cards does nothing fancy — fine.
**Expected:** Consistency, and got it — bugs included.
**Felt:** The site is the same site at any size, which I respect. But nobody should ship ON MODEL looking like this. (11-30, 11-31)
**Next:** continued — right, actually buying the jeans now

### Step 14 — Bought the jeans (well — got to the till)
**Did:** Back to BLUE WASH OG JEANS. Picked M — the address bar updated so the size is in the link, which I clocked approvingly. IN STOCK, "Ordered now — leaves tomorrow" (at 10pm, that's honest). ADD TO BAG → bag went [1] → cart: one line, M, £60, free Tracked 48 shipping already earned. Hit Check out.
**Got:** Straight onto a clean checkout — jeans, size M, £60, express-pay buttons at the top. Stopped there; card stays in the wallet tonight.
**Expected:** No surprises, and there were none.
**Felt:** The buying spine is tight. Every step told the truth. After an evening of finding the loose switches, the part that takes money is the part that works flawlessly — priorities in the right order. (11-36)
**Next:** continued to checkout — done

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached checkout with the £60 BLUE WASH OG JEANS in M. The measurements table (and its cm/inch toggle actually converting correctly) is what closed the sale; the fit numbers were right there and they checked out.
**Total time:** ~35 minutes (a normal shopper does this site in 10; I flipped every switch twice on two devices).
**Worst moment:** ON MODEL. "Why is the same bloke in shorts standing on every single card — he's not even WEARING the jeans." A view mode that replaces every product with one identical placeholder man is worse than no view mode.
**Best moment:** Tie: light mode looking like a photocopied case file rather than a lazy inversion, and 38cm flipping to a mathematically correct 15in. Honourable mention to the cuffs game right up until it asked for my phone number.
**Would they come back?** Yes — the brand won me over and checkout was clean. But I now know two of its switches are set dressing, and I'd tell friends "ignore ON MODEL".
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Nothing would have stopped the purchase — but if the crewneck had a measurements table like the jeans, this would have been a two-item order.

### Control verdicts (the O3 evidence)
- **LIGHT/DARK toggle — KEEP.** Findable, honestly labelled ("LIGHT MODE" tells you what it does, not what you're on... actually it tells you what you'll get, which is the right way round), flips every page, keeps your place, and the light theme is designed rather than inverted. A real feature.
- **CM/IN toggle — KEEP.** The best control on the site: instant, correct (38cm→15in verified by an actual pedant), caption updates to match. Two wishes: put a measurements table on every garment (the crewneck has none, so its shoppers never even see this control), and remember the choice across pages.
- **FLAT/ON MODEL toggle — HIDE until the photos exist.** The idea is right — flat shot vs on-body is a genuinely useful choice. The execution shows one identical placeholder man on all 14 cards, wearing none of the products; the jeans card stops showing jeans. As shipped it actively misinforms. Turn it off until every product (or at least each category) has a real on-model photo.
- **OUTLINE toggle — HIDE the control, keep the treatment.** Verdict on O3: the outline LOOK earns its place — it's the site's signature evidence-board aesthetic and it's rightly on by default. The TOGGLE does not. No shopper wants the off-state; the label tells you nothing before you press it; in light mode it's a fully dead button (visibly toggles, changes nothing); and a press made in light mode silently strips the halos from dark mode, so the site quietly degrades and the shopper never knows why. Bake the outline in per theme (on in dark, off in light) and retire the button. I'm the target audience for fiddly switches and even I only pressed it to find out what it was — then it bit me.
