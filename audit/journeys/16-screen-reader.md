# 16 — Dele, blind since his teens, buys all his clothes online with a screen reader

**Device:** desktop 1440×900, screen reader + keyboard, no mouse — the page exists for me only as what gets read out · **Goal:** the £60 blue jeans, in my size, in the bag, without having to ring anyone for help · **Mood:** braced. Streetwear sites are usually the worst offenders — all pictures, buttons called "button", and no way to tell what I've picked.

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage and waited to hear where I was
**Did:** opened the site cold and listened.
**Got:** a dialog announced as **"COOKIE CONSENT"** — *"We and our partners, including Shopify, use cookies and other technologies to personalize your experience, show you ads, and perform analytics, and we will not use cookies or other technologies for these purposes unless you accept them. Learn more in our Privacy Policy"* — with **"Manage preferences"**, **"Accept"** and **"Decline"**. It did not steal my focus, but it is the first thing in the page, so the very first thing I reach is *"Privacy Policy, link"* and only then **"Skip to content"**.
**Expected:** to hear the shop's name first.
**Felt:** a cookie box before the shop's name is normal these days, and at least the three buttons are named properly rather than being "button, button, button". Nothing else jumped out at me — I sat there several seconds waiting for the pop-up everyone warns me about and none arrived.
**Next:** continued — pressed **"Accept"**.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-home.png, audit/screens/16-home-after-consent.png

### Step 2 — Read the top of the page
**Did:** went through the landmarks and headings.
**Got:** a banner, a main region, a footer, one heading level 1 — **"CROOKSLDN"** — and level 2s in order: **"CATALOGUE"**, **"EVERY ORDER SHIPS LIKE THIS"**, **"REGISTER AS INFORMANT"**, then the footer's **"SHOP" / "INFORMATION" / "CONTACT" / "GAME"**. Header controls read as **"CATALOGUE, link"**, **"SEARCH, link"**, **"BAG [0], link"**, **"LIGHT MODE, toggle button, not pressed"**, **"MENU, button, collapsed"**. There is a line above it all reading *"FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH"*.
**Expected:** to have to hunt for the headings. Most shops give me one enormous unlabelled blob.
**Felt:** this is a properly built page. I know where the bag is, I know how many things are in it, and the shipping rule was read to me without my having to go looking. That never happens.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 3 — Went down the catalogue list
**Did:** listened to the product list.
**Got:** a real list of 12 items. Each one reads as a single long link, e.g. *"NO. 03 DENIM CROOKSLDN blue wash heavyweight denim jeans with baggy fit and white handcuff embroidery on back pockets BLUE WASH OG JEANS £60.00 AVAILABLE, link"*. Another: *"NO. 09 SWEATS V2 BAGGIES V2 BAGGIES £60.00 2 OF 5 SIZES LEFT, link"*.
**Expected:** "Product image, link" twelve times. That's the usual.
**Felt:** the price and whether it's in stock are *inside the link* — I know what a thing costs before I open it, which decides whether I bother. The downside is the long ones read the photo description and then the product name, so I hear the item twice; and some cards do it and some don't. Wordy, but I'd rather too much than nothing.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 4 — Poked at the buttons above the list
**Did:** tabbed across the row of controls before the products.
**Got:** three labelled groups — **"Choose how products are shown"** containing **"FLAT, pressed"** and **"ON MODEL, not pressed"**; **"Product image outline"** containing **"OUTLINE, pressed"**; and **"Filter exhibits"** containing **"ALL, pressed"**, **"T-SHIRT"**, **"DENIM"**, **"SWEATS"**, **"ACCESSORIES"**.
**Expected:** unlabelled toggles I'd have to guess at.
**Felt:** I can tell what each set of buttons is *for* and which one is currently on. "FLAT" and "ON MODEL" mean nothing to me personally — they're both pictures — but the fact they're grouped and named means I can leave them alone knowing I'm not missing information.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 5 — Opened the menu
**Did:** pressed Enter on **"MENU, button, collapsed"**.
**Got:** it became **"expanded"**, focus moved to a **"CLOSE"** button, the dialog announced itself as **"Main menu, dialog"**, and the rest of the page went silent — I could no longer read past the menu, which is exactly right.
**Expected:** to be dumped somewhere random, or to still be able to wander out into the page behind.
**Felt:** textbook. Someone thought about this.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-drawer.png

### Step 6 — Tried to tab through the menu — and couldn't
**Did:** pressed Tab to walk the menu items.
**Got:** nothing. Focus stayed on **"CLOSE"**. I pressed it five times: **CLOSE, CLOSE, CLOSE, CLOSE, CLOSE**. Shift+Tab threw me to **"BAG [0]"** instead. I then put myself on **"SHOP"** by hand and pressed Tab twice: still **"SHOP"**, still **"SHOP"**. There are 16 things in that menu and Tab reaches two of them.
**Expected:** Tab to walk me down SHOP → ALL → NEW → TEES → DENIM and round again.
**Felt:** that sinking "here we go" feeling. The menu is *there* — I could read every line of it — but the key I actually use to move between links does nothing. If I were less patient I'd have assumed the menu was broken and closed it.
**Next:** hesitated.

### Step 7 — Read the menu line by line instead
**Did:** stopped tabbing and read the dialog with the reading cursor.
**Got:** all of it, cleanly: **SHOP**, then a nested list — **ALL, NEW, TEES, DENIM, SWEATS, TRACKSUITS, ACCESSORIES** — then **TRACKING, QUESTIONS, TERMS, CONTACT**, and a link read out as *"PLAY CASE:001 NOW CASE 001: THE GETAWAY. Animated map: a thief moves through an alley grid collecting coins while a patrolling officer crosses below."* Escape closed it and put me back on **"MENU"**.
**Expected:** the game thing to be an unlabelled canvas I'd never know existed.
**Felt:** somebody wrote a description of a moving picture for me. I'm not going to play it, but I know what it is and I wasn't just left with silence. That's a small kindness and I noticed it.
**Next:** continued — pressed Enter on **DENIM**.

### Step 8 — Landed on the denim page
**Did:** checked where I'd arrived.
**Got:** heading level 1 **"DENIM"**, and four links: *"NO. 01 DENIM GREY WASH JORTS £50.00 AVAILABLE"*, *"NO. 02 DENIM BLUE WASH JORTS £50.00 AVAILABLE"*, *"NO. 03 DENIM BLUE WASH OG JEANS £60.00 AVAILABLE"*, *"NO. 04 DENIM GREY WASH OG JEANS £60.00 AVAILABLE"*.
**Expected:** to have to open all four to find out which was £60.
**Felt:** four items, four prices, read to me in one pass. Straight to the one I wanted.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-catalogue.png

### Step 9 — Opened the jeans and got talked at
**Did:** pressed Enter on **BLUE WASH OG JEANS**.
**Got:** heading level 1 **"BLUE WASH OG JEANS"**, then *"£60.00"*, *"PRODUCT 03 / 12"*, *"DENIM"*, a labelled group **"Evidence photographs"** with **"Photo 1 of 2"** and **"Photo 2 of 2"** buttons. Then, about a second after everything had gone quiet, the page spoke on its own: **"Select Size"** — it had already said *"Select a size"* when the page loaded, and the script rewrote it to *"Select Size"*, so I got told twice in slightly different words for no reason.
**Expected:** silence until I did something.
**Felt:** the page talking at me unprompted a second after I arrive makes me stop and check I haven't pressed something. It's harmless, but it's a small false alarm on every single product page.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-product.png

### Step 10 — Found the sizes
**Did:** went to the size controls.
**Got:** a group called **"Size"**, containing **"Size XS"**, **"Size S"**, **"Size M"**, **"Size L"**, **"Size XL"** — each a toggle button reading *"not pressed"*. The buy button at this point was **"SELECT A SIZE, button, unavailable"**.
**Expected:** five buttons called "XS", "S", "M" with no clue what they were, which is the standard.
**Felt:** they're called *"Size M"*, not *"M"*. That one word is the difference between guessing and knowing. And the buy button telling me it's unavailable *and why* — "SELECT A SIZE" — means I'm never left pressing a dead button wondering what I did wrong.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 11 — Chose M
**Did:** pressed Enter on **"Size M"**.
**Got:** it read back **"Size M, pressed"**, and the line under the sizes changed and was announced: **"IN STOCK"**. The buy button became **"ADD TO BAG"**. Focus stayed on the size button where I'd left it.
**Expected:** to have to go hunting to confirm anything had happened.
**Felt:** this is the bit almost everyone gets wrong and they got it right. I know which size I've picked, because the button says so; and I was *told* it's in stock rather than having to find out at checkout. Nothing moved under me.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-size-chosen.png, audit/screens/16-buy-spine.png

### Step 12 — Asked myself whether any size was running out, and got no answer
**Did:** went back over all five size buttons listening for a stock hint.
**Got:** **"Size XS"**, **"Size S"**, **"Size M"**, **"Size L"**, **"Size XL"** — identical, all five. But XL is flagged as low stock in the page and carries a little visual marker that says so to anyone who can see it. Nothing about that reaches me.
**Expected:** if the shop knows a size is nearly gone, to be told, the same as everyone else.
**Felt:** this is the one place the shop knows something and only tells the sighted half. It wouldn't have changed what I bought today — I want M — but if M were the nearly-gone one I'd want to hear it, because that's the difference between buying now and buying next week.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 13 — Opened the details sections
**Did:** worked down the four headings and pressed Enter on each.
**Got:** four level-2 headings each holding a button: **"SPECIFICATION, collapsed"**, **"ITEM DESCRIPTION, collapsed"**, **"MEASUREMENTS, collapsed"**, **"CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS, collapsed"**. Pressing Enter on MEASUREMENTS read back **"expanded"** and opened it. Opening a second one did *not* silently close the first — I could have all four open.
**Expected:** four "+" buttons that announce nothing, or accordions that close behind my back.
**Felt:** I always know whether a section is open or shut, because it tells me. And nothing closed itself when I opened the next one, which is the thing that usually makes me lose my place.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-accordion.png, audit/screens/16-accordion-2.png

### Step 14 — Read the measurements
**Did:** went into the MEASUREMENTS panel.
**Got:** a group **"Measurement units"** with **"CM, pressed"** and **"IN, not pressed"**, then a region called **"Measurements table"** — an actual table, with the caption *"TRUE TO SIZE — WAIST, CHEST AND LEG MEASUREMENTS ARE TAKEN AROUND THE GARMENT. ALL MEASUREMENTS IN CENTIMETRES."*, column headers *SIZE / WAIST / INSEAM / LEG OPENING*, and each row headed by its size: *"M, 86.4cm, 77.5cm, 50.8cm"*.
**Expected:** a size chart as a picture, or a PDF, i.e. nothing.
**Felt:** best moment of the whole visit. I can jump to the M row and hear waist, inseam and leg opening with the column names attached, and it tells me *how* they measured. Most shops give me a JPEG and a shrug. And the cm/inch switch tells me which one is on.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 15 — Added it to the bag, and lost my place
**Did:** tabbed from the sizes — **"SIZE GUIDE, button"**, then **"ADD TO BAG, button"** — and pressed Enter.
**Got:** the confirmation *"Added — 1 in bag"* with a **"View bag"** link appeared in a live area, and the header link changed from **"BAG [0]"** to **"BAG [1]"**. But my focus was thrown off the button onto nothing at all — no button, no link, just the page. The ADD TO BAG button's own wording never changed.
**Expected:** to be left standing on the button I pressed, hearing "added".
**Felt:** the good news is I *was* told, in plain words, and the bag count backs it up if I go and check. The bad news is that the confirmation lives in a strip that's empty and hidden right up until the moment it fills — that's the arrangement my screen reader most often sails straight past — and pressing Enter threw me off the page furniture entirely. For a second I genuinely did not know whether I'd bought a pair of jeans or nothing at all.
**Next:** hesitated.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-added-confirm.png, audit/screens/16-added.png

### Step 16 — Tabbed forward to find the confirmation I'd half-heard
**Did:** pressed Tab to get my bearings back.
**Got:** stop 1 was Shopify's own **Buy with Shop** button — a stop I couldn't pin a name on at first; stop 2 **"More payment options, link"**; stop 3 **"View bag, link"**.
**Expected:** to land straight on "View bag".
**Felt:** three presses in the dark before I hit something that proved the jeans were in the bag. Not a disaster, but it's three presses of not knowing.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 17 — Went back for the pair I'd heard was nearly gone
**Did:** the homepage had said *"V2 BAGGIES £60.00 2 OF 5 SIZES LEFT"*, so I went and had a look, and pressed Enter on **"Size M"**.
**Got:** M, L and XL all read as **"Size M, button, unavailable"** — still reachable, still readable, just marked unavailable. Choosing M announced **"SIZE M IS SOLD OUT"**, the buy button became **"SOLD OUT"**, and a form appeared: an email box named **"TELL ME WHEN THIS SIZE IS BACK"** and a **"NOTIFY ME"** button.
**Expected:** dead buttons I can't reach, or worse, buttons that let me "buy" a size that doesn't exist.
**Felt:** I could still get at the sold-out sizes to find out they were sold out, which is what I want — being skipped over entirely tells me nothing. The only word missing is *why* the button is unavailable; "unavailable" is what I hear, "sold out" is what it means, and I only learned that after pressing it. The email box having a proper name instead of just being an empty field is the sort of thing that decides whether I bother signing up.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-soldout.png

### Step 18 — Went to the bag
**Did:** activated **"BAG [1]"**.
**Got:** heading level 1 **"Cart 1"**, a table with column headers *"Product image / Product information / Quantity / Product total"*, and my row reading *"BLUE WASH OG JEANS Size: M Price £60.00"*. Controls: **"Decrease quantity, button, unavailable"**, **"Quantity, spin button, 1"**, **"Increase quantity, button"**, **"Remove BLUE WASH OG JEANS - M, button"**, **"Check out, button"**. Above it, a labelled **"Progress toward free carriage"** bar and the line *"£10.00 to free Tracked 24"*.
**Expected:** a table of unlabelled cells and a bin icon called "button".
**Felt:** the remove button says *which thing* it removes, with the size. That is the single most useful sentence on the page, because on most sites "Remove, button" appearing four times is how I end up deleting the wrong item.
**Next:** continued.
**Evidence:** audit/screens/16-cart.png

### Step 19 — Changed my mind and made it two
**Did:** pressed **"Increase quantity"**.
**Got:** the page told me, without my asking: **"Estimated total £120.00 GBP"**, then **"Free Tracked 24 — unlocked"**. Focus stayed on the button. But the header still read **"BAG [1]"** while the cart said 2.
**Expected:** silence, and a hunt around the page to find out what it now cost.
**Felt:** being told the new total *and* that I'd crossed into the better postage, without going looking, is the best thing this site does. Then I tabbed up to the bag link and it told me I had one item, which flatly contradicts the two it had just charged me for. Small, but it's the sort of thing that makes me go round the page a second time to check I haven't broken something.
**Next:** continued.

### Step 20 — Checked there was somewhere to put a discount code, then stopped
**Did:** opened **"Discount, collapsed"**.
**Got:** **"expanded"**, then a text box named **"Apply a discount code"** and an **"Apply"** button. Under the total: *"Duties and taxes included. Shipping is calculated at checkout."*
**Expected:** an unnamed box next to an unnamed button, which is the usual ending to a cart.
**Felt:** named, both of them. I'd have got a code in there without help. Stopped at **"Check out"** — I'd proved I could get this far on my own, which was the whole point.
**Next:** continued to the checkout button and stopped there.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** got as far as **"Check out"** with the right jeans in the right size, unaided, and stopped there deliberately. On any normal day this is a purchase — I'd have finished it.
**Total time:** about 14 minutes, most of it spent reading rather than fighting.
**Worst moment:** pressing Enter on ADD TO BAG and being thrown off the page onto nothing, with the confirmation sitting in a strip that had been empty and hidden a second earlier. Out loud: *"…did that work? Where am I?"* Second worst: pressing Tab five times inside the menu and going nowhere at all.
**Best moment:** the measurements. A real table, with the size on each row and the column names attached, and a caption telling me they measured the garment laid flat. I have never been able to check a leg opening on a streetwear site before without asking someone.
**Would they come back?** Yes, and I'd tell people. The things this shop gets right — sizes called "Size M", stock said out loud, a remove button that names what it removes, a total that reads itself out when it changes — are things I normally have to do without.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** after ADD TO BAG, leave me standing on something and say what happened. Move focus to the "View bag" link or back to the button itself. As it is, the one moment where I most need to know what just happened is the one moment the page drops me.

## Checking the accessibility claim in `SPEC.md` §9.10 — it holds

I was asked not to take this on trust, so I counted, on every page I actually used, using the names the browser itself computes:

| Page | Controls the screen reader can reach | Without a name |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage (cookie banner still up) | 46 | **0** |
| Product, size chosen, sections open | 44 | **0** |
| Product with sold-out sizes + notify form | 40 | **0** |
| Cart, with the discount box opened | 38 | **0** |

- **"0 of 35 interactive controls without an accessible name" — true**, and it is now more than 35 controls. I did not meet one thing announced as bare "button" anywhere on the route. The only nameless tab stop I hit is Shopify's own **Buy with Shop** wallet button in the payment block, which the theme does not own.
- **"Exactly one h1 per page" — true** on all four pages I visited: *"CROOKSLDN"*, *"DENIM"*, *"BLUE WASH OG JEANS"*, *"Cart 1"*. `header` / `nav` / `main` / `footer` landmarks are all present, plus a working **"Skip to content"**.
- **"`aria-label` + `aria-pressed` on size buttons" — true.** They read as *"Size XS"* … *"Size XL"*, they report *pressed* / *not pressed*, and sold-out ones stay reachable and announce as unavailable rather than being skipped.

That much is genuinely protect-list material and I would not touch it. What is *not* covered by the claim, and is what actually cost me something:

1. **Tab does not move inside the open menu.** Focus pins to *CLOSE*; Shift+Tab jumps to *BAG*; the 14 links between them are readable but not tabbable. (The dialog itself is right: named "Main menu", modal, the page behind goes quiet, Escape returns me to MENU.)
2. **ADD TO BAG throws focus onto nothing**, and the *"Added — 1 in bag"* confirmation sits in a live strip that is empty and hidden until the instant it fills — the arrangement screen readers most reliably miss. The words are correct; the delivery is the weak link.
3. **The low-stock marker on a size is visual only.** XL on the blue jeans is flagged as running low and reads exactly the same as every other size.
4. **A sold-out size announces "unavailable", never "sold out"** — the reason only arrives after you press it.
5. **The header bag count and the cart disagree** after a quantity change (*"BAG [1]"* vs a cart of 2 and *"Estimated total £120.00 GBP"*).
6. **Every product page speaks unprompted about a second after loading**, changing *"Select a size"* to *"Select Size"* — same instruction, different words, for no gain.
