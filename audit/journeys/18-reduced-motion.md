# 18 — Priya, has "reduce motion" switched on at the phone level because animation makes her ill

**Device:** mobile 390×844, ordinary 4G, iOS reduce-motion on · **Goal:** buy a plain-ish tee, £25-ish, without spending the rest of the evening lying down · **Mood:** cautious. Shopping on a phone is a calculated risk; a site that slides, drifts, auto-plays or types at me costs me an afternoon.

---

### Step 1 — Opened the site cold and braced for it

**Did:** Tapped through to the homepage and did the thing I always do — held the phone still and waited a second before looking properly, in case something swept in.
**Got:** Black screen, one grey line across the very top: `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH`. A handcuffs logo. A row of words: `CATALOGUE  SEARCH  BAG [0]  LIGHT MODE  MENU`. Then `> 12 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE`, `CROOKSLDN`, `OWN THE STREETS™`, and a purple `CATALOGUE` button. Then a `COOKIE CONSENT` panel took the bottom ~40%. **Nothing moved.** No fade-up, no cards sliding in from the side, no letters typing themselves out. (`audit/screens/18-r-01-arrive.png`)
**Expected:** Something to swoop. On a site this styled, something always swoops.
**Felt:** Genuine relief, and slight suspicion — the black-terminal look is exactly the kind of design that usually comes with a typewriter effect. It just sat there and let me read it. That is the whole ballgame for me and it took two seconds to earn.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Declined the cookies

**Did:** Tapped `Decline`, without reading it.
**Got:** Panel gone. Two real buttons, `Accept` and `Decline`, both the same size.
**Expected:** To have to hunt for the refuse option.
**Felt:** Fine. It's Shopify's stock wording — "We and our partners, including Shopify, use cookies and other technologies to personalize your experience, show you ads, and perform analytics…" — which reads like a different company wrote it, but it went away in one tap and it didn't animate on its way out.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Three seconds later the whole screen was taken over

**Did:** Nothing. I was reading the hero.
**Got:** A full-screen black takeover with an `×` in the top-left: **`CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY`** / "Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited." / `RUN IT` / `NOT NOW` / "One code per player. Code expires 20 minutes after you win." (`audit/screens/18-r-02-clear.png`)
**Expected:** An email-for-10%-off box. That's the normal version of this.
**Felt:** Two feelings at once. First: it arrived without animating, which I noticed and appreciated. Second: my stomach dropped at the word **game**. Ten per cent off a £25 tee is £2.50 — small, but it's the difference between paying £25 and feeling like I got the same deal everyone else got.
**Next:** hesitated

### Step 4 — Tapped `RUN IT` anyway, because I wanted the discount

**Did:** Tapped `RUN IT`. I knew what I was risking. I do this maybe once a month and regret it about half the time.
**Got:** The panel reloaded into its own version of the same screen — `CRACK THE CUFFS.` / "10% off your first order if you do. Three tumblers. Tap each one at the right moment." / another `RUN IT` / `NOT NOW` / "(this drop closes 15.09)". So I had to tap `RUN IT` twice. The panel only fills the bottom third of the screen; the top two-thirds is empty black. (`audit/screens/18-21-game-1.png`)
**Expected:** The game, first tap.
**Felt:** Mildly annoyed at tapping the same button twice for the same thing. Also: "at the right moment" is now on screen in plain words, so I knew exactly what was coming.
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — The game, and the moment this site stopped being for me

**Did:** Tapped the second `RUN IT` and looked at what came up.
**Got:** `TAP TO STOP`, three boxes side by side, each with a **large digit spinning continuously with a motion-blur smear on it** — you can see the ghost of the previous number behind the current one. Under them: `0/3 LOCKED`, and "Tap each tumbler to lock it. **No timer — take your time.**" I watched for about five seconds and the digits never stopped or slowed. (`audit/screens/18-52-play-2.png`)
**Expected:** Honestly? That having the system setting on might make it a slower or static version. It's a phone-level setting; the rest of this site had already obeyed it.
**Felt:** This is the moment. Three smeared reels flickering at me is precisely the thing I have the setting turned on to avoid, and it's the only screen on this whole site that does it. "No timer — take your time" is a kind sentence written by someone who thinks the pressure is the problem. The pressure isn't the problem. **The spinning is the problem.** I said out loud, "I can't look at that." Closed it with the ×, and sat for a second with that specific tight feeling behind my eyes that means I've overdone it.
**Next:** continued, but annoyed — and now the 10% is off the table permanently

### Step 6 — Watched the top line for a full minute to see if it would start moving

**Did:** Back on the homepage, I deliberately stared at the grey line across the top — the shipping one — because those bars usually rotate, and a rotating bar in my peripheral vision while I'm reading a product page is a slow-motion headache.
**Got:** `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH`, and it stayed exactly that. I checked again after browsing for a while; same line, every page. (`audit/screens/18-32-cat-tees.png` — same line still at the top a good while later)
**Expected:** For it to flip to something else within ten seconds. They always do.
**Felt:** Really good, and it's the kind of thing nobody would ever thank you for. It stopped, and it stopped **showing me a sentence**, not showing me a blank strip — I've had sites where turning motion off leaves an empty bar or a line of dashes where the text should be. Here I got the one message I'd most want: the free-shipping threshold. If there are others I'm not being shown, I don't feel their absence, and the one thing I might have missed — how many products there are — was already written in the middle of the homepage in bigger letters.
**Next:** continued

### Step 7 — Read the hero properly

**Did:** Read the middle of the homepage.
**Got:** `> 12 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE` / `CROOKSLDN` / `OWN THE STREETS™` / `CATALOGUE`. Complete, from the moment the page drew. I refreshed once out of habit and it was the same — the line is simply there, not assembled in front of me.
**Expected:** Given the `>` prompt and the terminal styling, I fully expected that line to type itself out character by character. That's the house style.
**Felt:** If there's a typing effect other people get, I'm not getting it, and I have lost precisely nothing: the sentence is the sentence. This is the right way round. The only thing that line carries is a number, and I have the number.
**Next:** continued

## Outcome

**Bought / didn't:** Didn't complete it tonight — but for once, not because the site defeated me. The tee is in the bag, in the right size and colour, and I'd have paid. What stopped me at the last step was the £2.50: the only discount this shop offers is locked behind a reaction game with three spinning, motion-blurred number reels, and that is the one thing on the entire site I physically cannot do. Paying full price because of how my head works is a sour note to end on.

**Total time:** About nine minutes, and — this is the number that matters to me — roughly ten seconds of that was spent looking at something moving. On most sites it's the whole visit.

**Worst moment:** The tumblers. *"I can't look at that."* Everything else on this site had honoured a setting my phone had already told it about, and then the one screen with money attached to it flickered at me. Runner-up, and the one that's actually the shop's own code: tapping `SIZE GUIDE` and having the page slide two and a half screens under my thumb when every other transition on the site is instant. It's brief, but it's bolted to the button that a careful shopper — the shopper this site otherwise suits perfectly — will always press.

**Best moment:** Genuinely a tie. `> Added — 1 in bag  View bag`, written in words, next to a counter that says `BAG [1]`, next to a bar that says `£25.00 · M · BLACK` — three confirmations, none of which needed anything to move. And the board at the bottom of the menu: a fully drawn still picture of the game instead of a blank frame, so I could see what I was being offered and turn it down on the merits.

**Would they come back?** Yes, and that surprises me. This is the calmest shop I've used in months: no carousel, no parallax, no reveal-on-scroll, no auto-playing gallery, no jiggling cart, nothing typed at me, and a top bar that stops on a message instead of stopping on nothing. I'd come back and go straight to `MENU` → `TEES`, and I would never press `SIZE GUIDE` again — I'd scroll to `MEASUREMENTS` myself.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Make `SIZE GUIDE` jump instead of glide when my phone has asked for that — it lands in exactly the right place already, so cutting straight there costs the shop nothing and takes the one piece of unasked-for movement out of the buying path. And if the 10% game can't be made watchable with motion off, offer the code some other way to people whose phones say they need it, rather than leaving the discount behind the one door they can't open.

## Verdict — does `SPEC.md §9`'s "reduced motion is respected" hold?

**Mostly yes, and where it holds it holds properly — information first, motion second.** Four things checked, three clean:

- **Status ticker** — stops, and stops *on a message*: `FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH`, still there after a solid minute of watching, on every page I visited. The message I never see (`12 PRODUCTS CURRENTLY ONLINE`) is duplicated in the hero as `> 12 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE` and again on the catalogue as `12 ITEMS`. Nothing lost.
- **Hero boot line** — no typing happened, and the line is complete and readable from the first paint. Nothing lost. (I never saw it type at all, so I can't say the setting is what stopped it; either way, the sentence is the sentence.)
- **Drawer board** — the model case. The canvas is *painted and frozen*: the full alley scene, thief, officer and coin all drawn, and nothing moves. A blank frame here would have cost the shop its best hidden feature; instead you can see the whole thing and decide.
- **`SIZE GUIDE`** — **does not respect it.** The page smooth-scrolls ~720px in both modes, identically. This is the only theme-owned motion that ignores the setting, and it is attached to the control a measurement-checking shopper is most likely to press.

**And one thing outside the theme:** the `CROOKSLDN: THE GETAWAY` pop-up honours the setting (it doesn't fade in), but the game it opens spins three motion-blurred digit reels continuously and never stops. The 10%-off code has no other route, so the discount is, in practice, gated behind motion.
