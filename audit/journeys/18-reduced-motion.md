# 18 — Priya, 34, graphic designer with a vestibular disorder; keeps "reduce motion" on system-wide because animation makes her physically ill
**Device:** iPhone-class mobile, 390×844, home wifi · **Goal:** heard about CROOKSLDN's baggies from a friend, wants a pair; also quietly stress-testing whether this site will make her sick like most streetwear sites do · **Mood:** Tuesday, 21:30, tired, braced — dark hype-brand sites are usually a strobing nightmare for her

### Step 1 — Opened the homepage and got ambushed
**Did:** Loaded the site. Cookie consent banner was already sitting over the bottom half of the screen; about three seconds later a full-screen dark overlay dropped over everything: "CRACK THE CUFFS — ESCAPE WITH A PRIVATE DISCOUNT", a red "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED" beacon, a BEGIN button. (18-01-arrival-popup.png, 18-10-game-idle.png)
**Got:** Two interruptions stacked before she'd seen a single product. The overlay itself appeared with a hard cut — no zoom, no slide — which she registered and appreciated. But the little red beacon on the game screen was already blinking at her.
**Expected:** Some popup, honestly. Every streetwear site has one.
**Felt:** "Okay, here we go." The instant cut instead of a swooshing entrance was a small mercy. The blinking red dot was a warning sign she chose to ignore because the word DISCOUNT was right there.
**Next:** continued — a private discount on £60 trousers is worth one tap

### Step 2 — Tapped BEGIN on the discount game
**Did:** Tapped BEGIN. (18-12-game-running.png)
**Got:** Three tumblers strobing through digits — 3, 4, 5, 6 flickering several times a second — a red "TIME REMAINING" bar draining down from 3 seconds, a pulsing "LOCK ACTIVE — TAP TO STOP" beacon. Exactly the kind of rapid, timed, flashing motion her setting exists to switch off. Her phone told this page she needs reduced motion; the game heard it and ran the spinners anyway. She shut her eyes, felt the room tilt slightly, and hit the X. Below the fold it said ONE ATTEMPT PER CUSTOMER — attempt burnt.
**Expected:** A code word puzzle, a scratch-card, something still. Not a slot machine.
**Felt:** "That's genuinely nasty. I told you not to do that, and you did it anyway — and now I don't get the discount at all." The only motion sickness moment on the whole site, and it's the one gate to the discount.
**Next:** went back — closed it, slightly queasy, genuinely annoyed

### Step 3 — Cleared the cookie banner and finally looked at the homepage
**Did:** Tapped Decline on the cookie banner, then actually read the top of the page. (18-02-home-cookie.png, 18-03-home-top.png)
**Got:** A page that holds completely still. The terminal-style boot line ("> 14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE") was already fully typed the moment she looked at it — no letter-by-letter typing. The shipping strip at the very top ("FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00…") just sat there; she watched it for a good ten seconds waiting for it to start crawling or swapping. It never moved.
**Expected:** A fake-terminal site to type at her and a ticker to scroll. They always do.
**Felt:** "Oh. Oh, this is nice." Genuine relief — the aesthetic is all there, but frozen solid, like a photograph of a terminal instead of a screensaver. She stopped bracing.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Opened the MENU drawer
**Did:** Tapped MENU, top right. (18-04-drawer-open.png, 18-05-drawer-case-board.png)
**Got:** The drawer appeared as a clean instant cut — no panel sliding in from the side, no menu items cascading in one by one. Every link was just there. At the bottom, a "PLAY CASE:001 NOW" panel with a pixel-art prison map — a little thief figure, an officer, cell bars — held perfectly still, a single frozen frame like a poster for the game rather than the game playing at her.
**Expected:** The drawer to slide and that game board to be wriggling away in the corner.
**Felt:** "Even the game board is holding still for me." She knows a live minigame ad when she sees one; this one being a still image felt deliberate and respectful. She was never going to tap PLAY, though. Fool me once.
**Next:** continued — tapped DENIM, because baggies are denim, obviously

### Step 5 — Dead end in DENIM
**Did:** Tapped DENIM in the drawer. Scrolled the collection. (18-06-denim-collection.png)
**Got:** Four items: two jorts, two jeans. No baggies anywhere. The page itself was calm — cards, prices, "AVAILABLE" tags, no hover tricks, nothing moving.
**Expected:** Baggy trousers to live under denim.
**Felt:** "Where are they then?" Mild irritation, none of it motion-related. Apparently "baggies" are sweats here. How is she meant to know that?
**Next:** went back — tapped CATALOGUE in the header instead

### Step 6 — Found the V2 BAGGIES in the full catalogue
**Did:** Scrolled the full 14-item catalogue to item NO. 11. (18-13-catalogue-baggies.png)
**Got:** V2 BAGGIES, £60, AVAILABLE, filed under SWEATS. The long scroll was uneventful in the best way — no cards fading in as she scrolled, no images sliding into place, nothing loading in with a flourish. Everything was simply already there.
**Expected:** Scroll-triggered reveal animations — the classic "content assembles itself as you pass" that usually forces her to scroll with one eye closed.
**Felt:** "This is the first shop scroll in months that hasn't cost me anything." Fine but genuinely notable for her.
**Next:** continued — tapped the card

### Step 7 — The product page: read it, poked a dead size
**Did:** Read the PDP top to bottom. Two photos with tap thumbnails ("PHOTO 1 OF 2") — no auto-playing carousel. Sizes: XS boxed and pre-selected, S live, and M, L, XL drawn in dashed boxes with the letters struck through. She tapped M anyway to check it was really dead. (18-14-pdp-top.png, 18-15-soldout-size.png)
**Got:** M didn't respond at all — no shake, no error toast bouncing in, just nothing. IN STOCK stayed on for XS. She's an XS, so this was her lucky night; anyone size M or above walks away empty-handed with no "notify me" offered on the dead sizes.
**Expected:** Sold-out sizes to be greyed out; some sites do a little "no" wiggle animation, which she hates.
**Felt:** "Three of five sizes gone on a Tuesday — glad I'm small." The struck-through dashed boxes read clearly as 'gone' without a single pixel moving. The page as a whole: still, legible, calm.
**Next:** continued

### Step 8 — ADD TO BAG
**Did:** With XS selected, tapped ADD TO BAG. (18-17-after-add.png)
**Got:** No cart drawer flying across the screen, no product image arcing into the bag icon, no bouncing badge. One quiet new line under the buttons — "> Added — 1 in bag  View bag" — and the header bag went from [0] to [1]. She checked: nothing animated anywhere on the page while it happened.
**Expected:** The usual cart drawer slamming in from the right — the single most nauseating pattern in mobile shopping, in her experience.
**Felt:** "That's it? That's all? Perfect." Best moment of the visit. She did have to notice the little line herself — it doesn't announce itself — but she'd take understated over airborne every time.
**Next:** continued — tapped View bag

### Step 9 — The bag and through to checkout
**Did:** Looked over the bag page, then tapped Check out. (18-18-cart.png, 18-19-checkout-landing.png)
**Got:** The bag showed her baggies, £60, and a free-shipping meter ("£10.00 to free Tracked 24") sitting motionless at 85% — no bar crawling upward to impress her. The page looked oddly more like plain default Shopify than the evidence-terminal styling everywhere else, which briefly made her double-check she was on the same shop. Check out cut straight to the standard white Shopify checkout, order summary £60.00, no animation on the way in. She got as far as the contact and delivery form and stopped for the night — she'd hand over card details on payday.
**Expected:** A styled checkout to match the theme; got vanilla Shopify, which at least behaves itself.
**Felt:** "Bright! Warn a girl." The dark-to-white jump was a blink, not a wobble. Nothing on the entire path from product to checkout moved without her telling it to.
**Next:** continued to checkout — done for the evening

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached Shopify checkout with V2 BAGGIES XS, £60 (audit stops at the payment wall; she had genuine intent and the size that was left happened to be hers).
**Total time:** ~14 minutes, two of which were recovering from the tumbler game.
**Worst moment:** Tapping BEGIN on Crack the Cuffs and getting three digit-strobing tumblers plus a draining red countdown despite her reduced-motion setting — "I told you not to do this, you did it anyway, and it cost me my one shot at the discount."
**Best moment:** Realising the whole shop holds still for her — boot line pre-typed, ticker frozen, menu a hard cut, the CASE 001 board a single still frame, add-to-bag a quiet line of text. She never once had to look away from the screen.
**Would they come back?** Yes — this is the calmest streetwear site she's used, and she'll say so to the group chat. She will never tap another popup here, and she quietly resents paying full price because the discount lives behind a spinning minigame.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** A motion-free path to the Crack the Cuffs discount (a still version of the game, or any static entry), and not burning the "one attempt per customer" when someone closes it three seconds in. She'd have had 10% off in her pocket instead of a headache.
