# 14 — Trevor, 56, night-shift security guard whose son keeps going on about this brand
**Device:** old Android, 360x800, congested 4G (slow, ~150ms lag) · **Goal:** have a proper look at the site his son rates, maybe grab the £6 socks · **Mood:** settled on the sofa about 10pm, expects the internet to be rubbish because it always is at this hour

### Step 1 — Opened the site and waited
**Did:** Tapped the link and sat there watching the screen.
**Got:** A dark screen with nothing on it for the first couple of seconds — by one second in, literally the only thing in the page was a "Skip to content" line. Then at about the 3-second mark the whole top of the page appeared at once: the FREE UK SHIPPING ticker, the handcuffs logo, CATALOGUE / SEARCH / BAG / MENU, "14 PRODUCTS AVAILABLE TO PURCHASE", and the big CROOKSLDN — OWN THE STREETS™ title. All text, all readable, typewriter-looking letters already in place. (14-01-home-3s.png)
**Expected:** A minute of white screen and spinning, like most shop sites on his connection.
**Felt:** "Oh — that's up already." Three seconds of black is nothing; he's waited longer for the news site to show a headline.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — The cookie notice landed
**Did:** Kept reading while the page finished loading.
**Got:** Around 3–4 seconds a big grey COOKIE CONSENT box slid up over the bottom half of the screen — a paragraph about partners and ads, with Accept and Decline stacked underneath. At 5 seconds and again at 10 seconds the page behind it was identical — nothing moved, nothing jumped, no pictures popping in and shoving text around. He read the first two lines of the notice, gave up on the rest, tapped Accept. (14-02-home-5s.png, 14-03-home-10s.png, 14-05-after-accept.png)
**Expected:** The usual cookie nag; expected the page to shuffle about as pictures arrived, because that's what pages do on his phone.
**Felt:** "Half the screen for cookies, honestly." But credit where due — the page behind it stood still the whole time. Nothing jumped. That never happens.
**Next:** continued

### Step 3 — Scrolled down the catalogue
**Did:** Scrolled down steadily through the product grid, pausing at each pair.
**Got:** Product photos were there every time he stopped — grey crewneck £50, shorts £45, blue jeans £60, jorts £50, all with prices and AVAILABLE labels. Because the whole site is near-black, he never saw white empty boxes even when a photo was presumably still on its way. Further down, a "PROPERTY BAG — EVERY ORDER SHIPS LIKE THIS" section with evidence tags and handcuffs. (14-07, 14-08, 14-09)
**Expected:** Grey placeholder squares and photos trailing in ten seconds behind him.
**Felt:** "It's keeping up." The evidence-tag packaging thing made him snort — bit silly, but he gets why his son likes it.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Tapped the socks
**Did:** Spotted WHITE/RED MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS at £6 in the last row and tapped it.
**Got:** The product page came up in about two, three seconds, complete: big photo of the socks, "PRODUCT 13 / 14", £6.00, and a bar pinned to the bottom of the screen with ADD TO BAG and CHECKOUT NOW. (14-11-pdp-3s.png)
**Expected:** Another proper wait — product pages with big photos are usually the slow ones.
**Felt:** "That was the quickest page yet." Six quid, one photo, two big purple buttons — easiest page on the site.
**Next:** continued

### Step 5 — Added to bag, nearly twice
**Did:** Tapped ADD TO BAG.
**Got:** Nothing visible happened where his thumb was — no popup, no basket sliding out, the button didn't change. About a second and a half later the BAG counter at the very top of the page ticked from [0] to [1]. He'd already lifted his thumb to tap the button again when he noticed the [1]. (14-17-after-atb.png)
**Expected:** Some obvious "added!" — a tick, a drawer, anything near the button.
**Felt:** "Did that go in or not?" On his internet, a button that doesn't react means it didn't work. He very nearly double-tapped.
**Next:** hesitated, then continued

### Step 6 — Tapped CHECKOUT NOW and nothing happened
**Did:** Tapped CHECKOUT NOW in the bottom bar.
**Got:** Nothing. Still the socks page. He waited. Ten seconds, twenty. Screen unchanged, no spinner, no message — a good half-minute of nothing at all. (14-20-checkout-arrived.png — still the socks page long after the tap)
**Expected:** The checkout page, eventually. Or at least a spinner to say it heard him.
**Felt:** "Here we go. This is where it dies." Said out loud, at the phone. This is the moment he'd have given up on a site he cared less about.
**Next:** hesitated — nearly gave up — tried again

### Step 7 — Tried again, and got a surprise at checkout
**Did:** Tapped CHECKOUT NOW again.
**Got:** This time it went — a few seconds and the checkout appeared: white page, CROOKSLDN at the top, grey empty blocks where the fast-payment buttons hadn't loaded yet, and the Order summary reading £12.00. Twelve pounds. For the £6 socks he'd added once. Two pairs, apparently — the checkout button had put another pair in on top of the one already in his bag. (14-21-checkout-probe.png)
**Expected:** £6.00 plus maybe postage.
**Felt:** "Twelve quid? For socks?" He didn't trust it. Late at night, slow phone, a total that's double what he pressed — no chance he's typing his address into that. He put the phone down and decided to sort it out another day, on the laptop.
**Next:** gave up (at the checkout, not before)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't — he genuinely reached checkout with the socks (that's further than he expected to get on his connection), but the summary said £12.00 for two pairs when he'd added one, and he wasn't untangling that at 10pm. (Audit never passes the payment step regardless.)
**Total time:** ~12 minutes
**Worst moment:** The two-part checkout ending — CHECKOUT NOW doing nothing for half a minute ("this is where it dies"), then finally working and showing £12 for one intended pair of £6 socks ("twelve quid? For socks?").
**Best moment:** The cold load. On genuinely bad internet the page was readable at three seconds, nothing ever jumped or flashed white, the typewriter text was just there, and photos kept pace with his scrolling. For his phone, that's remarkable.
**Would they come back?** Probably — the browsing was painless, which is rare for him, and it's only £6 at stake. But he'll do the checkout on the laptop, and if it says £12 again he's done.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** If CHECKOUT NOW hadn't quietly added a second pair on top of the one already in his bag (and had responded first tap), he'd have paid his £6 there and then.
