# 02 — Dan, 34, has been burned by an Instagram-ad clothes shop before and remembers it
**Device:** iPhone-ish, 390×844, decent home wifi · **Goal:** wants baggy jeans, saw CROOKSLDN mentioned somewhere, but £60 to a label he's never heard of needs proof: contact details, returns, shipping costs, and evidence anyone else has ever bought here · **Mood:** interested, arms folded

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage and got ambushed twice
**Did:** Opened the site at about 9pm. Before reading a word of it, dealt with what was on top.
**Got:** The whole page dimmed behind a cookie consent notice — and then a full-screen popup slid in ON TOP of that: "ACCESS BREACH DETECTED — CRACK THE CUFFS — escape with a private discount", a little game with a BEGIN button. The cookie banner's Accept/Decline buttons were physically underneath it, untappable until the popup was gone. Two taps just to see the shop: × on the popup, then Decline on the cookies. (02-02-popup-over-cookies.png)
**Expected:** A homepage. Maybe one banner.
**Felt:** Two layers of stuff between me and the shop before I've seen a single product. The game's actually kind of funny — and the small print under it was weirdly proper, "open to UK residents 16+, no purchase required" — but I'm not playing games with a shop I don't trust yet. Also I noticed I declined cookies and the site took it without a fight, which I quietly respected.
**Next:** continued

### Step 2 — Scrolled the homepage
**Did:** Thumbed down through the catalogue.
**Got:** "FREE UK SHIPPING OVER £20 — ORDER BY 18:00 FOR SAME-DAY DISPATCH" pinned at the very top. Then a full 14-item catalogue right on the homepage, numbered like evidence exhibits, prices visible on every card: £6 socks up to £85 set. Blue Wash OG Jeans, £60, "AVAILABLE". Footer at the bottom has SHIPPING, REFUNDS, QUESTIONS, TERMS, TRACK ORDER, and a CONTACT block with INSTAGRAM / TIKTOK / EMAIL. (02-04-footer.png)
**Expected:** A moody lookbook that hides the prices and buries the boring pages.
**Felt:** Fine. Better than fine, actually — prices upfront, and the shipping cost question answered before I even asked it. The police-evidence styling is a lot, but it's committed.
**Next:** continued — **shipping costs found: 0 taps** (it's the first line on the page)

### Step 3 — Tapped the £60 jeans
**Did:** Tapped BLUE WASH OG JEANS £60.00 from the catalogue grid.
**Got:** Clean product page: flat shot plus on-model shot, all five sizes in stock, size guide, "Order before 18:00 and it ships today (Mon–Sat) — Ordered now, leaves tomorrow" (true, it's 9pm Tuesday), a purple "Buy with Shop" button, and four accordions: SPECIFICATION, ITEM DESCRIPTION, MEASUREMENTS, CHAIN OF CUSTODY — SHIPPING & RETURNS. No reviews. No stars. Nothing. (02-07-jeans-pdp-top.png)
**Expected:** Some star rating, "437 sold", anything.
**Felt:** Page looks the business. But it's silent — not one other human being visible anywhere on it. I'm going to have to work out on my own whether this place is real.
**Next:** continued

### Step 4 — Opened the accordions and caught them in a contradiction
**Did:** Tapped SPECIFICATION, then ITEM DESCRIPTION, then MEASUREMENTS, then CHAIN OF CUSTODY.
**Got:** Spec: 14oz denim, made in Portugal. Measurements: proper laid-flat table in cm and inches. Chain of custody: same-day dispatch before 18:00, Royal Mail Tracked, free UK over £20, UK 1–2 working days, 14 days to return unworn with tags. But the ITEM DESCRIPTION, one accordion above, says "9-16 days delivery uk / 16-21 days international". (02-15-desc-open.png, 02-12-custody-open.png)
**Expected:** The same story told once.
**Felt:** Hang on. Which is it — tomorrow, or two and a half weeks? "9-16 days delivery uk", lowercase, no punctuation — that is EXACTLY the line dropshippers forget to delete. Everything else on this page says Royal Mail and Portugal, and then that one sentence says a warehouse in Guangzhou. That's the worst thing I've seen on this site and it nearly ended the evening.
**Next:** hesitated — hard

### Step 5 — Went to the footer for the returns policy
**Did:** Back to the homepage, scrolled to the footer, tapped REFUNDS. **(1 tap from homepage)**
**Got:** A real returns policy in actual sentences: 14 days from delivery, unworn with tags, free UK size swaps (you pay the return postage), faulty items refunded in full postage included, refunds in 5–7 days, "doesn't affect your statutory rights" — and a physical return address: Oairo UK Office, Bourne End Business Park, Buckinghamshire, SL8 5AS. (02-16-refund-policy.png)
**Expected:** Three lines of boilerplate or a blank template page.
**Felt:** This is a proper policy — someone who has actually posted parcels wrote this. Two squints though: who is "Oairo"? Not CROOKSLDN. Probably a fulfilment place, but nobody explains it. And returns go via… crooksldn@gmail.com. Gmail. A brand on a gmail address, like a Year 11 business studies project.
**Next:** continued — **returns policy found: 1 tap**

### Step 6 — Tapped SHIPPING
**Did:** Back, footer again, tapped SHIPPING. **(1 tap from homepage)**
**Got:** Actual numbers: free UK over £20, free Tracked 24 over £70, under that standard £3 or Tracked 24 £4.99. Same-day dispatch before 18:00 Mon–Sat. UK 1–2 working days. Even a paragraph warning about customs duties on international orders and a lost-parcel promise. (02-17-shipping-policy.png)
**Expected:** "Calculated at checkout" and nothing else.
**Felt:** Genuinely good. £3, £4.99 — numbers a scam site never bothers inventing. But it flatly contradicts that "9-16 days" line on the jeans page, and now I don't know which page to believe.
**Next:** continued — **shipping costs, full tariff: 1 tap** (headline was 0)

### Step 7 — Hunted for contact details
**Did:** Tapped MENU, then CONTACT in the drawer. **(2 taps from homepage)**
**Got:** A page that says CONTACT with a Name / Email / Phone / Comment form on a cream background that looks nothing like the rest of the site. No email address. No phone number. No address. No company name. Nothing to write down. (02-19-contact-page.png)
**Expected:** An email, an address, a "who we are" — the stuff I'd quote in a dispute.
**Felt:** This is the weakest page on the site. A form is where complaints go to die. The only actual contact detail this shop has is that gmail address hiding in the footer — tapping EMAIL there just opens my mail app to crooksldn@gmail.com. **(email found: 1 tap, via footer)** So: contactable, yes. Accountable-looking, no.
**Next:** continued, unimpressed

### Step 8 — Checked the FAQ and the Terms
**Did:** Footer → QUESTIONS, opened "HOW MUCH IS SHIPPING?", "CAN I RETURN SOMETHING?", "WHAT PAYMENT METHODS DO YOU TAKE?". Then the TERMS page.
**Got:** FAQ answers match the policies word for word — free over £20, 14 days, and payment is "Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay, plus all major cards at checkout". Terms page is nine numbered plain-English sections, "LAST REVISED 13.08.2026" — five days ago — with oddly convincing detail: 48 hours to report transit damage, Royal Mail's 10-working-day investigation window, "drops are for people, not scripts". But nowhere on it a company number, a registered name, a legal anybody. (02-21-faq-answers.png, 02-22-terms.png)
**Expected:** Contradictions between pages — that's how you catch them out.
**Felt:** Annoyingly consistent. The story holds together everywhere except that one cursed line on the jeans page. A terms page updated last week means someone is actively running this. Still no idea WHO, legally, but someone.
**Next:** continued

### Step 9 — Went looking for other human beings
**Did:** Tapped INSTAGRAM in the footer. **(1 tap from homepage)**
**Got:** Thrown out of the shop entirely — same tab, straight to instagram.com/crooksldn. The profile wouldn't load properly on my phone, so I never saw a follower count or a tagged photo. Came back. TikTok link also exists (@crooksldn); the terms mention "DM @crooksldn" as a support channel. On the site itself: no reviews, no press, no "as worn by", no customer photos. Nothing, anywhere. **(reviews found: never — no tap count reaches them)**
**Expected:** At minimum a review widget; ideally an Instagram embed with comments I could sniff-test.
**Felt:** So the grand total of proof that any other person has ever bought from this shop is: zero. Is that fatal? …Honestly, no — and here's my reasoning. Reviews can be faked in an afternoon; what can't be faked as easily is a shipping tariff in pounds and pence, a physical returns address, a terms page revised last Thursday, and — crucially — checkout that runs through Shop Pay / PayPal / Google Pay, which means my card details never touch these people and my money has a chargeback rope attached. If they'd asked me to type a card number into their own dark little terminal, no reviews would have killed it. They don't, so it survives. Barely.
**Next:** continued, decision made

### Step 10 — Put the jeans in the bag
**Did:** Back to the jeans, tapped M, tapped ADD TO BAG.
**Got:** A quiet "Added — 1 in bag · View bag" note under the button — no drawer flying out, no "HURRY 3 LEFT". Tapped View bag: cart shows the jeans in M, £60, a progress bar saying "£10.00 to free Tracked 24", "Pay in 3 interest-free instalments of £20.00 with Shop", "Duties and taxes included". (02-25-added-toast.png, 02-26-bag-page.png)
**Expected:** Countdown timers and fake scarcity — the usual pressure tricks.
**Felt:** The absence of screaming is itself a good sign. The £10-to-free-Tracked-24 bar nearly got me looking at the £6 socks, which I recognise as a trick and slightly admire.
**Next:** continued

### Step 11 — Walked to the checkout door
**Did:** Tapped Check out.
**Got:** Real Shopify checkout — and it's on crooksldn.com, an actual owned domain — with Shop Pay, PayPal and Google Pay express buttons at the top, £60.00 order summary. Stopped there. Nothing entered. (02-27-checkout-door.png)
**Expected:** Honestly, half-expected some janky payment page that would end the whole thing.
**Felt:** PayPal button. That's the whole ballgame. If this goes wrong I dispute it and PayPal claws it back. I'd do it through that, tonight or tomorrow — though I'd screenshot the "UK 1–2 working days" page first, as evidence, because if those jeans take 16 days I'm quoting it back at them.
**Next:** stopped at the payment step — as far as I go

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached checkout with the M jeans and would have paid via PayPal (never card-direct). The specific, self-consistent policies plus protected payment rails outweighed the silence where reviews should be.
**Total time:** ~25 minutes — most of it on the policy pages, twice interrupted by going back to re-read the delivery contradiction.
**Worst moment:** Opening ITEM DESCRIPTION and reading "9-16 days delivery uk" one inch above a shipping panel promising UK 1–2 working days. Out loud: "Which is it? That's the AliExpress line. That exact line."
**Best moment:** The shipping policy giving real numbers — £3, £4.99, free over £20 — followed by a returns policy with a physical Buckinghamshire address. "Someone who has actually posted a parcel wrote this."
**Would they come back?** Yes — if the first pair arrives in 1–2 days as promised, this becomes a trusted shop and the next visit skips all 25 minutes of vetting. If it takes 16 days, never, and he tells people.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Not the outcome — the margin. Delete the "9-16 days delivery uk" line from the product description (and put a real business identity — even just "CROOKSLDN is run by X Ltd, company no. Y" and a non-gmail email — on the Contact page) and this is a confident purchase instead of one made through PayPal with a screenshot folder open.

**On the missing reviews, in character:** survivable, not fatal — because the payment rails (Shop Pay / PayPal / Google Pay) carry the trust the reviews would have, and the policy pages are too specific and too consistent to be a template scam. But it's survivable *despite* the shop, not because of it: one gmail address, one mystery fulfilment name ("Oairo"), one contradictory delivery line, and zero visible customers is a trust stack held up entirely by Shopify's checkout.

**Verdict — would I trust this shop with £60?** Yes, through PayPal, once. Never card-direct, and it's on probation until the first parcel lands.
