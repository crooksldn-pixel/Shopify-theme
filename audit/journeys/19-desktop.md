## The four questions I was actually here to answer

### 1. What only really works on mobile?

| Thing | How it reads at 1440 |
|---|---|
| **The `CRACK THE CUFFS` pop-up** | A portrait panel roughly 440 wide by 680 tall — a phone screen — floating in the middle of a 1440×900 window with black all round it. Buttons stacked full-width like a phone sheet. Its close button is in the corner of the *browser window*, not the panel. |
| **The menu drawer** | A 420px column against the right edge with the rest of the screen dimmed and dead. On a phone that's a full-screen takeover and it's the right answer; here I'm looking at a sliver of menu and a big grey pane of shop I'm not allowed to touch. |
| **The `CASE 001` map inside the drawer** | Taller than a 900px laptop screen. The drawer's contents run past the bottom of the window, so the animation is clipped and `PLAY CASE:001 NOW` is below the fold — inside a panel you don't expect to have to scroll. |
| **The colour squares on the cards** | 12 pixels square, no label, no hover, click navigates. Fine for a thumb tapping a target it can see the size of; on a mouse they're smaller than the cursor and they don't tell you what they are. |
| **The four toggles and filters** | No hover state whatsoever. On a touchscreen there is no hover so nothing is lost. On a laptop it means nothing on the page ever responds to the mouse being on it. |
| **The catalogue photographs** | The pictures inside the cards are about 290px across — sized for a phone card, shown on a screen where the card itself is 317. They're the weakest images on the site and they're the first thing I see. |

### 2. Is there wasted space, and does it help or hurt?

**Three places, and only one of them helps.**

- **The homepage hero — hurts.** The name, the tagline and the button are all in the right-hand half; the left half is empty black, edge to edge, top to bottom. Worse, the right-hand block doesn't line up with the logo above it or the `CATALOGUE` heading below it, so the top of the page has three different left edges and one of them is halfway across the screen. It reads as a picture that didn't load. `19-69-hero-clean.png`
- **The questions and terms pages — hurts a bit.** The reading column is about a third of the screen against the left edge, with the right two-thirds black. The line length itself is right and I'd defend it. What hurts is that the *rows* are full width while the *text* isn't, so a question and its `+` end up a screen apart with a thousand pixels of nothing between them. Pull the `+` in to the end of the text and the emptiness stops being noticeable. `19-43-faq.png`
- **The product page — helps.** The photograph on the left, the buy panel on the right, and generous margin round both. Nothing is stretched, nothing is stranded, and everything I need to buy is above the fold. This is the page that knows what to do with a wide screen, and the four panels going full width underneath it — especially the custody strip running four across instead of four down — is exactly right. `19-26-pdp1-scroll0.png`

There's also the better part of seven hundred pixels of nothing in the middle of the header bar between the logo and the nav, which I only mention because it's the same habit: content pushed to the two edges and the middle left empty.

### 3. Does the sticky bar behave?

**Yes, completely, and it should be left alone.** At 1440 the bottom bar **never appears at all** — not on arrival, not on scroll, not after picking a size, not after adding to bag. I checked at seven different scroll positions on the crewneck page. What happens instead: the buy panel stays pinned beside the photograph for the first screenful, then scrolls away with the rest of the page when the specification panels start.

**At no point were the real `ADD TO BAG` and a floating copy of it on screen together.** That is the correct answer and it's the opposite of what most shops do to a laptop.

The only thing I'd note: once the panel has scrolled off there is nothing at all — no price, no size, no button — from the specification down to the bottom of the page. On this site it doesn't matter, because the whole page is under three screens tall and one flick of the wheel puts the button back. On a longer page it would. `19-36-scrolled-bottom.png`

### 4. Are the photographs big enough for a 1440 screen — can you see the fabric?

**On the product page, just about yes. In the catalogue, no.**

The crewneck photograph is a bit under half the width of my screen, and at that size I could see the brushed nap of the fleece, the rib on the cuff and hem, the piping down the sleeve and the embroidered cuffs on the chest. It's a shade soft — the sort of soft you get from a photo shown slightly larger than it wants to be — but I could tell it was heavy, and `450gsm brushed fleece` in the specification confirmed what the picture already told me. That's the job done. `19-32-fabric-crop.png`

Two things stop it being a clear yes:

- **There is no way to get closer.** Hovering does nothing, clicking does nothing. On a laptop, the picture is the whole product and I expect to be able to lean in. `PHOTO 1 OF 1` also means there is nothing else to look at — one photograph, of one side, of a £50 garment.
- **The catalogue pictures are much weaker than the product ones.** Roughly 290px across in a card that's 317 wide, on a 1440 screen. You can tell a sweatshirt from a pair of shorts. You cannot tell what anything is made of. Given the whole shop fits on one screen and there's a mountain of margin around every card, those images have room to be considerably bigger.

### 5. What does a cursor find that a thumb never will?

| Hovered | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Product cards** | Purple outline — and on **4 of 12**, the picture changes to a second photograph. The blue jeans become a person wearing them. This is the biggest hidden feature on the site. |
| **Menu drawer links** | Grey → purple. Works properly. |
| **Questions on the FAQ** | Cream → purple. Works properly. |
| **`FLAT` / `ON MODEL` / `OUTLINE`** | Nothing. Cursor only. |
| **Category filters (`DENIM`, `SWEATS`…)** | Nothing. Cursor only. |
| **Size buttons `XS`–`XL`** | Nothing. Cursor only. |
| **`ADD TO BAG`** (live) | Nothing. Cursor only. |
| **Colour squares** | Nothing — no tooltip, no name, no picture change. Clicking navigates to another product. |
| **The main product photograph** | Nothing. No zoom, no cursor change; clicking does nothing. |

**Discoverable only by hover:** the second photograph on four of the twelve cards — including the only picture of anything on a human being. **The button that claims to do this, `ON MODEL`, changes exactly one card, and it isn't one of those four.**

## Outcome

**Bought / didn't:** Bought — `CHARCOAL CELLBLOCK CREWNECK` size M at £50 and `BLACK/BLUE MOTIONTEC™ SOCKS` 3pc at £15, £65 in the bag, walked to the payment page and stopped there without paying. If this were my own money and not an audit I'd have gone through: the fabric weight was stated, the returns clause was stated in words I understood, and it told me the parcel leaves tomorrow. I'd have added another £5 of socks for the free Tracked 24, too — that bar worked on me.

**Total time:** about 25 minutes, which is roughly twice what I'd normally give a shop I'd never bought from. That's a compliment.

**Worst moment:** Step 4 into 5 — reading the packaging section, having the screen go dark under a phone-shaped pop-up, hunting for a close button that turned out to be in the far corner of the window, and then watching the offer rewrite itself into a *different* offer while I read it. Out loud: *"Hang on — is the code a text or isn't it? And which twenty minutes are we talking about?"*

**Best moment:** Step 3, and Step 12 right behind it. Two flicks of the wheel and I had seen every single thing this shop sells, four across, priced, with stock stated — no carousel, no "load more", no filters to fight. Then one click and a product page that puts the photograph, the price, the sizes, the dispatch promise and the buy button on one screen with nothing floating over the bottom. That's a shop that respects a big screen.

**Would they come back?** Yes. It's the most confident-looking small shop I've been on in a long time and the writing is honest — plain English on shipping, plain English on returns, no fake urgency, no "17 people are viewing this", no invented reviews. I'd come back and I'd browse rather than hunt, which is exactly what this layout is for.

**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** Make `ON MODEL` show the model photographs that already exist. I found four of them by accident with my mouse, including the only picture of a human being wearing anything, while the button that promises exactly that sat three inches above doing nothing on eleven of twelve cards. It is the difference between "flat cut-outs on black, I'll have to imagine it" and "here is what it looks like on a person". Everything else on my list — the empty left half of the homepage, the pop-up rewriting its own offer, `SELECT A SIZE` on a product sold by quantity, the photograph I can't zoom — is second to that.

## Three things I'd tell the owner, from someone who browsed properly rather than hunted

1. **The model photographs exist and almost nobody will ever see them.** They are behind a mouse hover on four of twelve cards. Everyone on a phone gets nothing, and everyone on a laptop who presses the button *labelled for this purpose* also gets nothing. Wire `ON MODEL` to the pictures you already have and it becomes the best feature on the site instead of the most disappointing one. (`19-72-hover-jeans-crop.png` vs `19-73-nohover-jeans-crop.png`; `19-63-flat.png` vs `19-64-onmodel.png` — one card of twelve changes.)

2. **The homepage hero leaves the whole left half of a laptop screen empty, and the brand name doesn't line up with anything.** The logo is at the far left, the `CATALOGUE` heading below is at the far left, and `CROOKSLDN` is stranded past the middle of the screen with nothing beside it. It's the first impression and it reads as a page with a missing picture. Either put something in that space or bring the type back to the same left edge as everything else. (`19-69-hero-clean.png`)

3. **The pop-up shows two different versions of the same offer, seconds apart, and has two close buttons in two different places.** First: *"Crack the cuffs. 10% off your first order — code sent by text. Attempts unlimited."* / *"One code per player. Code expires 20 minutes after you win."* Then it repaints to: *"CRACK THE CUFFS. 10% off your first order if you do. Three tumblers. Click each one at the right moment."* / *"One code per player. Attempts unlimited. Code expires in 20 minutes."* / *"(this drop closes 15.09)"*. One promises a text message, the other doesn't mention one; one clock starts when you win, the other has already started. Pick one set of words. And while you're there — it lands on top of whatever I'm reading about ninety seconds in, which is the exact moment I was getting interested. (`19-40-hero.png`, `19-16-ctc-plus6s.png`)
