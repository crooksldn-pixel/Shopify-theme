# 17 — Dev, 34, partially sighted, lives at 200% browser zoom
**Device:** desktop 1440x900 at 200% zoom (effective 720x450 viewport), fast connection · **Goal:** heard this indie label has World Cup polos in stock — wants one that's actually buyable, in his size · **Mood:** calm but braced — most shops shatter at 200%, and he's been burned before

*(Session note: the audit egress IP geo-detects as US; sessions were pinned `?country=GB` as a real UK visitor would see it — one un-pinned control run showed the same PDP silently flip to $32.00 USD. All figures are the 720x450 GBP view. Read-only run: cart used, terms box ticked to enable the button, stopped at the checkout landing page, nothing entered.)*

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage, and it didn't fall over
**Did:** Opened unfoundedstudios.com. Black header across the top — burger, bird logo, search/account/bag icons — 96px of his 450px viewport, sticky, permanent. Below it the hero: "UNFOUNDED — World Cup Drop Live!" with a white Shop Now pill.
**Got:** The whole first message intact in one screenful: headline fully visible, Shop Now fully visible (44px tall), slide counter 01/02 underneath. Zero horizontal scrolling — he checked deliberately, the way you check a floor you've fallen through before. Document width exactly 720, on every page he visited all session.
**Expected:** The usual 200% carnage — nav collapsed on top of itself, a hero cropped to half a word (that's exactly what this hero does to sideways phones at 390px tall, apparently — 450px is just enough).
**Felt:** "No sideways scroll, no overlap, and the button they want me to press is actually on my screen. Decent start — most shops fail me in the first five seconds."
**Next:** continued (u17-01-arrival)

### Step 2 — The newsletter popup: full-screen ambush, fair exit
**Did:** ~10 seconds in, a "Sign up for our newsletter" sheet slid up over the page — at 450px tall it's effectively a takeover: dimmed page above, white card covering the bottom ~60%, 100% of the viewport claimed one way or the other.
**Got:** Everything fit inside his little window: heading (big serif, easy), subline, EMAIL field with Subscribe inside it (48px tall), and an underlined NO THANKS sitting at y≈386 — 40px clear of the bottom edge, fully clickable. One click, gone for good. The only sniff: NO THANKS is 85x24px in 12px type — the smallest text on the card is the one that sets you free.
**Expected:** The classic short-viewport trap — a dismiss link shoved below the fold he can never reach, popup pinned over the page forever.
**Felt:** "You took my whole screen, but you left the door where I could see it. I'll allow it."
**Next:** continued (u17-02-popup, u17-03-after-popup)

### Step 3 — Searched "polo" — the overlay is huge, the suggestions are headless
**Did:** Wanted something in stock, so went straight to the magnifier. It opened a full-screen search: input at the top in 21px type with CLEAR and a big X — genuinely comfortable at zoom. Typed "polo".
**Got:** A predictive panel of product photos, two across, filling the screen… and that's all it showed: two headless polo shirts. The panel is 1632px of content in his 450px window with no internal scrollbar — every product name, price and the "Italy Polo" collection link render below his fold; the suggestion list is effectively pictures-only until you guess to scroll. He pressed Enter instead: a proper results page — "SEARCH RESULTS FOR polo⁶", Filters and Relevance dropdowns, "Showing 6 of 6 products", two-column grid, big photos. Scrolled to the blue one: ITALY POLO. (A floating scroll-to-top bubble squats on the bottom-right of every screenful, camped over card corners.)
**Expected:** Suggestions he could read, not a picture quiz. But full marks for a search results page that stays one-column-honest with no clipping.
**Felt:** "The search box is the most legible thing on the site. The suggestions under it forgot to bring their words."
**Next:** continued — clicked the Italy Polo (u17-05-search-open, u17-06-search-predictive, u17-07-search-results, u17-08-italy-card)

### Step 5 — Found the size chart hiding in photo 2 — and on the page he can READ it
**Did:** Wanted measurements before committing. No "Size guide" link anywhere on the page. Three photos in the gallery: flat lay, something, on-model. The 64px thumbnails are postage stamps at his eyesight — thumb 2 was just "small colourful blobs". Clicked it anyway.
**Got:** Photo 2 is the four polo colourways PLUS a full size chart baked into the image — INCH: Pit to Pit 20.25→24.25, Length 24→28, Sleeve Length 10→12, XS to XL. And here's the thing: on the page, the slide renders 625px wide; scrolled to the table (a screenful and a half down the image), the chart fills his window and is genuinely legible at 200% — he read every number without leaning in. Real measurements, on the product he's buying. No cm row, no "model wears", but real.
**Expected:** Nothing — this site has no size-guide page at all (other shoppers hunted for one; footer has Search/About/Privacy/Returns only). Finding a readable chart inside photo 2 of 3 is dumb luck disguised as UX.
**Felt:** "There IS a chart, and I can actually read it — as long as nobody 'helps' me. Pit to Pit 24.25 in XL. That's my number. Why is this a hidden picture and not a line of text?"
**Next:** continued — tried the lightbox to get it bigger (u17-13-gallery-slide1, u17-15-gallery-slide3, u17-18-chart-slide-onpage, u17-31-chart-onpage-read)

### Step 6 — The lightbox: "expand" made the chart 2.5x SMALLER, and zooming closes it
**Did:** Clicked the photo to open the lightbox — the universal "let me see this properly" move for a partially-sighted shopper.
**Got:** The opposite of help. The lightbox fits the tall image to his 450px viewport height: the chart image now displays at 250x450 — down from 625px wide on the page. The table shrank to ~180px across; the numbers he'd just been reading became texture. So he tried to zoom in: clicked the image — the lightbox CLOSED and dumped him back on the PDP. Reopened it. Ctrl+scroll-wheel — nothing. Drag — nothing. There is no zoom button, no pinch equivalent, no zoom at all: the cursor even shows "zoom-out", because the only gesture this lightbox knows is leave. Credit where due: the close X (36px, top-right), prev/next arrows (48px) and a 2/3 counter are all visible and reachable — the room is well signposted; it's just a room where photos go to get smaller.
**Expected:** A lightbox to do the one thing lightboxes are for. Click big photo → bigger photo → zoom into the detail. Instead: click big photo → smaller photo → click again → ejected.
**Felt:** "I opened the magnifying glass and everything shrank. Then I tapped to zoom and it threw me out of the room. This feature was designed by someone with perfect eyes."
**Next:** went back to the on-page view, read the chart there, moved on to buy (u17-19-chart-lightbox, u17-20-lightbox-clickzoom)

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — reached the checkout landing with the Italy Polo (XL, £23.00, taxes included) in the cart and genuine intent, having read the actual garment measurements first. Nothing on the path was blocked at 200%: no horizontal scrolling on any page (home, search, PDP, cart, checkout), no control ever off-screen or unreachable, popup dismissible, drawer complete in one screenful. (Audit stop line; nothing entered at checkout.)
**Total time:** ~12 minutes — roughly 2 of them lost to the lightbox fight, and a steady background tax of scrolling everything in a 354px letterbox under the immortal 96px header.
**Worst moment:** The lightbox on the size-chart photo. On the page the chart renders 625px wide and he can read every number; "expanding" it shrinks it to 250px, there is no zoom of any kind, and the click that should zoom in closes the viewer and dumps him back on the PDP. The one accessibility tool on the gallery is an anti-magnifier — for the exact user who needs magnification, on the exact image that matters.
**Best moment:** Add To Cart. The whole buy box (sizes, quantity, ATC, stock count) fits one screenful, and the drawer that answers it fits everything — item, size, stepper, bin, subtotal, terms, both buttons — into 450px with no scrolling. At 200% zoom, where most shops' feedback happens two screens from your eyes, this one puts the entire transaction in view. (Honourable mention: a size chart with real measurements existing at all — even hidden in photo 2 of 3.)
**Would they come back?** Yes — this is one of the more livable stores he's used at 200%: no sideways scroll anywhere, big search type, a one-screenful buy box, a complete drawer, a flawless checkout. But he'd come back knowing the ritual: never open the lightbox, read charts on the page, squint at 10px size pills to work out which greys are dead, and accept he won't know the postage until he's typed his address.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** The outcome was a success, so — the change that would dignify it: put the size chart in the page as TEXT (or a labelled "Size guide" link), not pixels in photo 2 of 3. Text scales with his browser zoom for free; images need the lightbox to do its one job — and this one does the opposite. That single change removes both the hunt and the fight.
