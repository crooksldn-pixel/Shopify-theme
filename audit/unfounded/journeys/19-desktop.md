## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought (audit-bought) — Italy Polo XL - In Hand £23.00 + Morocco Top S £40.00, subtotal £63.00: reached the branded Shopify checkout landing page with intent and stopped there.
**Total time:** ~28 unhurried minutes, most of them enjoyable.
**Worst moment:** The photo zoom that isn't — the cursor turns into a zoom arrow over an 808px product image and clicking does nothing, on a site whose products are 100% judged by photos because no product page contains a single sentence about fabric or fit. Runners-up: the 300px black void under "View More" on the homepage, and a Filters "drawer" carrying two controls across a 1440px screen.
**Best moment:** Card hover as a system: photo swap that surfaces the second angle (on polos, the actual size chart), a "+" that either adds one-size items instantly or opens a chooser with the size chart photo right beside the size chips — plus the sticky buy panel and follow-me add bar on the PDP. Desktop shoppers get the site's best selling machinery.
**Would they come back?** Yes, cautiously. Against a phone-first read this is the rare small shop where desktop is NOT the second-class citizen — hover swaps, quick-add, sticky buy bar, a search panel that earns its width. What would stop him returning has nothing to do with the screen: 12 of 44 products buyable, no notify-me on the dead 32, no fabric facts, no reviews anywhere, and a postage cost the entire site refuses to name (the shipping-policy URL is a 404).
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** It ended in a purchase, so: a working click-to-zoom (or any zoom) would have converted his three frustrated clicks into confidence about the £40 Morocco embroidery — and a visible delivery price would decide whether this £63 basket survives tomorrow's "shipping shock" at the address step.
