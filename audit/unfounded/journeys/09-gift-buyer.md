# 09 — Priya, gift-buying aunt (nephew turns 16 next week; football-mad; she doesn't know his size)
**Device:** iPhone-class mobile 390x844, normal network · **Goal:** a birthday present, £40–50 — ideally something from this brand he keeps going on about; failing that a gift card · **Mood:** warm but time-poor; she has one evening to sort this and a backup plan (cash in a card) she'd rather not use

### Step 1 — Landed on the homepage
**Did:** Opened unfoundedstudios.com on her phone. Read the hero, started scrolling; ~10 seconds in a NEWSLETTER popup slid over the page ("Sign up to find out about future releases and promotions"). Tapped "NO THANKS" (tap 1).
**Got:** "World Cup Drop Live!" hero, a "Latest arrivals" rail of nation polos and track pants (£23–30), a promise of "FAST GLOBAL SHIPPING". Zero mentions of "gift" anywhere on the homepage — she checked.
**Expected:** Somewhere in the header or footer, the word "Gift". Most shops she buys from have a gift-card link right in the menu before Christmas and birthdays alike.
**Felt:** "Football shirts, World Cup theme — this is exactly his thing. Now where's the gift stuff?"
**Next:** continued — opened the menu (tap 2).

### Step 2 — The gift-card hunt: menu, footer, search
**Did:** Read the whole menu: Home / Shop all / Collections (England Set, Nations Shorts, All products) / Contact. Closed it, scrolled to the footer: Search / About us / Privacy Policy / Returns Policy. No gift card, no gift guide, no FAQ. Opened search (tap 3) and typed "gift card".
**Got:** The search drawer: "Oh no! No results found for 'gift card'." The full results page finds 0 products and exactly one page match: **the Returns Policy** — which, she later discovered, contains the site's only "gift" sentence: "Gift cards, if offered are non refundable." A policy line for a product that does not exist. (Probes confirm: /products/gift-card, /pages/gift-cards, /pages/faq all 404.)
**Expected:** A £25/£50 digital gift card — the standard escape hatch for aunts who don't know sizes. Every Shopify shop can switch one on.
**Felt:** "No gift card at all? The one thing that can't be the wrong size, and they don't sell it. And the returns page talks about gift cards 'if offered' — so someone thought about them long enough to disclaim them, but not to sell them."
**Next:** continued — fine, she'll pick an actual shirt. He's England-mad; the menu literally has an "England Set". Tapped it (tap 4).

### Step 4 — Shop All: the £40–50 shelf is five shirts, all tiny
**Did:** Scrolled all 44 products across three pages, hunting anything giftable in budget.
**Got:** A wall of SOLD OUT badges — Bird hoodies, Brazil, France, Greece, Italy tops, Mexico, all dead. In her £40–50 bracket the entire buyable list is five tops: ARGENTINA £50, GERMANY £40, MOROCCO £40, SPAIN £40, USA £40 — and every single card says "**Available in 1 size**". She also noticed GERMANY TOP appears twice: once at £50 SOLD OUT and once at £40 in stock — same name, same shirt, ten pounds apart, no explanation.
**Expected:** A size choice. Gifts live or die on sizes; "Available in 1 size" on every option is a warning light. And two identical Germany tops at different prices made her double-check she wasn't misreading.
**Felt:** "So his team's gone, his second-favourites are gone, and what's left is other countries' shirts in one mystery size each. Which size?!"
**Next:** continued — Argentina first; he never stops talking about Messi. Tapped the £50 Argentina Top (tap 6).

### Step 5 — Argentina PDP: one size (XS), one photo, no size guide to send him
**Did:** Read the product page top to bottom hunting sizing help — this is the page where she'd normally screenshot a size chart and WhatsApp it to her sister. Tapped the greyed S and M pills to be sure.
**Got:** Size row XS / S / M — but only **XS** is live; the checked pill reads "XS — VARIANT SOLD OUT OR UNAVAILABLE" while the Add To Cart button sits active below it, and tapping S or M does nothing, the selection just snaps back to XS. "Only 1 left in stock. Order soon." The entire description is "Please Allow 2-5 Working Days For Item To Be Shipped". **One photo** — no back view, no model, no size chart slide (other personas found a size chart hiding as the last gallery photo on the track pants; the tops have nothing to hide it in — one photo is the whole gallery). No size-guide link anywhere: not on the PDP, not in the menu, not in the footer; /pages/size-guide is a 404 and searching "size guide" returns two unrelated products.
**Expected:** A size chart she could read or send him, or at least a chest measurement. And if a shirt is XS-only with one unit left, say so on the collection card — "Available in 1 size" let her walk in hoping.
**Felt:** "A £50 shirt for a 16-year-old boy, in adult XS, with one photo and a description that's a postage disclaimer. I can't even find out what XS means here — there's no chart to send him without ruining the surprise, and nothing to secretly check against his hoodie at home."
**Next:** went back — before gambling £40 on the Germany top (the only other realistic candidate, S), she wanted the answer to the question every gift buyer asks: what happens if it's wrong?

### Step 6 — Returns policy: readable, honest — and hostile to gifts
**Did:** Footer → Returns Policy (2 taps from the PDP). Read every word, specifically as a gift buyer.
**Got:** A genuinely clear, human-written policy. The facts: 14 days from delivery to give notice, then 14 more days to send back; items unworn with tags; start by emailing info@unfoundedstudios.com with **your order number, your full name**, the item and reason; post to an address in Milford, Surrey. **"Customers are responsible for return postage costs unless the item is faulty."** **"We do not currently offer automatic exchanges. If you would like a different size… return your original order for a refund and place a new order."** Refund goes **"back to your original payment method"** — i.e. her card, not to him. No gift receipt, no gift returns provision, no way for a recipient to swap a size.
**Expected:** Some notion of a gift: a gift receipt, or "recipients may exchange within 30 days". High-street standard.
**Felt:** "So if the S doesn't fit him: he can't take it anywhere — he'd have to come to me, I dig out the order number, I email, I pay the postage back, I wait up to 14 days for MY card to be refunded… and then 're-order the right size' — which doesn't exist, because S is the only size they have. The return path for a wrong-size gift is a refund and an apology to a teenager. And the birthday's Saturday — 2-5 working days' dispatch, this was already tight."
**Next:** hesitated — one last look: maybe there's gift wrap or a gift note at the till that softens the gamble. Added the £40 Germany Top (S) to the cart to see (tap 7).
**Screens:** u09-21-returns-top, u09-22-returns-mid, u09-23-returns-end

### Step 7 — Cart and the checkout door: no wrap, no message, shipping cost unknown
**Did:** On the Germany Top page, tapped Add To Cart. Inspected the cart drawer, then the full cart page, then ticked the required terms box and tapped Checkout to see the till itself. Stopped there — typed nothing.
**Got:** Drawer slides up instantly — "Your cart 1, GERMANY TOP £40.00, S, Subtotal £40.00, Taxes included" — tidy feedback, badge updates to 1. Gift options in the drawer: none. The full cart page has one collapsed "**Order note**" box — a plain note-to-the-merchant, no promise it becomes a gift message, no wrap option, no "this is a gift" tick. The checkout landing page is branded ("Checkout - Unfounded"), asks for email or phone (with a marketing opt-in checkbox beneath), delivery address — and **"Enter your shipping address to view available shipping methods"**: she cannot learn the delivery cost or speed without handing over an address first. No gift message, no gift receipt, no wrapping anywhere between product and payment. Also noticed the contact page (open 9am–9pm weekdays) with a message form — the one human channel if she wanted to ask "how big is your S?", though no answer would come tonight.
**Expected:** At minimum a "gift message" box at checkout; ideally wrap for £3. And a shipping price before the address form — the £40-50 budget has to cover postage too.
**Felt:** "It's a normal, competent till — for buying yourself a shirt. For a present it offers me nothing: no wrap, no note he'd see, no gift receipt, and I'd be posting him a guess in a plastic mailer with the price on the paperwork for all I know. I'm not gambling £40 of a £50 budget on 'adult S, probably?' for a Germany shirt he'd wear ironically at best. He's an England fan."
**Next:** gave up — closed the tab at the checkout door, left the shirt in the cart. Cash in the birthday card it is; she'll show him the site and let him fight the stock himself.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Didn't buy. Reached the checkout landing with a £40 Germany Top (S — the only size) in the cart, found the till gift-blind too, and backed out. Gives cash in a card instead — the exact outcome she came here to avoid.
**Total time:** ~16 minutes (about 4 on the gift-card hunt, 3 reading the returns policy as a gift buyer).
**Worst moment:** Realising there is no escape hatch: his team is entirely sold out, every in-budget alternative is a single mystery size (XS or S) with one photo and no size chart to send him, there's no gift card to fall back on — and the returns policy's only mention of gifts is a disclaimer for the gift cards they don't sell.
**Best moment:** The returns policy itself — plain-English, specific, honest about who pays (her) and what's possible (no exchanges). It killed the purchase, but it killed it BEFORE she spent £40, and she rated the shop for that candour. Honourable mention: the cart drawer's instant, correct feedback.
**Would they come back?** Not for gifts. Possibly to forward the link to her nephew with "pick something and tell me" — turning the gift into homework, which defeats the point. Nothing here serves a buyer who doesn't know a size: no gift card, no guide, no wrap, no recipient-friendly returns.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** A gift card. She had £50 ready and a brand her nephew loves; a digital gift card (one Shopify toggle) captures that money with zero size risk, zero stock risk, and instant delivery before Saturday. (Second prize: a size chart on the tops plus one M or L anywhere in stock.)
