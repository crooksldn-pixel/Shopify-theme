# 16 — Priya, screen reader user (NVDA on desktop), heard the Italy polo is £23 and wants it bought tonight
**Device:** Desktop 1440x900, normal broadband · **Goal:** Buy the Italy Polo unassisted — a mate said the football-nations drop is decent and going fast · **Mood:** Practised, patient, braced — small fashion stores are usually unlabelled-icon soup

*Method note: this run could not use a live screen reader. Every "I hear" below is what the accessibility tree would hand NVDA — roles, accessible names, states and live regions read via Chromium ARIA snapshots and DOM inspection. Where I'm inferring how a reader would voice something, I say so. (Session note: the audit egress IP geo-detects as US, so first contact announced "United States" and dollar prices; the session was pinned with `?country=GB` to match this UK shopper, same as personas 01–14. Everything below is the GBP view.)*

### Step 1 — Landed and took a first listen
**Did:** Opened unfoundedstudios.com, listened for title, language, landmarks, headings, and pressed Tab once.
**Got:** Page title "UnfoundedStudios" — one word, no tagline, but it names the place. Page language plain "en". First Tab stop is a real "Skip to content" link to #MainContent. Landmarks exist: banner, main, footer — and at least five `navigation` regions, not one of which has a name, so my landmark list reads "navigation, navigation, navigation, navigation, navigation". There is **no H1 anywhere on the homepage** — heading navigation starts at H2 "All Nation Items Available Now!", and the collection tiles announce as H4 "SALE Black + Grey Items6" — the item count glued straight onto the name, so I hear "Items six" and have to guess where the name ends.
**Expected:** No skip link at all, honestly. Getting one as the very first stop was a good sign.
**Felt:** A coin with two faces. Someone shipped a skip link and real landmarks; someone else shipped five anonymous navs and a page with no H1, which for me is a map with no title. H-key from the top drops me into "All Nation Items Available Now!" with no idea what site-level heading I skipped, because there isn't one.
**Next:** continued — started reading the page.
**Screens:** u16-01-arrival, u16-03-after-escape

### Step 2 — Seven seconds in, the site went quiet (the newsletter popup)
**Did:** Was reading the hero when the page changed under me, about 7 seconds after load. Went looking for what had happened, then tried to escape it.
**Got:** A `role=dialog aria-modal=true` overlay had opened — and its `aria-labelledby` points at the id "Newsletter Modal", **which doesn't exist in the page**, so the dialog has *no accessible name at all*. Focus never moved: my cursor was still in the body. Nothing was announced. Escape does nothing. I pressed Tab six times and never landed inside it — focus wandered the page underneath instead. (Inferred: with aria-modal honoured, everything OUTSIDE the dialog drops out of my browse mode — so from my side the entire site was just replaced by a nameless dialog I was never put into and can't Tab into.) Exploring the dialog by virtual cursor finds: heading level 4 "Sign up for our newsletter", a properly labelled "Email" textbox, "Subscribe", and "No thanks". I activated "No thanks" and got my site back. (Read-only rules: I never touched the email field.)
**Expected:** If a modal opens, I get put in it and told its name. That's the whole contract of aria-modal.
**Felt:** This is the silent-modal trap with the label plumbing visibly broken — someone typed the dialog's name into `aria-labelledby` instead of `aria-label`, so the name announces to nobody. A sighted person sees a signup card; I get a site that goes dead until I go hunting for why. And Escape — the one key every blind user tries first — is ignored.
**Next:** hesitated, found "No thanks" by exploring, carried on.
**Screens:** u16-04-popup-visible, u16-05-popup-closed

### Step 3 — The navigation, by ear
**Did:** Walked the banner: main nav, then the utility cluster.
**Got:** Main nav is a clean list of links — "Home", "Shop all", "Collections", "Contact". Collections has a hover dropdown with no aria-expanded to warn me it exists, but its sub-links (England Set, Nations Shorts, All products) sit right after it in reading order, so I meet them anyway. The utility side: a "Search" button (labelled, good), "Log in" link, a "Cart" button — and the country switcher, which announces as a *group* containing an image "United Kingdom" and the text "United Kingdom", with **no button role anywhere in it**. If I'd needed to change country by ear, I couldn't tell it's operable.
**Expected:** Unlabelled icon buttons. Mostly wrong — Search, Log in and Cart all have names.
**Felt:** Serviceable. The nav links are honest words, not icons. The country control pretending to be a picture is the one dud — lucky for me `?country=GB` had it right already.
**Next:** continued — read the product rail on the homepage.
**Screens:** u16-07-header

### Step 4 — The homepage cards: the promised gibberish, checked honestly
**Did:** Earlier sweeps warned that raw CSS text leaks into the homepage card headings and links ("​.product-card__media { --product-card-media-aspect-ratio: 3/4; … }" sits inside the card markup, and the sold-out England card headings carry a whole `:root { --color-badge-discount… }` style block plus "Sold out" in their DOM text). I checked what the accessibility tree — the thing my reader actually voices — does with all of it.
**Got:** Good news I want on the record: **none of the CSS junk is announced.** Chromium's accessibility tree drops `style` and `script` elements, so the card heading announces as a clean "Italy Polo, heading level 3, link", and the image link as "Italy Polo Italy Polo" (name doubled from two identical alts — wordy, not confusing). What I *do* hear per card: "Italy Polo… £23.00 Regular price £23.00… Available in 5 size" — the price spoken twice — and then, bizarrely, a **radiogroup containing no radios**, just loose text "XS - In Hand S - In Hand M - In Hand" and a listitem "+2". And the button on every card is "Choose options".
**Expected:** After the warnings, I braced for a heading that recites a stylesheet. It never reaches the ear — that bug is for sighted users and find-in-page.
**Felt:** Relieved and a bit suspicious. "Available in five size, radio group" — then no radios, just size names with "In Hand" stapled to each. What is "In Hand"? Stockroom talk. It's in every size name on this site, and I'm about to hear a lot more of it.
**Next:** continued — activated the Italy Polo card.
**Screens:** u16-06-home-cards

### Step 5 — The product page: one photo, one shipping line, and a size row that argues with itself
**Did:** Landed on /products/italy-polo. Read title, H1, description, images; then read the size group carefully in browse mode — the earlier sweeps flagged hidden "VARIANT SOLD OUT OR UNAVAILABLE" text stapled to available sizes, and I wanted to hear it the way I actually would.
**Got:** Title "Italy Polo – Unfounded", H1 "Italy Polo" — fine. The entire product description is "Please Allow 2-5 Working Days For Item To Be Shipped". The product has **one photo**, alt text "Italy Polo" — no colours, no crest, no fabric; I now know more about this shirt's dispatch schedule than about the shirt. No size guide anywhere (personas 03/12 confirmed none exists site-wide). The size group announces as "Size XS - In Hand, group". The radios are named via aria-label — "XS - In Hand", "S - In Hand"… — and here's the confirmed trap: in browse mode, **after every single radio sits static text "XS - In Hand Variant sold out or unavailable"** — verified in the accessibility tree on all five sizes, *including XS and XL, the two that are in stock*. So reading down the row I hear: "radio, XS - In Hand, checked" — then "XS - In Hand Variant sold out or unavailable". Is my size available or not? The page says both, about every size. Meanwhile S, M and L are native-disabled, so in forms mode Tab and arrow keys skip them entirely: ArrowRight from XS lands directly on XL *and selects it*. Unless I browse-read the static text, the product simply has two sizes; the other three exist only inside that contradictory mumble. Below: a status region "Only 9 left in stock. Order soon." and the price status "£23.00 Regular price £23.00 Taxes included." — which also contains a whole JavaScript function in its DOM text, but (checked) the script text is *not* in the accessibility tree, so it stays unspoken.
**Expected:** Sizes as bare buttons; sold-out ones missing. Instead I got sizes that are present, named, *and* all captioned "sold out or unavailable" — the in-stock ones included.
**Felt:** This row nearly ended the trip. I was ready to buy XS and the page whispered "Variant sold out or unavailable" right after it. A sighted person sees a strikethrough on S/M/L and knows instantly; I have to notice that the *same* disclaimer follows all five and conclude it's boilerplate glued to every label. That's a deduction, not an announcement. And "Only 9 left, order soon" urgency on a product the same page half-claims is unavailable is a bit rich.
**Next:** hesitated — reasoned the disclaimer was noise since the Add to cart button was live, kept XS selected.
**Screens:** u16-08-pdp-arrival, u16-09-size-selected

### Step 6 — Add to cart: the best thirty seconds on the site
**Did:** Moved to "Add to cart" and activated it.
**Got:** The cart drawer opened as `role=dialog aria-modal=true` named **"Your cart"** — and focus moved straight onto its "Close" button. A live region carrying the cart contents was injected, so the add announces itself. Escape closes the drawer and — checked — puts focus back on the Add to cart button, exactly where I left. One oddity afterwards: the header cart control now announces as "1, button". Just the number — the word "Cart" fell out of its name once it had something in it.
**Expected:** The classic silent add, then a trek to /cart to find out whether anything happened.
**Felt:** Textbook, genuinely. I hear "Your cart, dialog", I'm inside it, Escape returns me precisely. Whoever wired this drawer knew what they were doing — which makes the nameless newsletter dialog stranger. "One, button" in the header is a small riddle ("one what?") but I'd just heard the answer.
**Next:** continued — read the drawer top to bottom.
**Screens:** u16-10-after-atc

### Step 7 — Reading the cart drawer — and the popup ambushes it
**Did:** Read the whole drawer; then, mid-read, the newsletter popup fired again — this time on top of the open cart.
**Got:** The drawer, as heard: "Your cart 1" (a paragraph, not a heading — I can't H-key to it); image link "Italy Polo - XS - In Hand"; heading level 3 "Italy Polo"; "£23.00"; "XS - In Hand"; then "Decrease quantity for Italy Polo", a labelled "Quantity:" spinbutton at 1, "Increase quantity for Italy Polo" — quantity buttons named with the product, properly. The remove control is a link named only "Delete" (name scraped from its bin-icon alt): fine with one item, but three items would mean three identical "Delete"s. Then "£23.00 Subtotal… Taxes included… £23.00 GBP" — no shipping figure anywhere (no persona found one before checkout). A required checkbox properly labelled "**Agree to terms of sale as per the merchants terms of service.**", a "terms" link, "View cart", "Checkout". And then the ambush: about seven seconds after the page load, the **unnamed newsletter dialog opened on top of the open cart drawer** — two aria-modal dialogs stacked, focus still in the drawer, the top dialog nameless and never announced. Screenshot u16-11 shows the signup card squatting over my cart.
**Expected:** A drawer of unlabelled plus/minus icons. Got the opposite — then got mugged by the popup a second time, in the middle of my cart.
**Felt:** The drawer itself is the most literate part of the site. But a modal opening over a modal, silently, while I'm counting my money? By ARIA rules the popup now owns the page and my cursor is inside a dialog that supposedly doesn't exist. I only understood it because I'd already met this ghost on the homepage.
**Next:** continued — dismissed the popup again, back to the drawer.
**Screens:** u16-11-cart-drawer

### Step 8 — The terms gate, then checkout
**Did:** Tried "Checkout" with the terms box deliberately unticked, to hear what the gate says; then ticked the checkbox (its label is real and attached) and activated Checkout.
**Got:** Unticked: nothing navigates, and the checkbox raises native browser validation — "Please check this box if you want to proceed." (Inferred: NVDA voices native validation bubbles reliably; this is the one gate on the site that speaks its own error.) Ticked: straight through to the Shopify checkout. Landing: title "Checkout - Unfounded", page language now correctly "en-GB", a screen-reader-only H1 "Unfounded Checkout", then a proper H2 outline — Express checkout / Contact / Delivery / Shipping method / Payment / Order summary — with every field labelled ("Email or mobile phone number", "Country/Region", "Address"…), a skip link, and the order summary reading "Italy Polo, XS - In Hand, £23.00… Total GBP £23.00". Shipping: "Enter shipping address to view available shipping methods" — cost still unknown at the door, same as every persona before me. Brand holds: logo alt "Unfounded". I stopped there — audit rules, and card details are for keeps.
**Expected:** Shopify checkout to be the accessible part. It is — it's the only stretch of the journey where every control introduced itself first time.
**Felt:** The door works. It took the whole site's worth of guessing to reach a page where I stopped having to.
**Next:** done — stopped at the checkout landing with intent.

## Outcome
**Bought / didn't:** Bought — Italy Polo, XS, £23.00: reached the Shopify checkout landing unassisted with the item in the cart and the terms gate passed. But "unassisted" carried asterisks: I bought a shirt knowing nothing about it (one photo alt'd "Italy Polo", a description that is only a shipping disclaimer, no size guide), and I had to *reason my way past* the site's own hidden caption telling me my in-stock size was "sold out or unavailable".
**Total time:** ~32 minutes — a sighted shopper does this route in 6–8. The overruns: two rounds of the nameless popup, and several minutes deciding whether "Variant sold out or unavailable" after the size I wanted was truth or boilerplate.
**Worst moment:** The newsletter dialog, both times — `aria-modal` with a broken `aria-labelledby` (it points at an id that doesn't exist), no focus move, no announcement, Escape ignored — and its encore performance opening *on top of my open cart drawer*. A dialog that claims the whole page and never says its own name is the single most disorienting thing a site can do to me.
**Best moment:** Pressing "Add to cart" and hearing the world respond: focus lands in a dialog named "Your cart", the contents announce, and Escape puts me back on the exact button I left. Honourable mention: "Decrease quantity for Italy Polo" — a quantity button that says which product, which most big-name stores still don't bother with.
**Would they come back?** Only knowingly. The cart and checkout she'd trust again; but a store where every size — available or not — whispers "sold out or unavailable", where the product description is a courier note, and where a nameless modal stalks every page, is a store she'd only re-enter for a specific item a sighted friend has already described to her. She is telling the access-tech group chat about that size row.
**One thing that would have changed the outcome:** The outcome held, so: the thing that nearly broke it — make the hidden "Variant sold out or unavailable" caption conditional so it's only attached to sizes that actually are (it currently reads out after in-stock sizes too, verified in the accessibility tree). Second, cheap and huge: fix one attribute — the popup's `aria-labelledby="Newsletter Modal"` names nothing; make it `aria-label`, move focus in, honour Escape.
